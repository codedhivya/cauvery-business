The quarter in one paragraph · CB read

Record revenue, record profit — and the market sold it anyway. Polycab delivered its highest-ever first-quarter revenue (₹8,210 cr, +39% YoY) and PAT (₹797 cr, +33% YoY), with EBITDA margin at 13.8% — the upper half of the 11–14% band that is healthy for cables & wires. The shares still fell roughly 4% on the print.

The reason is the gap between value and volume. Management described domestic wires volume growth as low-to-mid single digit. Nearly all of the 39% is copper and aluminium price pass-through, not tonnage. On the sector's own logic that is neither good nor bad in itself — a cables business is a cost-plus converter and passing metal through is what it is built to do — but it means the headline growth rate is a commodity statement, not a demand statement, and it reverses when metal prices do.

What is genuinely new this quarter is FMEG. Segment EBIT margin of 8.0% against 2.1% a year ago is close to a fourfold improvement, and it puts FMEG at the floor of the 8–10% margin band Polycab has targeted for FY30 — four years early. Against that: exports fell to 3.3% of revenue from 5.2%, which moves away from the stated >10%-by-FY30 goal, and EBITDA margin is up 74 bps QoQ but down 74 bps YoY. Which of those two comparisons you lead with changes the story considerably; this report shows both.

⚡ The four things that decide this quarter
1 · Value growth vs volume growth

Consolidated revenue grew 39%. Management characterised domestic wires volume growth as low-to-mid single digit. The difference is copper and aluminium price inflation flowing through a cost-plus pricing model. This is the single most important thing to hold in mind when reading every other growth number in this report.

Value +39% Volume low-to-mid single digit Cost-plus pass-through

Exact volume growth was not quantified in the figures sourced for this report — the characterisation above is management commentary, not a disclosed number.

2 · FMEG turned the corner

FMEG revenue ₹761 cr, +71% YoY, the highest-ever quarterly figure, with EBIT margin at 8.0% against 4.1% in Q4 FY26 and 2.1% in Q1 FY26. That is the FY30 target band's floor, reached four years early. Solar was cited as the lead driver.

EBIT 8.0% vs 2.1% YoY Record quarter 10th straight qtr above industry growth
3 · Exports went backwards

Export revenue was ₹267 cr — 3.3% of total, down from 5.2% a year earlier, attributed to Middle East market conditions. Polycab's stated ambition is exports above 10% of sales by FY30. A halving of the share in one year is the clearest negative in the quarter, and it is a mix problem rather than a margin one.

3.3% of revenue, from 5.2% Target >10% by FY30
4 · Balance sheet did the opposite of what growth usually costs

Working capital cycle compressed to 15 days from 25, and the company sits on ₹3,990 cr net cash while spending ₹317 cr of capex in the quarter. In a sector whose characteristic failure mode is profit trapped in receivables, growing 39% while shortening the cash cycle is the quarter's quietest good number.

Debtor days and quarterly operating cash flow were not separately disclosed in the sources reviewed — the working-capital cycle is the disclosed proxy.

Reading the two margin comparisons. EBITDA margin of 13.8% is up 74 bps against Q4 FY26 (13.1%) and down 74 bps against Q1 FY26 (14.6%). Both are true and neither is the “right” one. The QoQ improvement reflects recovery from a weaker sequential quarter; the YoY decline reflects that Q1 FY26 was a high-margin base. A report quoting only one of them is making an argument, not a disclosure.

🏢 Business profile how Polycab makes money
Polycab India Limited
Capital Goods · Cables & Wires

India's largest wires and cables manufacturer by revenue, with three reported segments. The core Wires & Cables business (87% of revenue) sells through a distribution network to electricians, contractors and institutional buyers, plus direct institutional and export channels. FMEG (9%) covers fans, lighting, switches, switchgear, solar and appliances under the Polycab brand. EPC (4%) executes turnkey projects, currently weighted to government programmes — BharatNet and RDSS.

Cost-plus pricing model Distribution-led Net cash balance sheet
The economics in one place

Copper and aluminium dominate the cost base, and Polycab prices cost-plus — when metal rises, both cost and revenue rise, which is why a 39% revenue quarter can sit alongside low-to-mid single digit volume growth. Margin is therefore defended by speed of pass-through and mix, not by input cost itself.

The two mix levers that genuinely change margin are FMEG (higher-margin branded products, scaling now) and exports (higher realisation, currently shrinking). Everything else is throughput.

🛡️ Moats assessed sceptically — scale alone is not a moat
Brand + distribution reach
In wires and cables the purchase decision is usually made by an electrician or contractor, not the end user. A brand trusted at that layer, backed by a dealer network built over decades, is genuinely hard to replicate quickly — a new entrant can build a plant far faster than it can build shelf presence.
Durable
Scale in a pass-through business
Being the largest gives procurement leverage on copper and aluminium and fixed-cost absorption. Real, but bounded: it is a cost advantage in a commodity conversion business, not pricing power. Competitors with adequate scale close most of the gap.
Moderate
Net cash + short cash cycle
₹3,990 cr net cash and a 15-day working-capital cycle let Polycab fund capacity and absorb metal-price swings without external funding — a structural advantage over leveraged competitors in exactly the quarters when metals whipsaw.
Durable
FMEG brand extension
Extending an established electrical brand into fans, lighting and solar reuses the same dealer network. The 8.0% EBIT margin suggests it is working — but FMEG competes against well-entrenched incumbents and the advantage is contested, not structural.
Emerging, unproven
EPC order book
₹10,900 cr of contracted work gives visibility, but government EPC is competitively bid, margin-thin relative to products, and carries PSU payment risk. Visibility is not the same as advantage.
Not a moat
Claims to be sceptical of
“Capacity expansion” and “Make in India tailwinds” are available to every competitor and are not advantages. Manufacturing capacity in a commodity is only a moat if the cost position is better than the incumbent's — and Polycab does not disclose cost per unit that would let anyone verify that.

The order-driven caveat that applies to this sector. A trailing multiple mis-prices an order-driven business, because rerating follows order inflow rather than reported earnings — multiples can look most expensive exactly when visibility is strongest. Polycab is a partial case: only the EPC segment (3.7% of revenue) is genuinely order-driven, so the trailing multiple is more meaningful here than it would be for a defence or T&D company. The more relevant distortion for Polycab is the commodity one — a revenue base inflated by metal prices makes both revenue growth and, indirectly, the multiple harder to read across a price cycle.

🏆 Verdict research read of the disclosed numbers
On the fundamentals reviewed

This was an operationally good quarter that the headline growth rate describes badly. Strip out the commodity pass-through and what remains is: a core business growing volume in low-to-mid single digits at a healthy and roughly stable margin; a second business (FMEG) that has genuinely crossed into viable profitability for the first time; a third (EPC) executing slowly against a large book; and a balance sheet that got stronger, not weaker, while all of that happened.

The bear case does not require anything to go wrong. It requires only that metal prices stop rising. At that point the 39% becomes a much smaller number, the base effect turns hostile, and the stock is left carrying a high-40s trailing multiple against low-to-mid single digit volume growth. That is the tension the market appeared to price when it sold the print by ~4%.

What would settle it is the next two quarters of volume, the export share, and whether FMEG's 8.0% EBIT margin holds when competitors respond. All three are disclosed quarterly, so the thesis is checkable rather than a matter of opinion.

This is a research assessment of reported earnings quality. It is not a buy, sell or hold recommendation, and no price view is expressed or implied.

🤖 CB Assist — questions this report can answer
Why did the stock fall on a record quarter?
Because volume growth was low-to-mid single digit while revenue grew 39% — the gap is copper and aluminium price pass-through. The market read the growth as commodity-driven rather than demand-driven.
Is a 13.8% EBITDA margin good?
For cables & wires the healthy band is 11–14%, so yes — upper half. But it is down 74 bps YoY and up 74 bps QoQ, so the direction depends entirely on which comparison you use.
What is the single best number in the quarter?
FMEG EBIT margin at 8.0% against 2.1% a year ago — it reaches the floor of the FY30 target band four years early. The runner-up is the working-capital cycle at 15 days from 25.
What is the single worst?
Exports at 3.3% of revenue, down from 5.2%, against a stated ambition of more than 10% by FY30. In absolute terms export revenue fell roughly 13% while the company grew 39%.
What is not in this report?
Debtor days, inventory days, quarterly operating cash flow, consolidated order inflow, book-to-bill and capacity utilisation — none were disclosed in the sources reviewed. Q1 FY26 EBITDA and PAT are derived from disclosed growth rates rather than filed directly.
