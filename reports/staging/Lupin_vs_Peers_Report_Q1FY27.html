The Quarter in One Line Q1 FY27 · 6 of 7 declared as of 10 Aug 2026 · Zydus Lifesciences reports 11 Aug

The quarter split Indian pharma cleanly along one line: who sells generics into the US, and who manufactures for innovators. The CDMO and API names posted the strongest results in the set — Neuland's revenue up 119% and Laurus up 29% with margins at 35.5% and 31.8% respectively. The US-generics-exposed large caps went the other way: Dr Reddy's PAT fell 69% as Lenalidomide sales unwound, Cipla's fell 39% on a similar base effect, and Sun's US formulations declined 9.7%.

Lupin sits in the middle, and is the most interesting company in the set. It posted a genuine record — first quarter above ₹8,000 crore revenue and ₹2,400 crore EBITDA, with margin up 340bps to 30.0% — and North America grew 43%. But management guided US revenue down to a $250–280 million quarterly range from $366 million this quarter, with recovery only in FY28. The result is excellent; the guidance says it is a peak.

Geographic Revenue Mix the primary lens on an Indian pharma company

Geography is the most informative disclosure in this sector, because each market carries different growth, margin and risk. US generics face structural price erosion and are exposed to regulatory action on manufacturing sites; domestic branded generics (IPM) have no meaningful erosion and are stickier; emerging markets sit between. A company's US share is therefore both its opportunity and its main vulnerability — and this quarter, the same exposure that lifted Lupin sank Dr Reddy's.

Valuation P/E primary for pharma · market data early Aug 2026
The finding that most changes the read

The market has already priced the split this report describes — arguably past it. Laurus Labs, which scores highest on fundamentals, trades at roughly 114× earnings. Lupin, which delivered the best large-cap quarter in the set, trades at roughly 17× — the cheapest of the six.

That inverts the fundamentals ranking. The CDMO re-rating has already happened; Laurus's share price rose about 114% over the preceding year. Lupin's discount reflects exactly the guided US decline flagged in Risks — the market is pricing the guidance, not the quarter.

Business Profile — Lupin how the focus company actually operates
Business model

Lupin is a generics-led pharmaceutical company with North America as its largest market at 42% of revenue (₹3,591 crore this quarter, $366 million) and India second at 29% (₹2,380 crore). It also runs EMEA, LATAM and Rest-of-World books — all three growing faster than the group this quarter at 37%, 71% and 69% respectively — plus a small API business at ₹264 crore.

Its strategic direction is toward complex generics rather than plain oral solids: the pipeline carries 45+ injectables and 20+ inhalation products in development, with 15+ ANDA filings planned for FY27. Both categories carry higher barriers and fewer competitors than commodity generics, which is the structural answer to US price erosion. R&D ran at ₹608 crore, 7.4% of sales — at the upper end of the 6–9% norm for generics companies.

The balance sheet is net cash, at (0.12) net debt-to-equity. Cash fell to ₹2,831 crore from ₹4,636 crore, primarily on the VISUfarma acquisition.

Moats what a well-funded competitor could not replicate quickly
Lupin — Complex-Generics Capability
The 45+ injectables and 20+ inhalation programmes are the moat candidate, not the ANDA count. Inhalation products in particular are difficult to develop, expensive to bioequivalence-test, and attract few competitors — which is why they hold price where oral solids do not. A well-funded entrant would need years and specialised facilities to match it. Backed by a net-cash balance sheet and 7.4% R&D intensity, this is a real if slow-building advantage. A large plain-vanilla ANDA pipeline is not a moat — filings are not a barrier, and every peer has them.
Product-complexity barrier
Laurus Labs — Validated-Site Switching Costs
The structurally strongest moat in this set. Once an innovator validates a molecule at a CDMO site, moving it means regulatory refiling and revalidation — so they rarely do. That lock-in is what produces CDMO's 28–35% margin band versus generics' 20–25%, and it is visible here: CDMO revenue ₹870 crore, +67%, driving group margin up 700bps to 31.8%. Doubling FY27 capex to ₹2,000 crore is a bet on that pipeline continuing.
Switching costs
Sun Pharma — Scale plus Innovative Franchise
At ₹15,184 crore revenue Sun is roughly twice the size of the next company here, and its innovative medicines franchise (+12.8%) is a genuinely different business from generics — proprietary products with patent protection rather than commodity competition. India formulations at ₹5,475 crore (+16%) adds a branded-generics book where prescriber relationships take decades to build. Diversification is the moat: US weakness of 9.7% cost it far less than it cost Dr Reddy's.
Scale & diversification
Neuland — Commercial CMS Positioning, Unproven at Scale
Neuland's 35.5% margin and 119% revenue growth come from commercial-stage CMS projects, which carry the same validated-site logic as Laurus. But the evidence base is one quarter against a ₹13.9 crore PAT base, and a CMS book concentrated in a few commercial molecules is exposed to any one of them being discontinued. The moat mechanism is real; its durability at this company is not yet demonstrated. Stated plainly rather than inferred from the growth rate.
Unproven — watch
Dr Reddy's and Cipla are omitted from this section rather than given a manufactured moat. Both are large, capable companies, but nothing in this quarter's sourced disclosure demonstrates a durable advantage — and a 69% and 39% PAT decline respectively is the opposite of evidence for one. Cipla's One India franchise is the likeliest candidate, but the India revenue figure was not disclosed separately in sourced material, so the claim cannot be grounded in a number. A moat claim without a number is an opinion.
Risks & Outlook live and near-term, distinct from SWOT's structural threats
The dominant risk this quarter — stated by management, not inferred

Lupin has guided US revenue down to a $250–280 million quarterly range, from $366 million in Q1 FY27, with recovery expected only in fiscal 2028. That is a 23–32% decline in a business representing 42% of group revenue — implying roughly a 10–13% hit to group revenue if other geographies merely hold. It is the single most important forward statement in this report, and it comes from the company itself rather than from analysis.

Read against the peer set, it fits a pattern rather than being company-specific: Sun's US formulations fell 9.7%, Cipla's North America fell 28%, Dr Reddy's fell 35.4%. Lupin's record quarter looks less like an exception to the US generics downturn and more like a company reaching it later than its peers.

Laurus grew 29% with margin up 700bps to 31.8%, driven by CDMO revenue up 67% to ₹870 crore — and did it against a normal prior-year base, which is what separates it from Neuland's larger percentages. The validated-site switching costs behind CDMO revenue are the most durable competitive structure in this set. Doubling FY27 capex to ₹2,000 crore signals management sees the pipeline continuing.

Neuland scores marginally higher at 89 and deserves the argument. Its 35.5% margin is the highest here and its 119% revenue growth the fastest. But PAT rose 962% from a ₹13.9 crore base — a near-tenfold move off a depressed quarter is a different signal from the same move off a normal one, and one quarter of commercial CMS revenue does not establish durability. The scores are within a point; the confidence behind them is not.

On Lupin specifically — the focus of this report. It delivered the best quarter of any large cap here: record revenue and EBITDA, margin up 340bps to 30.0%, and the only US book in the set that grew. It scores 77 and lands in green. But management has guided US revenue down 23–32% next quarter, and its peers' results suggest Lupin reached the US generics downturn later rather than avoiding it. The result is excellent and the guidance says it is a peak — both are true, and a reader who takes only the headline away has missed the more important half.

And valuation inverts this ranking. Laurus trades at roughly 114× earnings against Lupin's 17× — the CDMO re-rating has already happened, while Lupin carries the cheapest multiple in the set precisely because the market is pricing the guided decline. A ranking on this quarter's fundamentals is not a ranking on value, and here the two point in opposite directions. ⚠ Source conflicts encountered and how they were resolved
Lupin revenue. Press coverage reported ₹8,277 crore (+32.04%); the company's own Q1 FY27 investor presentation states ₹82,172 million (₹8,217 crore, +33%). The presentation is used — Tier-1 company disclosure wins over secondary coverage. Similarly PAT: ₹1,417 crore reported vs ₹14,150 million (₹1,415 crore) disclosed.

Dr Reddy's PAT. One outlet reported "net profit drops 8% to ₹1,290 crore". Multiple others, including Business Standard and the company's SEC 6-K filing coverage, report a 69% decline to ₹443.5 crore. The 69% figure is corroborated across sources and against the ₹1,417.8 crore prior-year base; the −8% figure appears erroneous and is not used.

Laurus CDMO revenue. Reported as both ₹870 crore (+66.67%) and ₹835 crore (+69%). ₹870 crore is used as the more frequently corroborated figure, but the segment number carries lower confidence than the group figures.

Laurus PAT. ₹361.99 crore (+124.73%) and ₹368 crore (+126%) both appear. ₹362 crore is used.

Zydus naming trap. Searches for "Zydus Q1 FY27" return Zydus Wellness (consumer/FMCG — revenue ₹1,437 crore, PAT ₹118.9 crore, reported 4 August). That is a different listed company from Zydus Lifesciences (pharma), which reports on 11 August. No Zydus Wellness figure appears anywhere in this report.

Neuland sector mislabelling. One outlet described Neuland as a "specialty chemicals" company. It is an API and contract manufacturing services business and is analysed as pharma here.
What is missing, and why it matters.
• USFDA data direct from the FDA's own databases. The Regulatory tab is now populated, but from press coverage of inspections (Tier 3–4). The FDA publishes inspection classifications, Warning Letters and Import Alerts in searchable public databases — checking those per site would make it Tier 1. No finding was sourced at all for Laurus or Neuland.
• Revenue exposure per manufacturing site — without it, a Form 483 cannot be translated into a revenue-at-risk figure.
• Neuland's market cap and P/E — not sourceable, so the highest-scoring company in this report has no valuation attached.
• EV/EBITDA and forward multiples — only trailing P/E was sourced, which is a blunt instrument on a quarter this distorted.
• Zydus Lifesciences — reports 11 August; regenerate after that to complete the set.
• Sun Pharma absolute EBITDA, and R&D intensity for all companies except Lupin.
• Segment and therapy-area splits, manufacturing footprints, and management/board detail — all in company investor presentations and annual reports.
• Price erosion rates — without them, US revenue growth cannot be fully interpreted.
