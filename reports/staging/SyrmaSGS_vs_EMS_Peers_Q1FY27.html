The Quarter in One Line 3 of 4 declared · Amber's trading window still closed

Every EMS company in this set grew revenue fast. Only Syrma grew margin with it. Syrma's revenue rose 67% with EBITDA margin up 30bps to 11.0% and PAT roughly doubling. Kaynes grew revenue 40% but margin fell 120bps and PAT declined 24%. Dixon grew 21% with margin down 80bps to 3.0%, and its headline PAT of ₹663 crore — up 195% — is largely a fair-value gain: excluding it, PAT was ₹218 crore and adjusted EBITDA actually fell 2%.

The common cause is capacity. Kaynes is spending on OSAT and PCB facilities; Dixon is absorbing the expiry of Mobile PLI 1.0 and higher input costs while lower-margin scale businesses grow fastest. Growth is being bought with margin across the sector — Syrma is the exception, and that is the finding of this quarter.

The EMS Margin Ladder why 3% and 15.6% are both normal

These three companies are not doing the same thing, and their margins should not be compared directly. EMS spans a value ladder, and position on it determines the margin band far more than execution does:

Box-build / assembly
~3%
Dixon. High-volume consumer assembly. Customer owns the design; the manufacturer earns a conversion fee. Enormous revenue, thin margin, working-capital heavy.
Design-led / ODM mix
~11%
Syrma. Contributes design and owns some IP. ODM work earns materially better margin than pure contract assembly — hence the ₹1,200 cr FY27 ODM target.
Industrial / semiconductor
~15.6%
Kaynes. Industrial, aerospace, medical and now OSAT semiconductor packaging. Longer qualification cycles, fewer competitors, better pricing — but heavy capex to get there.

Dixon earning 3% is not underperformance and Kaynes earning 15.6% is not superior execution — they occupy different rungs. What is comparable is the direction of margin within each band, and on that test Syrma is the only one moving up.

A benchmark in our own sector file needs revising
CB Research's capital-goods.md currently states an EMS EBITDA-margin norm of 4–8%. This quarter's data does not support that as a single band: Dixon at 3.0% sits below it while Kaynes at 15.6% and Syrma at 11.0% sit well above it.

The band was derived from box-build economics and applied to all EMS. It should be split by ladder position — roughly 2–4% box-build, 9–13% design-led/ODM, 14–18% industrial and semiconductor. Flagged here rather than silently applied, and noted for correction in the sector file.

Two things this table shows that the P&L does not

1. Dixon's 46.4× is the cheapest multiple here, and it is flattered by the same fair-value gain that flattered its PAT. Trailing earnings include the ₹445 crore non-operating gain discussed in Financials. On operating earnings the effective multiple is materially higher — Dixon's apparent valuation discount to peers is at least partly an artefact of what sits in the denominator. Treat 46.4× as a floor, not a fair reading.

2. The sector has already de-rated hard, and the scores do not line up with the multiples. Kaynes trades 50% below its 52-week high, Dixon 23% below, Amber 18% below. Yet Amber — which has not yet reported and cannot be scored — carries the richest multiple at 179×, while Syrma, the only company that expanded margin this quarter, sits at 82×. Ranking by CB Score gives almost the reverse order of ranking by multiple, which is the useful tension in this set: quality and price are not pointing the same way.

Syrma grew revenue 67% while lifting EBITDA margin 30bps and PAT margin 140bps, with profit roughly doubling. In a quarter where both peers bought growth with margin, doing the opposite is the whole story. Management reiterated FY27 guidance of 35%+ revenue and EBITDA growth, with ₹1,600 crore of exports and ₹1,200 crore of ODM revenue targeted — the ODM number matters most, since that is the rung of the ladder where margin actually improves.

The case against, and it is a real one: valuation. Syrma trades at roughly 82× trailing earnings. The FY27 guidance is close to what that multiple already assumes, so this is a company that must deliver 35% growth rather than one where 35% growth would be a surprise. A single quarter of missed execution is expensive at that price.

Kaynes deserves a fairer reading than its score. Its 24% PAT decline is capex-phase drag from OSAT and PCB capacity — spending ahead of revenue is what building semiconductor packaging looks like, and it still holds the highest margin in the set at 15.6%. A score of 70 reflects this quarter, not the strategy. Dixon's 50 is the more concerning number, because its headline looked the best in the set and the underlying operating result was the weakest: adjusted EBITDA down 2%, margin down 80bps, and the PAT growth almost entirely non-operating.

This is a research read of disclosed fundamentals, not investment advice. One quarter, three of four companies, no order-book data, and valuation available for only one name. Not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security.
