The Quarter in One Line Q1 FY27 · all banks standalone unless noted

Margins compressed across the sector while asset quality held — and the two largest banks diverged sharply. ICICI Bank grew profit 16% on 19.6% advances growth and the strongest capital position of the group. HDFC Bank grew profit just 5%, with NIM at 3.26% — described in results coverage as its lowest on record — and CASA slipping to 32.30%. Axis and Kotak both delivered low-20s profit growth, Axis on falling provisions with management calling 3.46% NIM the cycle bottom, Kotak on a sector-leading 4.53% margin. IndusInd's 72% profit jump is real but comes off a depressed base, with NII up just 1% and GNPA at 3.25% — roughly triple the big-four average.

Asset Quality the axis that sinks banks — and the one that lags

A bank can report record profit while quietly accumulating stress, which is why asset quality is read separately from earnings. On this quarter's disclosure the big four are in comfortable territory — all between 1.17% and 1.67% GNPA — and IndusInd sits well outside it at 3.25%, though improving.

The important caveat: GNPA can fall through write-offs and recoveries while fresh stress keeps forming. Slippage ratio and credit cost — the metrics that separate genuine improvement from cosmetic improvement — were not disclosed in the sources located here. Without them, this section shows the level but not the direction of formation, and should not be read as a clean bill of health.

Method. CB Score = Growth 30% + Profitability 25% + Asset Quality 25% + Capital & Outlook 20%, scored 1–10 per component and scaled to 100. Banking substitution applied: Asset Quality replaces the generic PAT Quality component, because for a bank asset quality is profit quality; and Debt/Leverage is never a component, since borrowing is a bank's raw material rather than a risk. Bands: ≥75 🟢 · 55–74 🟡 · <55 🔴.

Component basis. Growth = advances and deposits growth, plus NII growth. Profitability = NIM level and direction. Asset Quality = GNPA/NNPA level and trend. Capital = CRAR/CET-1 headroom above the 11.5%/8% minimums. Where a component input was not disclosed in sourced material — advances and deposits growth for HDFC Bank, Kotak and IndusInd; capital ratios for three banks — the component was scored on the inputs available. Those scores are therefore weaker evidence than ICICI's and Axis's, and the table should not be read as five equally-grounded numbers.
Read this before using the ranking
IndusInd's 49 reflects this quarter's disclosed position — high GNPA, flat NII — not a judgement on its turnaround, which is visibly underway on both asset quality and margin. A score built from one quarter of a recovery understates a bank that is improving and overstates one that is coasting. This is a research read of disclosed fundamentals, not investment advice and not a recommendation.
Caveats, Conflicts & Sources
⚠ Source conflicts encountered and how they were resolved
Kotak PAT — consolidated vs standalone. Outlets reported both "₹4,123 cr, +26%" and "₹5,480 cr, +23%" for the same quarter. Both are correct: the first is standalone, the second consolidated (including securities, AMC and insurance subsidiaries). Standalone is used throughout for like-for-like comparison with the other four banks; the consolidated figure is shown on the Kotak card.

Kotak GNPA — garbled in source. One outlet stated GNPA "increased from 1.48% to 1.18%", which describes a decrease. The figures are right and the verb is wrong: GNPA improved to 1.18% from 1.48% a year earlier.

HDFC Bank NIM direction. One source reports NIM "narrowed to 3.26% from 3.38% in the March quarter, its lowest level on record"; another describes 3.26% as "flat YoY and QoQ". These are irreconcilable on direction. The level of 3.26% is consistent across both and is what this report uses; the sequential direction is flagged as disputed and should be checked against the bank's own presentation.

IndusInd PAT growth. Reported as +72% YoY (from ₹604 cr) by most outlets and +88% by one, the latter appearing to be a sequential comparison. +72% YoY is used.
What is missing, and why it matters. This report is built from results coverage rather than the banks' own investor presentations. That leaves real gaps:
• Slippage ratio and credit cost for all five — the metrics that distinguish genuine asset-quality improvement from write-off-driven improvement. Without them the Asset Quality section shows level, not formation.
• Advances and deposits growth for HDFC Bank, Kotak and IndusInd.
• CASA for four of five; capital ratios for three of five.
• RoA and RoE for all five — the cleanest cross-bank profitability comparisons.
• ICICI Bank's NIM.
All of this is published in the banks' quarterly investor presentations. Pulling those directly is the right step before this moves out of staging.
