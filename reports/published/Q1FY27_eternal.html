Eternal Limited — Q1 FY27 Deep Dive · Quarter Ended 30 Jun 2026 · NSE: ETERNAL · CB Research
Eternal Limited (formerly Zomato) · Group CEO: Albinder Dhindsa · CMP ₹289.5 · Mkt Cap ₹2.79L Cr · Results Approved 22 Jul 2026
🔴 What Changed This Quarter — Blinkit's Full Turn

Q1 FY27 is the quarter Blinkit stopped being a drag on group profitability and started contributing to it — adjusted EBITDA of ₹102 Cr (0.6% of NOV) versus a ₹162 Cr loss in Q1 FY26, the fifth straight quarter of margin improvement. Consolidated revenue crossed ₹20,000 Cr for the first time, up 182% YoY, powered by Blinkit's shift to a first-party (1P) inventory-led model that recognises full gross value rather than net commission — meaning the headline revenue jump is partly an accounting-model change, not pure like-for-like growth (management still reports genuine 21% QoQ quick-commerce volume growth, in line with seasonal patterns). The quarter's most important structural signal, however, is tax: Eternal has now fully used up its standalone carried-forward losses and has begun recognising tax on business income, not just treasury income — which explains why net profit fell 47% sequentially (tax expense rose from ₹54 Cr to ₹180 Cr) even as pre-tax profit rose 19% QoQ and adjusted EBITDA hit a record ₹555 Cr. Read correctly, this is a business getting healthier reporting a smaller number, not a business getting weaker.

Corporate Action: Board approved transfer of the 'Nugget by Zomato' AI support-platform business to wholly-owned subsidiary Carthero Technologies Pvt Ltd (CTPL) for ₹350 Cr cash consideration, as part of internal restructuring — no shareholding change for any entity. Management Guidance & Commentary

Store Trajectory: 200 net new Blinkit dark stores added in Q1, taking the network to 2,443; management targets 3,000 stores by March 2027.

Capex Escalation: Capex per new store has been raised from ~₹1 Cr to a targeted ₹2.5 Cr, reflecting a shift toward larger-format, "gourmet" and premiumised stores — management guides to a 40% steady-state ROCE for the quick-commerce business despite the higher capex intensity and ~12 days of working capital.

Long-Term Targets: 60% NOV CAGR and $1 Bn consolidated adjusted EBITDA by FY29 reaffirmed as "increasingly achievable" per multiple brokerages' readthrough of management commentary.

Strategic Focus (Group CEO Albinder Dhindsa): Assortment expansion in the top 8 cities, geographic expansion into the next 30, demand densification, and premiumisation through "gourmet" store formats.

Cost & Margin Headwinds to Track

1. Minimum Wage Resets: Several states implementing minimum-wage revisions pushed Blinkit's direct cost per order up ~2.0% QoQ to ₹115, a 10bps drag on contribution margin despite a 60bps take-rate improvement.

2. Tax Normalisation: Standalone carried-forward losses are now fully utilised — Eternal will report tax on business income from here on, meaning PAT growth will structurally track EBITDA growth more closely than in prior "tax-shielded" quarters.

3. Blinkit Inventory Risk: Under the inventory-led (1P) model, Blinkit separately disclosed a ₹308 Cr hit from inventory spoilage, damage and theft in the quarter — a new cost line investors should track as the model scales.

4. Hyperpure Sequential Dip: B2B supplies revenue fell 55% QoQ to ₹1,034 Cr even as adjusted EBITDA rose to ₹6 Cr (from ₹1 Cr) — margin over volume, worth watching for a rebound in scale.

What is "Take Rate"? Take rate is the slice of every rupee of order value (NOV) that Blinkit actually keeps as its own revenue — it works like a commission percentage. If a customer's basket is worth ₹500 and Blinkit's take rate is 27.5%, Blinkit books roughly ₹137.50 of that as revenue; the rest flows through to product cost, seller payouts, and other pass-throughs. Blinkit's take rate rose 60bps QoQ to 27.5% in Q1 FY27 — meaning Blinkit is retaining a slightly larger share of every order than it did last quarter, largely from ad monetisation, packaging/handling fees, and platform charges layered on top of the product margin. A rising take rate is generally a positive profitability signal, but it needs to be read alongside direct cost per order (which also rose, to ₹115) — that's why contribution margin, not take rate alone, ultimately dipped 10bps this quarter. In short: take rate tells you how much Blinkit earns per order; contribution margin tells you how much of that it actually keeps after direct costs.

Blinkit's 21% QoQ NOV growth (vs 8.2% in Q4 FY26) alongside a sequential rise in monthly transacting users and order frequency signals genuine market-share gains, not just seasonal quick-commerce demand — even as AOV dipped marginally on mix and larger-store rollouts.

🏆 CB Research Verdict
Q1 FY27 is a "quality beat, optics miss" quarter for Eternal. Every operating metric that matters — adjusted EBITDA, Blinkit margin, food-delivery NOV growth, contribution margin trend — improved, while the one number the market watches most closely (headline PAT) fell sequentially purely because of a tax base-effect, not weaker operations. For an investor, the real work this quarter is separating the tax story from the business story: on the business story, Blinkit's transition from a five-quarter margin-improvement trend to genuine profitability is the single most important New-Age sector data point this earnings season. The risk to watch is whether the doubling of per-store capex (to ₹2.5 Cr) actually delivers the guided 40% steady-state ROCE, or whether it becomes the next multi-quarter drag the way dark-store expansion capex was in FY24–25.
Meesho Limited — Q1 FY27 Update · Quarter Ended 30 Jun 2026 · NSE: MEESHO · CB Research
Meesho Limited · Social Commerce · Bharat-First · CFO: Dhiresh Bansal · Reported 23 Jul 2026
🟣 The Story: Losses Keep Halving, Growth Stays Fast

Meesho posted its fourth consecutive quarter of narrowing losses alongside accelerating revenue growth — a combination that is genuinely rare for a loss-making e-commerce platform at this scale. Net loss fell 54% YoY to ₹133 Cr while revenue grew 48% YoY to ₹3,713 Cr, and contribution margin expanded 54bps sequentially to 4.6% of NMV, the clearest evidence that monetization quality (not just order growth) is improving. The company simultaneously invested ₹75 Cr into Meesho Groceries and continued integrating its Kirana Club acquisition, signalling it is using an improving core to fund new-vertical optionality rather than pulling back on growth investment.

CB Assist — Ask About Eternal's Q1 FY27 Results
English
Why did Eternal's profit fall 47% sequentially if the business actually improved?
Because of tax, not operations. Eternal's standalone carried-forward losses are now fully used up, so it has started paying tax on business income for the first time. Tax expense jumped from ₹54 Cr in Q4 FY26 to ₹180 Cr in Q1 FY27. Pre-tax profit and adjusted EBITDA both rose sequentially — it's the tax line, not the business, that pulled reported PAT down.
Is Blinkit actually profitable now, or is this a one-off?
This is Blinkit's fifth consecutive quarter of adjusted-EBITDA-margin improvement, and the second straight quarter of positive adjusted EBITDA (₹102 Cr this quarter vs a ₹162 Cr loss a year ago). It's a trend, not a one-off — but the margin (0.6% of NOV) is still thin, and capex per store is rising sharply (to ₹2.5 Cr), so the next few quarters will show whether profitability holds as expansion accelerates.
Why did revenue jump 182% YoY — is that organic growth?
Partly an accounting effect. Blinkit shifted to a first-party (1P) inventory-led model, which recognises the full gross value of goods sold rather than just a net commission — this inflates reported revenue growth. The cleaner like-for-like indicator is NOV (Net Order Value), which grew 86% YoY for Blinkit and 54% YoY for the consolidated B2C business — still very strong, but well below the headline 182%.
How does Meesho's Q1 FY27 compare to Eternal's?
Different stage of the same broader story: both are showing improving unit economics, but Eternal is now solidly profitable (PAT positive four quarters running) while Meesho is still loss-making, just losing much less (−54% YoY loss narrowing vs +268% YoY profit growth for Eternal). Meesho's revenue growth (+48%) is also on a genuine like-for-like basis, without an accounting-model distortion the way Eternal's headline number has.
