Quarter Snapshot — Update 17 Aug 2026: All 10 Listed Companies Have Now Reported

  • All 10 companies posted double-digit YoY revenue growth, and 9 of 10 posted YoY PAT growth — only Senco's profit slipped (-3%) despite the strongest revenue growth (+67%) in the group, as EBITDA margin compressed to 7.0% from 10.1% on discounting and higher gold costs.   • Sharpest PAT growth this quarter: Sky Gold (+140.65%), Goldiam (+119.59%) and SGIL (+104.87%) — all B2B manufacturers/exporters — comfortably outpaced the store-first retailers on profit growth, aided by operating leverage, a tariff refund (Goldiam) and new-capacity ramp-up (SGIL).

Why Kalyan's Share Price Went Up

  • Citi's two-notch upgrade to Buy with a ₹750 target (~97% implied upside) landed alongside the results, directly citing confidence in the FOCO-led expansion strategy.
  • Profit growth (+31%) outran the market's initial worry — the preliminary business update in July had shown revenue growth of only 38% (below Citi's own 45% estimate), so the final 45% audited number and 31% PAT growth reassured investors that the shortfall fear was overdone.
  • Return-ratio improvement is now visible in hard numbers — ROCE at 30.3% and ROE at 25.8% give the FOCO thesis a track record rather than just a narrative.
  • Candere's 112% revenue growth validated the digital-omnichannel pivot, turning what had been a drag (a ₹12cr loss as recently as Q4 FY25) into a growth engine.
  • Margin protection via gold recirculation — with over 46% of Q1 revenue (and 55%+ in June alone) sourced from recycled gold, investors read this as a credible hedge against the 6%→15% import-duty hike that hurt sequential margins elsewhere in the sector.

Net effect: shares rallied ~19% over two trading sessions following the update, before settling at ₹591.40 (down 2.73% intraday) on 4 Aug 2026 as some profit-taking set in after the initial pop.

Why Thangamayil's Share Price Fell — 27% Over 3 Sessions, Despite a Results Beat

  • Cautious forward guidance overshadowed a strong quarter. Even with PAT +86% YoY, management said there had been no visible sales improvement in the first 28 days of Q2 FY27 — that single forward-looking line drove the stock to a 10% lower circuit on results day itself.
  • Sequential deterioration was hard to ignore. Revenue fell 6% QoQ and net profit fell a sharp 41% QoQ from Q4 FY26 — the market weighted the quarter-on-quarter trend more heavily than the year-on-year comparison.
  • Gold import duty hike (6%→15%, effective 13 May 2026) and rupee depreciation raised procurement costs and prompted customers to defer purchases in anticipation of a price correction — directly hitting near-term volume.
  • West Asia conflict uncertainty and lower expatriate remittances were cited by management as additional demand drags for Tamil Nadu's NRI-linked jewellery buying.
  • Volume data confirmed the demand softness — gold ornament volumes fell 11% QoQ (even as they still rose 9% YoY) — validating the market's concern that rupee-value growth was masking a real volume slowdown.

Expansion continues regardless: 2 more Chennai outlets planned 23 Aug 2026 and 2 more on 13 Sep 2026, taking the network toward 72 stores — management expects the deferred demand to return in H2 FY27.

Business Model Deep Dive — How BlueStone Actually Works

Origin & evolution

  • Founded 2011, incubated at GrowthStory (K. Ganesh's venture studio) by CEO Gaurav Singh Kushwaha — started as a pure online jewellery e-commerce platform, betting that large marketplaces (Amazon, Flipkart) would struggle to enter trust-heavy, supply-chain-intensive categories like jewellery.
  • Introduced "Home Try-On" early on to solve the online-jewellery "touch and feel" trust problem.
  • 2018-2021: pivoted to omnichannel after recognising ~90% of India's jewellery sales still happen in physical stores — opened its first "experience store" in Delhi and scaled to metros, backed by Premji Invest and Hero Enterprise.
  • 2022-2025: scaling phase — raised ~$65mn (₹550Cr) in 2023 near a $450mn valuation, later reaching ~$970mn pre-IPO; Nikhil Kamath (Zerodha) personally invested ₹100Cr; listed August 2025.

Manufacturing & supply chain

  • Vertically integrated — manufactures over 75% of products in-house at facilities in Mumbai and Jaipur, using CNC machining and a Just-In-Time (JIT) model rather than holding large finished-goods inventory.
  • Design-to-delivery in 3-5 days — closer in operating logic to Lenskart's manufacture-on-demand model than to a traditional jeweller or an online marketplace.
  • Portfolio spans 7,400-8,000+ designs across gold, diamond, platinum and gemstone jewellery; diamonds carry IGI/GIA-linked certification.
  • JIT + in-house manufacturing reduces stagnant inventory and dead stock, and partly cushions the business from sharp gold-price swings versus retailers who pre-stock large finished inventories.

Store economics & revenue mix

  • ~85% of revenue is direct retail sale of jewellery, generated across 200 company-owned stores and 75 franchisee-operated stores (franchisee model is capital-partner-driven, but BlueStone still controls operations, inventory and staffing — comparable in spirit to Kalyan's FOCO).
  • New store locations are chosen using online browsing data, cart-addition patterns and demand mapping — stores are placed where digital demand already exists, not on instinct.
  • 75% of stores reach operational break-even within 3 months of opening; average revenue per store was ₹76.8 lakh/month in FY25.
  • Same-store sales growth has been normalising as the base scales: 72.06% (FY23) → 51.16% (FY24) → 32.14% (FY25) → 39% (Q1 FY27) — still strong, but decelerating as expected with size.

Customer & growth levers

  • Targets a younger, design-forward, everyday-wear buyer — lightweight, contemporary designs starting around ₹5,000 — deliberately different from the wedding-set-led, gold-bullion-linked demand that dominates legacy jewellers' revenue.
  • Repeat-purchase revenue share has risen from 34.67% (FY23) to 44.61% (FY25), and average order value reached ₹59,907 in Q2 FY26 — both signs of a maturing, increasingly loyal customer base rather than one-time buyers.
  • Online engagement scaled 3.65x in two years (8.19 crore sessions FY23 → 29.89 crore FY25), feeding the store-placement and merchandising data loop.
  • "Big Gold Upgrade" — BlueStone's own version of an old-gold exchange scheme, letting customers trade up to higher-caratage jewellery.
  • New margin lever: a 74%-owned lab-grown-diamond (LGD) subsidiary, Ethereal, targeting price-conscious, environmentally-conscious Gen-Z buyers — LGD typically carries better retail margins than natural stones.

Net positioning: BlueStone is the only company in this dashboard built online-first with manufacturing and stores layered on afterward — every other retailer here (Titan, Kalyan, Senco, Thangamayil, PNG) started as a physical-store chain and is now adding digital/omnichannel capability on top of an existing store network.

Why the model is different from every other company in this dashboard

  • Built online-first (founded 2011) with physical stores layered on afterward — the reverse sequence of store-first retailers now adding digital arms.
  • Design and inventory engineered centrally for a wide range of price points; stores function as an experience/trust layer for customers who discover and configure products online.
  • CEO Gaurav Singh Kushwaha attributed the turnaround to embedded operating leverage — EBITDA grew at nearly 3x the pace of revenue this quarter.

Why Tamil Nadu leads on aggregate gold demand

  • Scale of the state economy: TN's large population combined with broad-based income (not concentrated in one city) gives it the highest aggregate/total-volume gold demand among individual Indian states, even though Kerala has shown higher per-capita gold spending in some consumer surveys (driven by Gulf remittance inflows).
  • Deep cultural embedding of gold in weddings and festivals: gold purchases are near-mandatory for Tamil weddings; nationally, weddings alone drive ~43% of household gold buying, and Tamil Nadu's wedding culture is especially gold-intensive.
  • Preference for plain 22K gold jewellery over lighter studded/diamond pieces — plain-gold demand is more gold-weight-intensive per purchase than diamond-led buying, which pushes up volume.
  • IT and manufacturing hub status: TN's economy is diversified and prosperous, giving broad middle-class purchasing power — the segment that, per India Gold Policy Centre research, accounts for the largest total volume of gold bought nationally (~56% of volume from households earning ₹2-10 lakh/year).
  • A dense, established local-jeweller ecosystem (Coimbatore is a major gold-jewellery manufacturing hub) — meaning intense competition from trusted legacy/family jewellers, which is precisely why Kalyan judged its national-format stores insufficient and built a distinct regional brand instead.

Management's own framing: Ramesh Kalyanaraman, Executive Director, called Tamil Nadu "one of India's largest and most mature jewellery markets," and the natural launch market for Kalyan's first regional brand, ATM — designed specifically to compete with TN's legacy/unorganised jewellers on their own turf rather than to cannibalise Kalyan's existing national-format stores. 4. Gold Market Scenario — India & South India

National gold demand & price backdrop

  • India remains the world's second-largest gold consumer. Q1 FY27 (Apr-Jun 2026) demand fell 6% YoY by volume to 131.4 tonnes (World Gold Council), even as record prices pushed rupee spend to an all-time-high ₹1.98 lakh crore for the quarter — a "buy less, spend more" pattern playing out across the sector.
  • Domestic gold prices have risen sharply — 24K touched an all-time high around ₹1,69,349/10g in early March 2026 before easing; as of 16 Aug 2026, 24K stood near ₹1,55,130/10g and 22K near ₹1,42,200/10g, still roughly double 2024 levels.
  • Investment demand (bars/coins/ETFs) has structurally gained share versus jewellery — bar-and-coin demand nearly matched jewellery demand in Q1 FY27, the highest such share on WGC record since 2013, as some buyers treat gold as a portfolio hedge rather than an ornament purchase.
  • The gold import duty was hiked from 6% to 15% effective 13 May 2026 — the largest single revision on record, fully reversing a July 2024 cut — lifting domestic gold prices further and reportedly triggering a rise in gold smuggling per Parliament disclosures.
  • The organised jewellery retail segment continues gaining share from the unorganised sector (BIS hallmarking, GST compliance, digital/branded preference) but unorganised players still account for an estimated 62-65% of the overall market nationally — meaning the runway for organised players like Lalithaa to keep taking share remains long.

South India & Tamil Nadu specifics

  • South India accounts for roughly 40% of India's total gold jewellery demand — the largest of any region — ahead of West India (~25%), North India (~20-21%) and East India (~14-15%).
  • Tamil Nadu is India's single largest gold-consuming state, driven by high gold-ownership culture, dense organised and unorganised retail competition, and one of the country's highest gold-loan markets (Tamil Nadu alone accounts for over ₹4.1 lakh crore in gold-backed loans, the largest of any state, as southern states collectively command roughly three-quarters of India's gold-loan market).
  • Kerala's gold demand is additionally supported by large Gulf-remittance inflows; this is a regional demand driver Lalithaa does not currently tap directly, since it has no stores in Kerala as of the RHP.
  • This regional concentration cuts both ways: Lalithaa benefits from operating in India's single deepest gold-demand region, but 100% dependence on the South means any region-specific shock (a weak monsoon hitting rural incomes, a state-level economic slowdown, or intensified local competition from Malabar Gold, Joyalukkas, Kalyan, Thangamayil or GRT) hits the whole business at once — there is no other region to fall back on.

Fault lines vs. the listed cohort

  • Highest revenue/store, but thinnest disclosed hedging discipline. Lalithaa's ₹410Cr/store output is genuinely best-in-class, but peers with formal hedge programmes (PN Gadgil ~70-80% coverage, Kalyan's recycled-gold engine at 46%+ of revenue) have built structural buffers against gold-price reversals that Lalithaa's RHP does not describe having in place.
  • Single-region concentration is the most extreme in the dashboard. Thangamayil is Tamil-Nadu-only and already shows how sharply sentiment can swing on regional demand commentary (its stock fell 27% over three sessions in Jul 2026 on a single cautious remark). Lalithaa spans five southern markets but has zero presence outside the South — a strictly regional profile even by this dashboard's standard.
  • A larger, more specific legacy governance overhang than typical IPO risk-factor boilerplate. A March 2021 income-tax department raid on LJML's corporate office and stores led to tax demand orders spanning Assessment Years 2015-16 through 2021-22; the resulting contingent liability was reported at ~₹1,055.7cr in FY2023 by ICRA, broadly consistent with the ~₹1,066cr GST/tax dispute figure cited in current IPO reviews. This is a materially larger and more specific item than the ₹56.03cr of "routine" contingent liabilities the RHP separately discloses as of 31 Mar 2026 — investors should read the RHP's litigation section directly rather than rely on any single secondary summary, including this one.
  • Pricing looks cheap only if FY26's margin holds. The ~11.1x FY26 P/E is a genuine discount to Kalyan/Titan, but that P/E is anchored to a FY26 PAT number brokerages themselves say was significantly gold-price-assisted — if margins revert toward the FY24/25 ~2% band once prices stabilise, the effective forward multiple would be meaningfully higher than 11.1x.
  • No public listed track record at all — every other company in this dashboard has at least one full quarter (often years) of market-tested price behaviour to reference; Lalithaa's entire "performance" record to date is private-company financials and RHP disclosures, so execution, disclosure quality and capital-allocation discipline as a public company are all unproven.
