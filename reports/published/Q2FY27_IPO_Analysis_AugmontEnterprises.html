Business Model

02 / Two Platforms, One Value Chain

Incorporated in October 2012 and headquartered in Mumbai, Augmont operates a technology-led, asset-light omnichannel model across the entire precious-metals value chain — procurement, in-house refining, bullion trading, digital gold/silver, jewellery manufacturing, international sales and gold-backed fintech rails. As of 31 March 2026 it had a presence across 24 states, 296 permanent employees (plus 3 contractual), refining capacity of 284 tonnes/year (Rudrapur + Mumbai) and a jewellery unit at Sitapur SEZ, Jaipur.

Enterprise · B2B

Augmont SPOT

A proprietary electronic OTC bullion-trading platform giving jewellers, bullion dealers and manufacturers real-time price discovery with assured physical delivery.

  • 5,223+ registered enterprise/business members (as of 31 Mar 2026)
  • 20 spot delivery centres across 13 states (9 direct, 11 franchisee)
  • Enterprise + international sales contributed 92.9% of FY26 revenue from operations
  • Bars meet BIS, LBMA & India Good Delivery purity standards
Consumer · B2C

Augmont Gold For All

A consumer app for buying, selling and storing digital gold/silver, monthly SIP-style accumulation plans, coins, and physical redemption — including via partners.

  • 49.6 million+ registered consumers (direct + partner-sourced)
  • 54,934,891 transactions processed on the platform in FY26
  • Redemption tie-ups with jewellers including Kalyan Jewellers and CaratLane
  • Built in-house by a ~40-person technology team

In Simple Terms

A gold factory + a gold shop, combined

1
Buy the raw metal

Augmont buys gold and silver from banks, imports semi-refined bars, and buys back old/scrap gold — like a flour mill buying wheat.

2
Refine it themselves

Instead of outsourcing, they run their own refineries to purify it into standard-quality bars — so they control quality and skip a middleman's cut.

3
Sell wholesale (~93% of business)

Jewellers and bullion dealers buy gold bars online for their own shops — an online marketplace where Augmont is essentially India's largest gold wholesaler.

4
Sell retail, in tiny pieces

Ordinary people buy gold worth as little as ₹1 on the app, set up monthly gold savings, buy coins, or take a loan against gold.

Why revenue looks huge but profit looks tiny

Imagine buying gold for ₹100 and selling it for ₹100.30 — you made 30 paise, but the full ₹100 counts as "revenue." Do that at massive scale and you get exactly Augmont's numbers: ₹94,186 Cr of revenue but only ₹348 Cr of profit. It's not a red flag — it's how a high-volume, thin-margin bullion wholesaler works, not a jewellery brand marking up prices.

One line: Augmont buys raw gold, refines it in-house, sells it in bulk to jewellers online (the big money-mover), and separately lets ordinary people buy tiny bits of gold through an app (the growth story).

Key Risk Factors — expand each

01 · Wafer-thin operating margins+

FY26 PAT margin was only 0.37% and EBITDA margin 0.41% — inherent to high-volume, low-margin bullion trading. Profitability is highly sensitive to inventory management and cost control.

02 · Customer & supplier concentration+

Top 10 customers contributed 52.09% of FY26 revenue (vs ~36% in FY25/FY24); top 10 suppliers accounted for 74.18% of FY26 procurement. No long-term agreements exist with either customers or suppliers.

03 · Revenue skew to enterprise/international sales+

Enterprise and international sales made up 92.90% of FY26 revenue from operations (95.72% in FY25, 95.88% in FY24), leaving the business exposed to swings in institutional/export demand.

04 · Gold/silver price volatility+

Bullion price swings affect both demand for Augmont's products and the mark-to-market valuation of its inventory, flowing directly through revenue and margins.

05 · Platform & cybersecurity dependence+

Nearly all revenue routes through the proprietary Augmont SPOT and Gold For All platforms; any outage, disruption or data-security breach could materially affect operations and reputation.

06 · No directly comparable listed peer+

Per the RHP, Augmont has no directly comparable listed peer in India or globally, which makes valuation benchmarking difficult and reduces the reliability of any single "fair value" estimate.

SWOT Analysis

04 / Strategic Position

SStrengths

  • Fully integrated value chain — procurement, in-house refining (284 t/yr), trading, digital gold and jewellery manufacturing under one roof
  • Recognised "Number 1 Gold Platform of the Year 2024–25" (India Gold Conference); Best Bullion Dealer and Leading Gold Refiner awards
  • Near debt-free balance sheet — D/E of 0.01x, borrowings down from ₹54.86 Cr (FY24) to ₹12.67 Cr (FY26)
  • Strong return ratios — FY26 ROE 51.0%, ROCE 40.3%, aided by fast bullion-trade settlement cycles
  • Large digital footprint — 49.6M+ consumers and 20 physical delivery centres across 13 states
  • Experienced promoter-led management with 13+ years of operating history since 2012

WWeaknesses

  • Extremely thin margins — FY26 PAT margin of just 0.37%, typical of commodity bullion trading
  • High customer concentration — top 10 customers were 52.09% of FY26 revenue
  • High supplier concentration — top 10 suppliers were 74.18% of FY26 procurement, with no long-term contracts
  • No directly comparable listed peer, making independent valuation benchmarking difficult
  • ₹205 Cr of the ₹825 Cr issue is an OFS — this portion does not add fresh capital to the company
  • Total assets were non-linear — rose to ₹1,857 Cr in FY25 then fell to ₹1,257 Cr in FY26

OOpportunities

  • India remains among the world's largest gold consumers, with rising formalisation of the bullion trade
  • Fresh-issue proceeds earmarked for inventory scaling into under-penetrated Tier 3/4 markets
  • Growth headroom in gold-backed financial services and lending-adjacent technology
  • Digital gold SIP-style plans can widen retail participation as financial literacy improves
  • Export/international bullion sales offer diversification beyond the domestic enterprise channel

TThreats

  • Gold/silver price volatility from global rates, currency and geopolitical shifts can swing both revenue and inventory valuation
  • Regulatory changes to bullion import duty, taxation or trading norms could raise compliance costs
  • Cybersecurity or platform-availability incidents on Augmont SPOT/Gold For All would hit a digital-first model directly
  • Thin margins leave little cushion to absorb price shocks or a slowdown in institutional demand
  • Competing bullion platforms, banks and fintech-gold entrants could pressure trading spreads
