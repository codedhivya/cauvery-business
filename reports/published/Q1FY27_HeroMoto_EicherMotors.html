Financial Results Deep-Dive — Bajaj Auto · Standalone vs Consolidated

Standalone operating metrics (revenue +36%, EBITDA +25%, PAT +29%) are genuinely strong. The -16.9% consolidated headline is purely a high-base effect from a one-off ₹722 Cr gain in Q1 FY26 — not a sign of current weakness.

The core differentiator: These four companies sit at very different points on India's two-wheeler value ladder. Hero MotoCorp is unambiguously the "common man's bike" — the world's largest 2W maker by volume, built on the ₹75,000-95,000 commuter segment (Splendor, HF Deluxe), prioritising reach and affordability over margin. Eicher's Royal Enfield sits at the opposite pole as the "high-end bike" — premium 350-650cc middleweight motorcycles at ₹1.5-4+ lakh, selling a fraction of Hero's volume but commanding by far the richest margin and valuation. TVS and Bajaj are the diversified players straddling the middle — spanning commuter to premium to EV to exports — which is exactly why their product mix, margins and multiples sit between the other two.

Key Analytical Takeaway

Four very different businesses reported strong Q1 FY27s, but the underlying models diverge sharply. Hero MotoCorp is the volume king and the truest "common man's bike" — 16.77 lakh units this quarter, the highest of the four, built on the world's largest-selling commuter franchise, but with the thinnest margin (13.3%) and cheapest valuation (~20× P/E) as the market prices in its low pricing power. Eicher's Royal Enfield is the polar opposite — the genuine "high-end bike," selling roughly a fifth of Hero's volume at Q1 FY27's richest margin (~24%) and valuation (~38-42× P/E), proving premium brand equity can outearn scale. TVS (EV leader, richly valued, margin still undisclosed) and Bajaj (higher-margin diversified play spanning 3W, exports and now KTM/Triumph) sit in between, each pursuing a different route to bridge the volume-versus-margin trade-off that Hero and Eicher represent at the extremes.
