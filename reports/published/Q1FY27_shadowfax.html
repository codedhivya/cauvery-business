Read of the Quarter

Growth + margin, together

Revenue up 65% and PAT up 715% in the same quarter — operating leverage is now visibly showing up in the P&L, not just in management commentary.

Balance sheet gives room to execute

Near-zero net debt and positive free cash flow mean the FY27 growth guidance upgrade (38–40%) doesn't need to be funded by dilution or debt.

Valuation is the swing factor

At 84x P/E, the stock still prices in a lot of future growth. The dashboard's other tabs (Valuations, SWOT) unpack what could re-rate it further — or compress it.

Business Model

Shadowfax positions itself as a technology-led, asset-right third-party logistics (3PL) platform — it owns the software and orchestration layer, but deliberately avoids owning most physical assets, leasing infrastructure and using a crowdsourced delivery workforce instead.

What the company does
ElementDetail
Core propositionEnd-to-end express logistics and large-scale hyperlocal delivery through a proprietary tech platform and nationwide asset-light network.
Customer baseE-commerce marketplaces (Flipkart, Amazon, Meesho), D2C brands, quick-commerce platforms (Zepto, Blinkit, Amazon Now), food delivery, and mobility platforms.
Network modelLeased first-mile, last-mile and sort centres rather than owned real estate; delivery executed via a crowdsourced partner fleet rather than an owned vehicle/employee fleet.
Founded2015 (Bengaluru); listed on NSE/BSE via IPO in January 2026.
Scale (Q1 FY27)16,372 pincodes, 5,095 operating touchpoints, 53+ lakh sq ft of operating area, 2.9 lakh+ unique active delivery partners in the quarter.
How it makes money

Express Parcel

Intercity e-commerce parcels, same-day delivery, reverse logistics and exchange services for marketplaces and D2C brands. The largest and fastest-growing revenue line.

Hyperlocal

On-demand delivery for quick-commerce, food-delivery and other instant-delivery platforms — a shorter-distance, higher-frequency order model.

Prime Large

A premium sub-vertical for heavy/volumetric shipments over 15kg, priced and routed differently from standard parcels — Shadowfax's newest growth engine.

SME & Personal Courier (Shadowfax 360)

A self-service digital shipping platform launched for small businesses and individual sellers, extending Shadowfax's network beyond large enterprise contracts.

Why the model matters for the numbers

Because Shadowfax leases rather than owns, and pays its delivery workforce per-transaction rather than as fixed payroll, its cost base flexes with volume. That is the structural reason fixed-asset turnover runs at ~21x (see Cash Flow & Ratios tab) and why EBITDA margins have been able to expand quickly once volume passed a critical mass — there is comparatively little fixed depreciation or idle capacity dragging on margins.

Discount-to-Delhivery thesis

Sell-side commentary (JM Financial, cited via independent research notes) flags Shadowfax trading at roughly a 30% EV/EBITDA discount to Delhivery despite comparable-or-higher growth and materially better ROIC — a gap bulls argue is structurally unjustified given Shadowfax's ~21x fixed-asset turnover versus Delhivery's ~6x.

From 1,018x to 84x

At IPO (Jan 2026), Shadowfax priced at roughly 1,018x FY25 earnings — an eye-watering multiple built almost entirely on a growth/optionality bet. Eight-fold profit growth since then has done the heavy lifting in bringing the multiple down to a still-rich 84x TTM.

SWOT Analysis

Strengths

  • Market leader by order volume in same-day delivery, reverse logistics and quick commerce in India
  • Asset-light, tech-led network with superior fixed-asset turnover (~21x) vs. peers
  • Negligible net debt; balance sheet gives flexibility to fund automation capex internally
  • Fast-scaling Prime Large (heavy cargo) vertical diversifying revenue beyond parcel delivery
  • Tight, near-neutral working capital cycle for a logistics business of this scale

Weaknesses

  • Thin absolute PAT margins (2.7% FY26) despite the improvement — still early in profitability journey
  • High customer concentration among a small set of e-commerce/quick-commerce platforms
  • Crowdsourced delivery workforce model carries service-quality and retention execution risk
  • Cash-on-delivery exposure ties up working capital and adds collection risk

Opportunities

  • Structural formalisation of India's 3PL market — outsourced share still only 12–15% industry-wide, leaving large runway
  • Quick-commerce and D2C brand growth directly expands Shadowfax's addressable volumes
  • Prime Large ARR trajectory (₹75 Cr and climbing) can become a meaningfully higher-margin second engine
  • Potential further re-rating if the EV/EBITDA discount to Delhivery narrows

Threats

  • Large clients (e.g., Meesho) insourcing logistics (Valmo) could pressure volumes, as seen historically with Ecom Express
  • Intense competition from Delhivery, XpressBees, Ecom Express (now under Delhivery) and Ekart
  • Valuation remains demanding (84x P/E) — any growth or margin disappointment risks sharp de-rating
  • Sensitive to broader FII flow direction and Indian equity market sentiment given new-age tech stock classification
Cauvery Assist — Investor Q&A

Quick answers to the questions investors most commonly ask about this result. Tap a question to expand.

Why did profit grow so much faster than revenue?+
Revenue grew 65% YoY, but fixed network and technology costs did not grow proportionately, so incremental revenue dropped through to EBITDA and PAT at a much higher rate. This is classic operating leverage — margins expanded from 3.1% to 6.8% EBITDA margin in one year.
Is the cash flow picture as strong as the profit picture?+
Broadly yes. FY26 operating cash flow of ₹350 Cr comfortably covered ₹185 Cr of network capex, leaving free cash flow of roughly ₹165 Cr — a sharp improvement from the ₹49 Cr operating cash flow seen in FY25. Working capital is also a quiet strength: receivable days (~50–58) and payable days (~36–39) sit close enough together that net working capital stays roughly neutral, which is unusual for a logistics business at this scale.
Is the stock expensive at the current price?+
On trailing P/E (84x) and P/B (8.13x), yes — it is priced for continued high growth, not for today's earnings. Whether that is "expensive" depends on whether the 38–40% FY27 growth guidance and margin expansion trajectory hold. Some analysts argue the current EV/EBITDA discount to Delhivery is unjustified given Shadowfax's superior capital efficiency; others flag the multiple as a risk if growth slows.
What is Prime Large and why does it matter?+
Prime Large is Shadowfax's vertical for heavy/volumetric shipments above 15kg. It has an annual recurring revenue run-rate of about ₹75 Cr, growing 170% YoY, and reached its full-year pincode coverage target in a single quarter. It matters because it diversifies Shadowfax beyond small-parcel last-mile delivery into a segment with potentially better unit economics.
What is the single biggest risk to this thesis?+
Customer concentration. A large share of volume comes from a handful of e-commerce and quick-commerce platforms. If any major client shifts to insourced logistics (as Meesho has partly done with Valmo, and as happened to Ecom Express), it could hit volumes and revenue quickly.
Why did the CPO's resignation not hurt the stock more?+
The results themselves were strong enough that the market appears to have treated the leadership change as a secondary, watch-list item rather than a thesis-breaking event on the day. It remains worth monitoring for any change in product roadmap execution in subsequent quarters.
