Two Ends of the Same Value Chain

Coal India (CIL)

Maharatna CPSE, world's largest coal mining company, ~80% of India's domestic coal output. Sells coal to power, steel, cement and other sectors via Fuel Supply Agreements (FSAs) and e-auctions. NSE/BSE: COALINDIA.

NTPC Limited

Maharatna CPSE, India's largest integrated power utility — thermal (coal-led), hydro, nuclear (JV) and renewables (via listed subsidiary NTPC Green Energy). One of Coal India's single largest coal-consuming customers. NSE/BSE: NTPC.

The Nexus

NTPC's coal-fired fleet ran at a sector-beating 76.71% PLF in Q1 FY27 — a demand pull-through for CIL even as CIL's own e-auction premiums widened. But NTPC is also building its own captive coal-mining arm (NTPC Mining Ltd) — a long-term diversification away from pure CIL dependence. See the Coal-to-Power Nexus tab.

CB Research Quick Take

Coal India's Q1 FY27 was a mixed quarter — revenue grew a healthy 7.8% YoY on stronger offtake, but production fell 7% YoY, EBITDA declined 4.1% YoY on cost inflation, and consolidated PAT was effectively flat (+0.6%), down 18.3% sequentially. The bright spots were a sharp 22% cut in pit-head inventory and e-auction premiums widening to 47%, alongside a ₹5.50/share interim dividend. NTPC, by contrast, posted a genuine beat — standalone PAT up 12% and consolidated PAT up 13%, powered by capacity additions, a 76.71% coal PLF (vs 70.32% national average) and margin expansion, with its renewable arm NTPC Green Energy growing profit 38% YoY. The stock market's reaction diverged sharply: Coal India fell over 3% intraday post-results while NTPC's stronger print reinforces its "regulated earnings + growth" thesis.

Business Model & Key Developments

Core Business

Coal mining and sale to power, steel, cement and other core-sector consumers via long-term Fuel Supply Agreements (FSAs) and e-auctions, through subsidiaries such as SECL, NCL, MCL, ECL, BCCL, CCL and WCL.

Volume vs Output Gap

Production fell 7% YoY to 169.63 MT while offtake rose 4% YoY to 197.86 MT — CIL sold more coal than it mined this quarter, drawing down pit-head inventory rather than relying on fresh output.

E-Auction Strength

E-auction volumes jumped 25% YoY with premiums widening to 47% over notified prices — a positive realization lever that partly offsets cost inflation, and came in well ahead of pre-results brokerage estimates of ~35%.

Diversification — Solar & Coal Gasification

Commissioned a 100 MW solar plant at Bhadramali, Gujarat (first-ever energy sales revenue, ₹5.68 Cr) and a further 200 MW at Khavda, Gujarat (8 July 2026) — early steps into renewable energy alongside its core coal business.

Leadership & Governance

Chairman-cum-Managing Director

B Sairam took charge as CMD effective 15 December 2025, succeeding P M Prasad (superannuated 31 October 2025). He was earlier CMD of subsidiary Northern Coalfields Ltd (NCL). The Board subsequently also appointed him as CIL's first-ever CEO, alongside his CMD role.

Board Approval

Q1 FY27 results and the interim dividend of ₹5.50/share were approved by the Board of Directors on 27 July 2026.

Subsidiary Watch

Mahanadi Coalfields Ltd (subsidiary) is seeking banker proposals for a ~₹10,000 Cr IPO — a potential value-unlocking event for the CIL group over the coming quarters.

CB Research View — Coal India

  • Strength: Revenue growth (+7.8%) was driven by genuine volume (offtake +4%) and pricing (e-auction premium +47%) tailwinds even as own production fell — a sign of healthy end-demand and inventory monetization.
  • Watch: EBITDA fell 4.1% YoY and net profit was effectively flat — cost inflation (contractual/outsourcing expenses, diesel prices) is outpacing revenue growth. The pending non-executive wage revision (estimated ~₹3,400 Cr impact through December 2026) is a further margin overhang not yet fully reflected.
  • Balance sheet: CIL remains a virtually debt-light, high-dividend PSU — the ₹5.50/share interim dividend continues its reputation as one of India's most consistent dividend payers (~6% trailing yield cited by brokerages pre-results).
  • Read-through: Falling own production (-7% YoY) while offtake and e-auction volumes rose means CIL leaned on drawing down inventory this quarter — sustainability of offtake growth into H2 FY27 without a production recovery is the key monitorable flagged by brokerages.

Business Model & Segments

Thermal Generation (Core)

Coal and gas-based generation sold largely under regulated cost-plus tariffs to state discoms via long-term PPAs — providing revenue visibility (~15.5% regulated RoE on equity) that anchors the earnings base.

Hydro & Nuclear

Hydro capacity plus a nuclear JV footprint, adding diversification beyond thermal — part of a broader push to balance the generation mix as India's power demand grows.

NTPC Green Energy (Listed Subsidiary)

The group's dedicated renewable energy vehicle — Q1 FY27 consolidated PAT of ₹305 Cr (+38% YoY) on revenue of ₹1,107 Cr (+63% YoY), EBITDA margin 89.3%. Group installed RE capacity reached 10,671 MW after fresh Gujarat/Rajasthan commissionings, targeting 60 GW renewable capacity by FY32.

Captive Coal Mining (NTPC Mining Ltd)

NML completed acquisition of the remaining coal mine business effective 1 April 2026 for a total consideration of ₹6,339.18 Cr (₹8,651.86 Cr balance payable by 30 September 2026) — a strategic move to secure captive fuel supply and reduce reliance on external coal linkages.

Leadership & Governance

Chairman & Managing Director

Gurdeep Singh was re-employed as CMD on a one-year contract basis by the Ministry of Power, effective 1 August 2025 until 31 July 2026 (or until a regular incumbent takes charge, whichever is earlier) — a tenure that concludes almost immediately after this results announcement, making CMD succession a near-term governance watchpoint.

Board Approval

Q1 FY27 results were approved by the Board of Directors on 24 July 2026.

Capacity Expansion Approval

The Board approved a ₹20,456.70 Cr investment for the Lara Super Thermal Power Project Stage-III (2×800 MW) in Chhattisgarh on 11 July 2026, underscoring continued brownfield thermal capacity addition alongside the renewables push.

CB Research View — NTPC

  • Strength: A genuine earnings beat — standalone PAT (+12%) and EBITDA (+22.6%) both topped street estimates, driven by capacity additions and a sector-leading 76.71% coal PLF. Consolidated PAT growth (+13%) was further boosted by a 62% jump in group companies' profit contribution.
  • Watch: Gurdeep Singh's CMD contract runs only to 31 July 2026 — leadership continuity/succession at the very top is an immediate governance item to track into Q2 FY27.
  • Growth engine: NTPC Green Energy's 38% PAT growth and 89.3% EBITDA margin show the renewable arm scaling profitably; combined with the ₹62,200 Cr FY27-32 renewable capex plan and 60 GW FY32 target, this remains the key re-rating lever alongside the stable thermal base.
  • Structural driver: The NTPC Mining Ltd captive coal acquisition (₹6,339 Cr, plus ₹8,652 Cr payable by Sep-26) is a long-term hedge against fuel-supply risk from external sources including Coal India — see the Coal-to-Power Nexus tab.

CB Research Comparative Take

On pure earnings momentum, NTPC clearly outperformed Coal India this quarter — both PAT growth (12–13% vs 0.6%) and EBITDA growth (+22.6% vs −4.1%) favour NTPC decisively. Coal India's headline revenue growth (7.8%) was comparable to NTPC's consolidated revenue growth, but cost inflation ate into CIL's profitability while NTPC converted its volume and efficiency gains straight into margin expansion. Both remain regulated, dividend-paying Maharatna CPSEs at opposite ends of India's coal-to-power value chain — CIL's e-auction pricing strength and NTPC's record PLF are, in fact, two sides of the same strong power-demand story, even though it showed up very differently in each company's P&L this quarter.

Coal-to-Power Nexus — Reading CIL and NTPC Together

Coal India is the fuel supplier; NTPC is one of its largest customers. Reading their Q1 FY27 results side by side reveals the health of India's core power-generation value chain.

How the Two Companies Connect

Demand Pull-Through Positive for Both

NTPC's coal-fired fleet ran at a 76.71% PLF in Q1 FY27, well above the 70.32% national average — meaning strong, above-average draw on coal supply from producers including CIL. CIL's own power-sector supplies rose in tandem, supporting the 4% YoY offtake growth reported this quarter.

E-Auction Pricing vs Regulated Tariffs Structural

CIL's e-auction premiums widening to 47% reflects tight coal availability at the margin — a positive for CIL's realizations, but a potential future cost pass-through consideration for power producers buying outside long-term FSA linkage. NTPC's regulated cost-plus tariff structure, however, insulates it from most spot coal price volatility relative to merchant power generators.

NTPC's Captive Coal Mining Push Watch

NTPC Mining Ltd's acquisition of the remaining coal mine business (₹6,339 Cr, effective 1 April 2026) is a structural long-term diversification away from full reliance on CIL and other external suppliers. While immaterial to CIL's volumes today, sustained expansion of NTPC's captive mining capacity is a monitorable for CIL's largest-customer concentration over the coming years.

Shared Cost Pressures Watch

CIL flagged rising diesel prices (~8.6% hike in May 2026) and an upcoming non-executive wage revision (~₹3,400 Cr impact through December 2026) as margin headwinds. Any resulting increase in CIL's e-auction or FSA pricing would flow through, with a lag, into power producers' fuel costs — though NTPC's regulated tariff mechanism allows pass-through of fuel cost variations to discoms within CERC norms.

Capacity & Expansion Roadmap

Where each company stands today, and where it is headed — production/capacity targets, ongoing capex, and diversification plans.

Coal India — Production & Diversification Roadmap

FY27 Production Target

Company-guided FY27 production target is 815 MT, with dispatch capacity of up to 850 MT if rail-rake logistics cooperate. CIL has explicitly clarified that "1 billion tonnes" is not its FY27 production target, despite that figure circulating widely — see note below.

Inventory Normalization

Pit-head stock stood above 130 MT at the start of FY27; CIL is targeting a reduction to around 95–50 MT over the year (a ~27%+ cut), shifting strategy from stockpiling toward a "produce-and-sell" model with more direct dispatch.

₹1 Lakh Crore Capex Plan (through FY31)

Covers mining, coal evacuation/first-mile connectivity (FMC) and power projects. FMC network is planned to scale to 94 projects by FY30 with a combined handling capacity of ~995 MT.

Renewable Diversification

Targeting 3,000 MW of solar capacity by FY28 to become a net-zero entity (~675 MW expected by FY26-end, up from ~247 MW as of December 2025). A broader group ambition of ~9.5 GW renewable capacity by FY29-30 is also cited, built via subsidiary/JV routes including 1,000 MW joint ventures each with NTPC and NLC India, and a dedicated SPV, CIL Navikarniya Urja Ltd.

NTPC Group — Capacity Expansion Roadmap

Current Scale

NTPC Group crossed 90 GW of installed power generation capacity in May 2026 (following commissioning of the Patratu/PVUNL 800 MW unit), with roughly 32 GW under construction across thermal, hydro/pumped-storage and renewables.

FY32 Target: 149 GW

NTPC has raised its total capacity target for FY32 to 149 GW, up from an earlier 130 GW goal — split broadly across ~30 GW of incremental thermal, 60 GW of renewables, plus hydro, nuclear and other emerging businesses.

Thermal Buildout

Targeting 30,000 MW of new coal-based capacity by FY32 (raised from 26,000 MW), executed entirely via brownfield expansion of existing sites. The newly approved Lara Super Thermal Power Project Stage-III (2×800 MW, ₹20,456.70 Cr, approved 11 July 2026) is the latest instalment.

Renewable Push (NTPC Green Energy)

Targeting 60 GW of renewable capacity by FY32, backed by a ₹62,200 Cr FY27-32 capex plan and a 30 GW project pipeline; the group added ~5,488 MW of renewable capacity in FY26 alone (solar, wind, pumped storage) and over 23 GW is already contracted/awarded.

Nuclear & Emerging Businesses

A long-dated target of 30 GW nuclear capacity by FY47, alongside early-stage moves into green hydrogen, battery energy storage systems, e-mobility and waste-to-energy — diversification beyond the traditional thermal/hydro base.

CB Research Read — Capacity Plans Compared

The two companies' roadmaps point in almost opposite directions on volume. Coal India's FY27 production guidance (815 MT) is actually lower than its FY26 target (868 MT) — a deliberate destocking/normalization move rather than a growth story, even as it pursues a large ₹1 lakh crore capex programme aimed more at evacuation efficiency (first-mile connectivity) and diversification (solar, coal gasification) than at raw output growth. NTPC, by contrast, is in an unambiguous capacity expansion phase — targeting a jump from ~90 GW to 149 GW by FY32, with both thermal (+30 GW) and renewables (60 GW via NTPC Green Energy) growing simultaneously. This asymmetry is worth watching: if NTPC's own captive coal mining (NTPC Mining Ltd) scales up meaningfully while CIL's production growth stays deliberately modest, the demand-supply balance in the coal-to-power nexus could shift gradually over the FY27-32 window.

CB Research Rating Summary

NTPC earns an "A" CB Rating (8.15) on the back of a broad-based beat — revenue, EBITDA and PAT all grew ahead of expectations, led by capacity additions and a sector-leading coal PLF. Coal India earns a "B" (6.05) — its balance-sheet strength and cash generation remain excellent, but the operating quarter itself was genuinely mixed, with EBITDA declining and PAT flat under cost pressure even as revenue grew.
