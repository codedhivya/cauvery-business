Business Highlights (as stated by management/brokerages)

Top-10 Indian renewable IPP with a 7,910 MW diversified portfolio (solar, wind, Wind-Solar Hybrid, and FDRE with BESS) as of 30 June 2026, across 50 projects.

~98% of capacity is backed by long-term PPAs with central/state government-linked off-takers — GUVNL and MSEDCL alone contributed over 86% of FY26 revenue, giving strong visibility but concentrated counterparty exposure.

In-house, end-to-end EPC and O&M model; ranked the second-largest bidder by capacity awarded (Apr 2021–Mar 2026) with a ~97% bid-to-project conversion rate. Over 12,000 acres of land and 300+ wind turbine locations already secured, supporting the future pipeline.

Collection cycle of ~22 days is among the fastest in the sector, reducing working-capital drag despite a large project base.

What Changed

MNRE has confirmed there will be no blanket extension of ALMM List-II beyond 1 June 2026 — every ALMM-mandated project (government-backed, net-metering, open access) must now source solar cells exclusively from List-II-enlisted domestic manufacturers, on top of the pre-existing List-I requirement for finished modules.

A limited, case-by-case relief window has been kept open only until 31 December 2026 for net-metering and open-access projects that can demonstrate an advanced stage of grounding or completed module installation with traceable paperwork — this is relief on timing, not a policy reversal.

The 8th revision (22 Jul 2026) continues MNRE's pattern of periodic capacity additions to the approved list: prior revisions added Premier Energies (+607 MW TOPCon cells, 25.37% efficiency), Mundra Solar PV/Adani (+405 MW), FS India Solar Ventures (+221 MW, cumulative 3,433 MW) and Fujiyama Power Systems (437 MW Bifacial Mono-PERC, 23.41% efficiency, valid to Feb 2030).

Operational & Strategic Highlights

Module production of 3.24 GW in Q1 FY27, up 41.51% YoY, on operational efficiency and scale.

Order book stands at ≈₹61,500 Cr per the Q1 FY27 investor presentation.

10 GW ingot-wafer facility at Unn, Gujarat is progressing on schedule; cell production is expected to commence within the current financial year (FY27) — a key step toward backward integration and ALMM List-II cell self-sufficiency.

Acquired a 55% stake in Associated Power Structures Pvt Ltd, adding power-infrastructure/transmission capability to the group.

New export orders through July 2026: 125 MW HJT modules via Waaree Solar Americas (21 Jul) and an incremental 212 MW order taking a related customer's cumulative order to 562 MW (16–17 Jul).

Stock closed at ₹2,736.20 on NSE on 29 Jul 2026 (+1.56%), with results announced post market hours.

Margin read: the sequential and YoY EBITDA-margin contraction (18.15% vs 22.53% Q1 FY26) reflects continued pricing pressure in solar modules even as volumes and revenue scale up sharply — consistent with the broader Chinese ASP overhang flagged for the sector. The 10 GW Unn cell facility, once operational, is the company's principal lever to defend margins by capturing more of the cell-to-module value chain in-house, which also directly addresses ALMM List-II cell-sourcing requirements covered in the previous tab.

Key Risks & Management / Board

Key Risks — Juniper Green Energy

Customer concentration: GUVNL and MSEDCL alone account for over 86% of FY26 revenue — any payment delay or renegotiation risk at these off-takers has an outsized impact.

Leverage: borrowings grew nearly 5x in two years to ₹12,920.54 Cr (FY26); net debt/equity ~2.75x. IPO proceeds address only a portion of this.

Valuation: priced at ~270x FY26 trailing earnings and ~30x EV/EBITDA at the upper band — a growth/visibility premium that leaves little room for execution slippage.

Flat profitability despite asset growth: PAT has stayed in the ₹36–40 Cr range for three years even as the balance sheet nearly quadrupled — rising finance costs and depreciation are outpacing topline gains.

Regulatory/component-cost exposure via ALMM List-II compliance on new project builds (see ALMM tab).

Key Risks — Waaree Energies

EBITDA margin compression (18.15% vs 22.53% YoY) on Chinese ASP pressure despite strong volume growth.

US tariff exposure given a meaningful export share; execution risk on the 10 GW Unn ingot-wafer/cell ramp-up within FY27.

Sequential moderation from a record Q4 FY26 base — revenue and PAT both declined QoQ even as YoY growth remained strong.
