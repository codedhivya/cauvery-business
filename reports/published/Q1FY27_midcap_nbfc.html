🎓 CB School — NBFC Metrics & Regulatory Framework

What each metric means, why it matters, and the RBI rules these four companies operate under

Part A · Metrics Glossary
💰 Profitability & Earnings
PAT (Profit After Tax)
The bottom-line profit after all expenses, provisions and taxes. Compared YoY (vs same quarter last year) and QoQ (vs previous quarter) — YoY strips out seasonality, QoQ shows near-term momentum. Watch for base-effect distortion: a low year-ago quarter (like Poonawalla's ₹62.6 Cr in Q1 FY26) can make YoY growth look dramatic even when the sequential trend is steadier.
ROA (Return on Assets)
Annualized net profit as a % of average total assets. The single best measure of how efficiently an NBFC's balance sheet generates profit, because it's leverage-neutral — unlike ROE, a highly-levered NBFC can't inflate ROA. Typical healthy NBFC range: 1.5-3.5% depending on business mix (secured/low-yield books run lower, unsecured/gold-loan books run higher).
ROE (Return on Equity)
Annualized net profit as a % of average shareholder equity. Unlike ROA, ROE rises mechanically with leverage — a company can boost ROE simply by borrowing more, without actually improving underlying business quality. Always read ROE alongside D/E ratio to know whether it's earned or leveraged.
🛡️ Asset Quality
GNPA / Gross Stage-3 Assets
The % of the loan book that is 90+ days overdue (the RBI-mandated default definition, uniform for banks and NBFCs since Nov 2021). Under Ind AS, this is called "Stage-3" — loans where credit risk has materialized. Lower is better; below 2% is considered strong for a retail NBFC.
NNPA / Net Stage-3 Assets
GNPA minus provisions already set aside for those bad loans. Shows the real unprotected exposure. The gap between GNPA and NNPA tells you how conservatively a company has provided — a small gap (like M&M Finance's low Net Stage-3 relative to Gross) signals strong providing discipline.
Stage 1 / Stage 2 / Stage 3
Ind AS 109 classifies every loan into three buckets: Stage-1 (performing, no significant credit deterioration), Stage-2 (some deterioration, typically 30-90 days overdue — an early-warning bucket), Stage-3 (90+ days overdue, effectively NPA). Watching Stage-2 trends often flags stress before it shows up in headline GNPA.
Credit Cost / Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR)
Credit cost = provisions made during the period as a % of average AUM — the real-time cost of expected loan losses. PCR = total provisions held ÷ Gross NPA — what % of bad loans are already covered by provisions. Higher PCR means less earnings shock if those loans are eventually written off.
🏦 Capital & Leverage
CRAR / CAR (Capital Adequacy Ratio)
Total capital (Tier-1 + Tier-2) as a % of risk-weighted assets. This is the core regulatory solvency check — it measures how much of a loss the company could absorb before depositors/lenders are at risk. RBI mandates a 15% minimum for most NBFCs (see Part B below). Tier-1 (core equity capital) is the more important sub-component since it's permanent, loss-absorbing capital.
Debt-Equity (D/E) Ratio
Total borrowings ÷ shareholder equity. Shows how much of the balance sheet is funded by debt versus owners' capital. Higher D/E amplifies both ROE and risk — the same leverage that boosts returns in good times magnifies losses in a downturn. There's no universal "good" number; it depends on the NBFC's rating, funding access, and asset quality (M&M Finance's 4.97x is more acceptable than it would be for a lower-rated NBFC, given its AAA rating and deposit-taking status).
📈 Growth, Funding & Liquidity
AUM (Assets Under Management)
The total loan book — both on-balance-sheet loans and loans sold down / securitized but still managed by the company. AUM growth is the primary top-line growth metric for an NBFC, analogous to "revenue growth" for a regular company.
NIM (Net Interest Margin)
(Interest income − interest expense) ÷ average earning assets. The core spread the lender earns on its money. A rising NIM alongside stable asset quality is a strong positive signal; a rising NIM driven purely by higher-risk unsecured lending is a yellow flag.
Cost of Borrowing / Cost-to-Income Ratio
Cost of borrowing = average interest rate paid on all liabilities — falling cost of borrowing (like MAS Financial's 42bps YoY drop) directly expands margins. Cost-to-income ratio = operating expenses ÷ operating income — measures efficiency; a falling ratio (like Poonawalla's move to 44.6%) shows operating leverage kicking in as the book scales.
LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio)
High-quality liquid assets ÷ expected net cash outflows over the next 30 days under stress. A regulatory requirement (100% minimum for NBFC-UL) that shows whether the company can survive a month of severe funding stress without needing fresh borrowing.

📋 Key Regulatory Requirements — All NBFCs
Capital Adequacy
Minimum CRAR of 15% for NBFC-ICC/ICC-ML/ICC-UL (raised progressively from 10% pre-2022 under SBR). No separate Tier-1 sub-limit is mandated for standard ICCs, though Upper Layer NBFCs face additional core capital scrutiny.
NPA / Income Recognition Norms
Uniform 90-day overdue classification for NPA recognition, aligned with banks since November 2021 (previously NBFCs had a more lenient 180-day norm). Ind AS 109 Expected Credit Loss (ECL) provisioning is mandatory for all Ind AS-compliant NBFCs.
Exposure & Concentration Norms
Single/group borrower exposure ceilings apply, tightened for Upper Layer NBFCs. Large Exposure Framework (LEF) style disclosures are mandatory for NBFC-UL entities like M&M Finance.
🏛️ Upper Layer (NBFC-UL) Specific — M&M Finance & L&T Finance
Why They Sit Here
RBI identifies the top ~25-30 systemically important NBFCs annually as Upper Layer based on size, interconnectedness and complexity. Both M&M Finance (~₹1.37 lakh crore AUM) and L&T Finance (~₹1.30 lakh crore AUM) sit firmly in this bracket alongside names like Bajaj Finance and Tata Capital — making this dashboard's peer set two Upper Layer NBFCs, three Middle Layer NBFCs, and HLF (parent Ashok Leyland listed, HLF itself unlisted — layer unconfirmed).
Additional NBFC-UL Requirements
Mandatory listing within 3 years of UL classification; a Board-approved internal capital adequacy assessment process (ICAAP); differential/enhanced provisioning norms; large exposure disclosures; and closer RBI supervisory engagement comparable to a bank.
Deposit-Taking Status
M&M Finance is one of the few large NBFCs permitted to accept public deposits (NBFC-D), subject to a public deposit ceiling linked to CRAR and credit rating, and RBI directions on deposit tenure, interest rate ceilings, and mandatory liquid asset maintenance (SLR-style) against deposits.
🥇 Gold Loan Specific — IIFL Finance
RBI's gold loan directions cap loan-to-value (LTV) at 75% of gold value at origination, mandate standardized gold purity assay and valuation processes, prescribe auction procedures for defaulted gold loans, and require enhanced KYC and end-use monitoring. RBI tightened gold-loan operational norms industry-wide in 2025, following supervisory findings of LTV breaches and valuation gaps across several NBFCs and banks — a live regulatory theme worth tracking each quarter for gold-loan-heavy books like IIFL's.
🏘️ Housing Finance & Co-Lending — MAS Financial, M&M Finance
MAS Rural Housing (MAS Financial's HFC subsidiary) and Mahindra Rural Housing Finance (M&M Finance's subsidiary, under merger evaluation) fall under RBI's Housing Finance Company (HFC) regulations (transferred from NHB to RBI in 2019), which mirror NBFC-ICC norms but add project-finance exposure limits. Co-lending arrangements — used extensively by MAS Financial with banks — follow RBI's November 2020 co-lending model circular, requiring a minimum 20% exposure retention by the NBFC on its own books.

Ranking & Methodology
Q1 · Why does Poonawalla rank #1 when its 391% PAT growth is mostly a base effect?
The CB Score isn't built off headline PAT growth alone — it weights profitability at just 25%, alongside asset quality (25%), capital & leverage (20%), growth quality (20%), and funding efficiency (10%). Even after discounting the base-effect-flattered YoY number, Poonawalla still leads on the metrics that are harder to manufacture: GNPA fell both YoY and QoQ even as AUM grew 84% YoY and 11% QoQ, ROA rose 130bps YoY, and Stage-1 assets are at 97.6%. Growing fast without the book getting dirtier is the signal, not the PAT percentage itself.
Q2 · Why does M&M Finance rank last despite being the largest and having the strongest turnaround story?
Scale and turnaround momentum aren't the same as current relative standing on NBFC-specific quality metrics. M&M Finance has the highest Gross Stage-3 (3.5%) and highest leverage (D/E 4.97x) of the four peers at this point in time — even though both are structurally more acceptable given its AAA rating and deposit-taking status, and even though the trend is improving (8-year asset-quality low). The ranking reflects where each company stands today on the five weighted metrics, not the size of its franchise or the direction of travel alone. On a pure turnaround-momentum lens, M&M Finance's story is arguably the most dramatic of the four.
Company-Specific
Q3 · How concentrated is IIFL Finance's earnings in gold loans, and is that a risk?
Gold loans are roughly 51% of IIFL's ₹1.15 lakh crore consolidated AUM, growing 114% YoY — the single largest driver of this quarter's results. It's a double-edged point: gold loans are low-credit-risk (fully collateralized, short tenor, easy to recover), which is exactly why IIFL's capital ratios and asset quality look strong. But it also means IIFL's earnings are increasingly a function of one product and India's gold-loan regulatory environment (LTV norms, RBI guidelines) rather than a genuinely diversified book. A future RBI tightening on gold-loan LTVs, or a sharp gold price correction, would disproportionately affect IIFL versus the other three.
Q4 · MAS Financial hasn't reported Q1 FY27 yet — how reliable is its placement in this ranking?
Treat MAS Financial's #3 ranking as provisional. It's built entirely on Q4 FY26 (quarter ended March 2026) data, since the Q1 FY27 earnings call is scheduled for July 30, 2026 — after this dashboard was built. What's unlikely to change much quarter-to-quarter is MAS's structural position: it has consistently run the lowest leverage (D/E ~3.3x) and strongest capital adequacy (CAR ~22.8%) of the group, alongside the slowest AUM growth (19% YoY). Unless Q1 FY27 brings a surprise, that "safest but slowest" profile should hold — but the actual PAT and asset-quality numbers need to be confirmed once declared.
Q5 · Is Ashok Leyland Finance listed? Why does it show FY26 annual figures instead of Q1 FY27?
Two different entities are involved here, and it's worth being precise about which is which. Ashok Leyland Limited — the commercial vehicle manufacturer — is very much a listed company (NSE: ASHOKLEY, BSE: 500477), and it reports quarterly like any listed company. Hinduja Leyland Finance (HLF), the NBFC financing arm often referred to as "Ashok Leyland Finance," is a separate legal entity — a subsidiary of Ashok Leyland — that has never completed its own IPO despite filing a Draft Red Herring Prospectus years ago. So HLF itself remains unlisted, even though its parent is not. In practice, this means HLF's AUM and PAT figures only surface publicly when Ashok Leyland discloses them as part of its own results. Ashok Leyland's Q1 FY27 results are scheduled for August 13, 2026, and should include an HLF update — until then, the FY26 annual figures (disclosed alongside Ashok Leyland's Q4 FY26 results in May 2026) are the latest available.
Advanced
Q6 · If funding costs fall further through FY27 as RBI rate cuts transmit, which of these four benefits most?
The benefit scales with (a) how much of the book reprices with policy rates and (b) how much of the balance sheet is wholesale-funded versus deposit-funded. MAS Financial, having already cut its cost of borrowing 42bps YoY to 9.39% with more debt tie-ups planned, has clear room to compound the benefit given its currently higher relative funding cost versus AAA-rated peers. M&M Finance, as an AAA deposit-taking NBFC, already funds at the cheaper end, so the incremental benefit is smaller in percentage terms but still meaningful given its ₹1.37 lakh crore book size. Poonawalla's cost-to-income improvement (down to 44.6%) suggests it's already capturing some of this benefit through better operating leverage alongside funding cost gains.
Q7 · Between Poonawalla's unsecured book and IIFL's gold-loan concentration, which is the bigger structural risk?
Different risk types entirely. Poonawalla's unsecured exposure (47% of on-book) carries credit risk — if macro conditions worsen, unsecured personal/consumer loans default faster and recover less than secured loans, and this is the segment most sensitive to over-leveraged retail borrowers, a live concern across Indian unsecured lending currently. IIFL's gold-loan concentration carries collateral-value and regulatory risk rather than credit risk — gold loans rarely default meaningfully because the collateral is liquid, but a sharp gold price correction reduces collateral cover, and RBI has tightened gold-loan norms industry-wide before. Poonawalla's risk shows up in credit costs; IIFL's would show up more in regulatory or collateral-value shocks. Both are worth tracking, but they are not directly comparable on the same risk axis.

கே2 · M&M Finance மிகப்பெரியதாகவும் வலுவான மீட்சி கதையாகவும் இருந்தும் ஏன் கடைசி இடத்தில் உள்ளது?
அளவும் மீட்சி வேகமும் தற்போதைய தர அளவீடுகளில் நிலைப்பாட்டிற்கு சமமல்ல. கே3 · IIFL Finance-யின் தங்க கடன் குவிப்பு எவ்வளவு, இது ஒரு அபாயமா?

கே4 · MAS Financial இன்னும் Q1 FY27 அறிவிக்கவில்லை — இதன் தரவரிசை எவ்வளவு நம்பகமானது?

கே6 · RBI வட்டி விகித குறைப்புகள் தொடர்ந்தால், இந்த நான்கில் யாருக்கு அதிக பயன்?
பயன் (அ) புத்தகத்தில் எவ்வளவு கொள்கை விகிதங்களுடன் மறு விலை நிர்ணயம் செய்யப்படுகிறது, (ஆ) இருப்புநிலை எவ்வளவு மொத்த விற்பனை-நிதியளிக்கப்பட்டதா அல்லது வைப்பு-நிதியளிக்கப்பட்டதா என்பதைப் பொறுத்தது. M&M Finance, AAA வைப்பு-எடுக்கும் NBFC ஆக, ஏற்கனவே மலிவான முனையில் நிதியளிக்கிறது, எனவே அதிகரிப்பு பயன் சதவீத அடிப்படையில் சிறியது ஆனால் ₹1.37 லட்சம் கோடி புத்தக அளவைக் கருத்தில் கொண்டு இன்னும் குறிப்பிடத்தக்கது. Poonawalla-யின் செலவு-வருமான விகித மேம்பாடு (44.6% ஆக குறைந்தது) நிதி செலவு ஆதாயங்களுடன் சேர்ந்து சிறந்த செயல்பாட்டு அந்நியச்செலவு மூலம் ஏற்கனவே இதன் சில பயனை பெறுவதைக் காட்டுகிறது.
