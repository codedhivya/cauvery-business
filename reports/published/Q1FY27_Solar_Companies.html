Business Model Differentiation

How each company makes money · Revenue structure · Risk profile · Investor lens
🏭 Manufacturer
Waaree, Premier, EMMVEE, Vikram Solar, Fujiyama
  • Sells physical solar modules/cells to installers, EPC companies, utilities
  • Revenue = Volume (GW) × Average Selling Price (₹/W)
  • Margin risk: Chinese ASP competition, ALMM-II cell-sourcing transition
  • Capital: Heavy — manufacturing plants ₹3,000–10,000+ Cr capex
  • Working capital intensive; 60–90 day receivables
  • USP: Scale + ALMM-I/II compliance + backward integration
  • Investor thesis: Volume growth × margin expansion as cell integration rises
⚡ IPP / Developer
Adani Green, ACME Solar, NTPC Green
  • Builds, owns & operates solar/wind/hybrid power plants + BESS
  • Revenue = Units generated × PPA tariff (25-yr contract)
  • Margin: EBITDA 85–94% (IPP typical) but interest eats profit
  • Capital: Very heavy — ₹4–6 Cr/MW project capex, 70:30 D:E
  • Risk: ISTS waiver taper on new COD, DISCOM payment delays
  • USP: PPA annuity visibility, BESS/FDRE premium tariffs
  • Investor thesis: Capacity MW × PLF × tariff − debt cost − ISTS charge
🔧 EPC Contractor
Sterling & Wilson, Waaree Renewable Tech.
  • Builds solar/evacuation-infra projects for IPPs, utilities, C&I for a fee
  • Revenue = Order book execution × billing milestones
  • Margin: Thin, ~5–10% net (S&W) to 18%+ (WRTL with captive modules)
  • Capital: Working capital intensive (negative WC possible)
  • Risk: commodity price spikes, liquidated damages, order delays
  • USP: Track record GW executed, O&M recurring income, T&D expansion
  • Investor thesis: Order inflow → revenue recognition → margin improvement
🏢 C&I / B2C
CleanMax, KPI Green, Fujiyama (B2C)
  • OPEX model: Builds + owns + operates, customer pays per unit consumed
  • Revenue = Long-term energy supply agreements (ESA) + O&M fees
  • Margin: Better than EPC (~20–30% EBITDA at operating level)
  • Capital: Asset ownership but shared/subsidized by customer PPAs
  • Risk: Customer churn, credit risk of corporate off-takers
  • USP: Corporate RE target fulfillment + RPO compliance
  • Investor thesis: Contracted capacity × tariff × customer stickiness

🔌 ISTS Charges Waiver — The "Evacuation Concession" Phase-Out
Commissioning (COD) windowISTS Waiver
On or before 30 Jun 2025100% waived
1 Jul 2025 – 30 Jun 202675% waived
1 Jul 2026 – 30 Jun 202750% waived
1 Jul 2027 – 30 Jun 202825% waived
After 30 Jun 20280% — full ISTS charges apply
Hydro-PSP + co-located BESS100% waived till 30 Jun 2028 (protected)
"Evacuation concession" = waiver of Inter-State Transmission System (ISTS) charges — the fee for wheeling power from a remote solar/wind site to the grid. It has been the single biggest reason India could build utility-scale RE in low-irradiation-cost but transmission-poor states (Rajasthan, Gujarat) at competitive tariffs. CERC Regulation 13(2) allows up to two 6-month force-majeure extensions (max 1 year) to preserve waiver eligibility for delayed projects — effectively pushing the true cutoff for 100% waiver to ~30 Jun 2026 for some projects.
Est. tariff impact: +₹0.40–0.50/kWh ~17% hike vs ₹2.40–2.50/kWh bids

What to Watch Next
  • Whether MNRE grants a further ALMM List-II extension beyond 31 Dec 2026 for net-metering/Open Access projects, or holds the line into 2027.
  • List-II capacity growth pace — cumulative enlisted domestic cell capacity needs to keep outrunning List-I module demand for the mandate to avoid bottlenecking supply.
  • Whether NSEFI/industry lobbying secures a milestone-based ISTS waiver extension (e.g., grandfathering projects that had grid-connectivity applications in before mid-2023 but were delayed by discom/CTU processes beyond their control) — CERC/courts review of the extension mechanism is flagged as pending by legal advisors.
  • New COD data each quarter from Adani Green, ACME Solar and NTPC Green — the ISTS-taper cost will start showing up in blended tariff realisation and DSCR commentary from FY27 onward.
