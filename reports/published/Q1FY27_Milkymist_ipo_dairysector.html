🥛 Q1 FY27 Sector Verdict — CB Research
Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026) was a textbook lean-season quarter for Indian dairy: all four listed companies posted double-digit revenue growth (11–19%) driven by strong summer demand for curd, buttermilk, flavoured milk and ice cream, yet every single one reported a YoY PAT decline (−1% to −38%) as raw milk procurement costs spiked industry-wide (Heritage's procurement price rose 7% YoY to ₹46.61/litre; Dodla's rose from ₹37.4 to ₹41.3/litre). Combined revenue across the four listed players rose to ~₹6,571 Cr (+17.6% YoY) while combined PAT fell to ~₹221 Cr (from ~₹266 Cr, −16.9% YoY) — a clean illustration of the structural margin volatility that defines this sector. Value-added products (VAP) were the one bright spot everywhere: Heritage hit a record 44% VAP mix, Dodla posted record VAP revenue of ₹415 Cr, and Parag's New Age portfolio (Pride of Cows + Avvatar) grew 59%. Against this backdrop, Milky Mist Dairy Food's IPO — opening the very next day (11 Aug 2026) — is positioned as a 100% VADP business with no liquid milk drag, though it carries by far the highest leverage in the peer set (D/E ~3.6x) and the richest IPO valuation (~74–85x FY26 P/E).

🥛 CB Research View — Milky Mist IPO (No Recommendation)
Milky Mist is a genuinely differentiated listing: it is the first Indian dairy IPO built entirely around value-added products, with none of the liquid-milk margin drag that weighs on Heritage, Hatsun, Dodla and Parag. The FY24–FY26 growth trajectory — revenue +34% and PAT +176% in the most recent year — is exceptional, and the ~14% EBITDA margin is already the highest in the dairy peer set (listed or unlisted). The counterweight is valuation and leverage: at ~74–85x FY26 earnings and ~3.6x debt/equity, the issue prices in continued hyper-growth and successful deleveraging simultaneously — either slipping would compress the multiple quickly. CB Research does not recommend subscribing to, or avoiding, this IPO. Investors should independently weigh the VADP-only positioning and brand strength against the leverage and pricing before applying, and should read the full RHP.

CB Research Read — Heritage Foods Q1 FY27

Heritage delivered its highest-ever quarterly revenue and a record 44% VAP mix, showing real progress on the premiumisation strategy flagged through FY26. But the headline number masks a sharp profitability hit: EBITDA margin compressed by 188 bps and consolidated PAT fell 38% YoY as procurement prices rose 7% to ₹46.61/litre — among the steepest cost increases in the peer set this quarter. The Heritage Novandie and Peanut Butter & Jelly moves signal continued portfolio-building in premium desserts/yogurt, consistent with the VAP-mix strategy, but near-term margin recovery will hinge on how much of the cost inflation gets passed through in Q2–Q3.

CB Research Read — Hatsun Agro Q1 FY27

Hatsun's ₹3,000+ Cr quarterly milestone is a genuine scale achievement — a ~20% YoY jump reflecting robust summer demand for its ice cream, milk and VAP portfolio. But margin compression was the sharpest among peers on a percentage-point basis: EBITDA margin fell over 300 bps YoY to 11.34%, and PAT was essentially flat despite the strong top line, underscoring how exposed even the highest-VAP-mix player remains to raw milk cost spikes. The ₹1,000 Cr FY27 capex plan and packs-per-day scale-up target show continued aggressive expansion intent; whether debt reduction (flagged as an ongoing priority in prior quarters) keeps pace with this capex will be the key swing factor for FY27 PAT growth.

CB Research Read — Dodla Dairy Q1 FY27

Dodla's Africa business continues to be the standout story — 45.6% revenue growth and 74% EBITDA growth in Uganda/Kenya/Ethiopia operations, with margins there structurally superior to the India business. CB Research Read — Parag Milk Foods Q1 FY27

Parag posted its highest-ever Q1 revenue, and the New Age portfolio (Pride of Cows + Avvatar) continues to be the clear growth engine, up 59% YoY and now 13% of turnover — validating the premiumisation bet. Milk Chain · From Farmer to Consumer · Collection · Processing · Distribution · Cold Chain
🐄 The Indian Dairy Value Chain — Procurement to Consumer
Step 1
🐄 Farm / Farmer
Small-holder farmers (avg 2–5 cows). Morning + evening milking. Cost in Q1 FY27 ran ₹41–47/litre across peers — well above the flush-season lows of ₹33–36/litre.
Step 2
🏠 Village Chilling Centre
Bulk Milk Coolers (BMC) at village level. Milk cooled to 4°C within 2 hours. Quality tested (fat, SNF). Farmer payment linked to fat content.
Step 3
🚛 Cold Chain Transport
Insulated tankers collect from multiple BMCs. Transported to processing plant within 8–12 hours. Temperature maintained at 4–6°C throughout.
Step 4
🏭 Processing Plant
Pasteurisation (72°C/15 sec or UHT). Milky Mist's Perundurai plant is fully automated and dedicated entirely to VADP conversion — no liquid milk packaging line.
Step 5
📦 Packaging
Pouched milk (1L, 500ml, 200ml). Tetra Pak for flavoured milk/UHT. Tubs for ice cream/curd. Cartons for paneer/cheese. Milky Mist's ₹155 Cr IPO capex is aimed squarely at cooler/freezer packaging infrastructure.
Step 6
🏬 Distribution
Direct-to-home (milk routes), Modern Trade (Reliance, DMart), General Trade (local kirana). Institutional (HoReCa, hospitals, catering). Quick commerce (Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart) emerging.
Step 7
👨‍👩‍👧 Consumer
Growing demand for curd, cheese, yogurt, paneer, ice cream. Premium brands (Pride of Cows, Ibaco) and VADP specialists (Milky Mist) are the fastest-growing shelf segment.

Sector Dynamics · Tailwinds · Market Structure · Competitive Landscape · Milky Mist's Entry
🚀 Structural Tailwinds — India Dairy Sector (FY26–FY30)
1. India is the World's Largest Milk Producer — the largest producer globally, giving Indian processors unparalleled raw material access, though lean-season availability swings (as seen in Q1 FY27) remain a recurring margin risk.

2. Low Per-Capita VAP Consumption — India's per-capita cheese and yogurt consumption remains a fraction of Western markets, implying a long runway for value-added product growth — the exact thesis underpinning Milky Mist's 100% VADP model.

3. Organised Sector Gaining Share — organised players are growing faster than unorganised loose-milk trade, driven by FSSAI enforcement, cold chain build-out, and urban preference for quality-assured, branded products.

4. Rising Disposable Incomes + Urbanisation — a growing middle class is upgrading from loose milk to pouched milk, then to VADP — the same upgrade curve every one of the five companies in this dashboard is riding.

5. Protein Awareness Driving Premium Products — fitness-culture-driven demand for whey protein, Greek yogurt and high-protein milk benefits Parag's Avvatar, Hatsun's Ibaco, and premium milk segments.

6. Quick Commerce Expanding Dairy Reach — 10-minute delivery platforms are unlocking new consumer cohorts and increasing impulse purchase of ice cream, curd and specialty dairy.

7. Milky Mist's IPO Is Itself a Sector Signal — a well-capitalised, VADP-only entrant choosing to list in 2026 reflects institutional confidence (Temasek's pre-IPO placement) that India's branded VADP category has matured enough to support a large-cap-scale pure-play listing.

8. Export Opportunity — India is a competitive exporter of SMP, casein and ghee; Milky Mist itself already exports to 15+ countries (~4% of revenue), and Dodla's East Africa business proves Indian dairy brands can scale abroad.

9. Technology in Procurement — digital farmer payments, automated chilling and AI-based milk testing are reducing procurement costs and improving quality across the organised sector.

10. Demographic Dividend — India's large, young, growing population sustains multi-decade underlying consumption growth for dairy, independent of any single company's execution.

CB Assist · Common Questions on Milky Mist IPO & Q1 FY27 Sector Results
Q: What makes Milky Mist different from Heritage, Hatsun, Dodla and Parag?

Milky Mist derives 100% of its revenue from value-added dairy products — paneer, cheese, curd, ghee, ice cream and more — with zero liquid milk sales. Every listed peer still sells 39–65% of revenue as liquid milk, which is a lower-margin, more commoditised category. This is the single biggest structural difference and the main reason Milky Mist's EBITDA margin (~13.9% FY26) is meaningfully higher than any listed peer's latest quarterly margin (4.6–11.3% in Q1 FY27).

Q: Why did all four listed dairy companies report falling profit despite rising revenue in Q1 FY27?

Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun) is a lean-season quarter with lower milk availability and higher procurement costs across the industry. Heritage's procurement price rose 7% YoY, Dodla's rose from ₹37.4 to ₹41.3/litre, and Parag's overall procurement cost rose 13% YoY — none of the four fully passed these costs through to consumer prices in the quarter, compressing EBITDA margins by 188–326 basis points even as revenue grew 11–19%.

Q: Is Milky Mist's IPO valuation expensive compared to listed peers?

At the upper price band, Milky Mist's post-issue P/E works out to roughly 74–85x FY26 earnings, which is materially richer than the typical range across listed dairy peers. This reflects the market pricing in continued high growth and the VADP-margin advantage — but also means the stock has limited room for earnings disappointment once listed, especially given its leverage (D/E ~3.6x, the highest in the peer group).

Q: Which listed company handled Q1 FY27 lean-season pressure best?

On margins, Hatsun retained the highest absolute EBITDA margin (11.34%) despite the sharpest percentage-point compression. On PAT resilience, Hatsun's PAT was nearly flat (−1.1%) versus double-digit declines at Heritage (−38.4%), Dodla (−35.4%) and Parag (−19.9%). Parag was the only peer to grow EBITDA in absolute terms (+6% YoY), even though PAT still fell due to the tax impact the company cited.
