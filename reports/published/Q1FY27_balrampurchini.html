THE SUGAR MILL AS A ZERO-WASTE FACTORY
One tonne of sugarcane, four industries downstream

A sugar mill is one of the cleanest examples of circular economy thinking in Indian manufacturing. Roughly a third of the cane by weight leaves the mill as sugar; almost everything else — fibre, molasses, mud, wastewater — is a feedstock for another industry rather than a disposal problem. Balrampur's model layers ethanol, power and now bioplastics on top of this base, and is adding a lactogypsum unit to close the loop on its newest waste stream too.

Bagasse
~28-32% of crushed cane by weight
  • Burned in mill boilers to generate steam & power — BCML's 288.47 MW cogen fleet runs almost entirely on this
  • Sold to paper & pulp industry as a wood-fibre substitute, reducing deforestation pressure
  • Increasingly explored as a lignocellulosic feedstock for bioplastics and packaging board
Molasses
~4-4.5% of crushed cane by weight
  • Primary ethanol feedstock via C-heavy and B-heavy routes — feeds directly into the EBP/E20 blending programme
  • Also used for potable alcohol, industrial chemicals (acetic acid, etc.) and cattle/poultry feed additives
  • The single biggest link between a sugar mill's balance sheet and national fuel policy
Press mud (filter cake)
~3-3.5% of crushed cane by weight
  • Rich in organic matter and nutrients — composted into bio-manure sold back to cane farmers, closing the agronomic loop
  • Emerging use as feedstock for compressed bio-gas (CBG) plants under India's SATAT scheme
  • Reduces synthetic fertiliser dependence in the cane-growing catchment
Spent wash / vinasse
Distillery effluent, high organic load
  • Anaerobically digested for biogas, which can fuel boilers or generate captive power
  • Concentrated and used in fertigation (irrigation + fertilisation) on cane fields
  • Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) technology — which BCML states all its distilleries run on — treats this so no liquid effluent leaves the plant
BALRAMPUR'S OWN CIRCULARITY: SUGAR → ETHANOL → PLA → LACTOGYPSUM

The PLA plant is itself a second-order circular economy story, not just a diversification bet. PLA (polylactic acid) is made by fermenting sugar/dextrose into lactic acid and then polymerising it — so BCML's existing cane-juice and B-heavy molasses infrastructure feeds straight into it. The polymerisation process itself throws off gypsum as a by-product; rather than landfilling it, BCML has committed ~₹160 Cr to a dedicated lactogypsum processing plant, most likely for reuse in cement, plaster, or soil-conditioning applications — turning a bioplastics-plant waste stream into yet another saleable output.

Why this matters for investors: every stage of this chain (bagasse→power, molasses→ethanol, sugar syrup→PLA, PLA waste→lactogypsum) converts what used to be a disposal cost into a revenue line. It is also why BCML frames PLA as building "an ecosystem for the sugar industry," not a one-off product launch.
ETHANOL POLICY: FROM E20 TO E100 AND FLEX-FUEL VEHICLES
India's ethanol blending timeline
2014
Ethanol Blending Programme (EBP) blending stood at ~1.5%. National Policy on Biofuels (2018) later set an E20-by-2030 target.
2021-22
Target advanced by 5 years, to E20-by-2025, after strong OMC procurement growth from cane and grain feedstocks.
Mar 2025
India reaches nationwide 20% ethanol blending (E20) in petrol — five years ahead of the original schedule.
Apr 2026
MoRTH issues draft notification recognising Flex-Fuel Vehicles (FFVs) able to run on any blend from E20 up to E100.
Jun 2026
E100 fuel and FFV regulations formally approved; excise duty on 22-30% ethanol blends aligned with E20. Maruti Suzuki launches the Wagon R Flex Fuel — India's first commercial FFV.
Dec 2026 →
Roadmap targets ~500 flex-fuel-ready outlets (Delhi-NCR, Mumbai-Pune-Nagpur corridor), scaling to ~5,000 outlets nationally by end-2027.
E20
Petrol blended with 20% ethanol by volume. Achieved nationwide in March 2025. This is the current standard fuel at Indian pumps and the baseline BCML's existing distillery capacity is built to supply.
E100 & Flex-Fuel Vehicles
E100 is near-pure ethanol fuel. FFVs use an engine control unit that auto-detects ethanol content and adjusts combustion parameters, so the same car can run on anything from E20 to E100. Legally recognised in India from June 2026.
The trade-off to know
Ethanol has a lower calorific value than petrol, so mileage typically falls as blend percentage rises — industry estimates put E100 mileage over 30% lower than pure petrol. Existing E20-only vehicles cannot safely run on E85/E100 without engine and fuel-system changes.
Why this matters for BCML: every step up the blending curve (E20 → E85 → E100) expands the addressable market for the ethanol BCML already produces from molasses and cane juice — but only once (a) OMC procurement prices are revised to reflect current cane costs, and (b) FFV vehicle penetration and E100 retail infrastructure actually scale beyond the initial NCR/Mumbai-Pune-Nagpur pilot corridors.
REGULATORY ISSUES SPECIFIC TO THE CIRCULAR ECONOMY / ETHANOL CHAIN
⚖️
Policy Tailwinds
Central Government
SUP BAN
Single-Use Plastics Ban (eff. Jul 2022)
Bans identified single-use plastic items nationally, creating structural demand for compostable substitutes like PLA — direct tailwind for BCML's Kumbhi plant.
BioE3
BioE3 Policy (Aug 2024)
Government's biotechnology-for-economy-environment-employment framework explicitly promotes bio-manufacturing — BCML frames its PLA plant as aligned with this policy.
EXCISE
Excise Parity for Higher Ethanol Blends
Centre exempted 22-30% ethanol-petrol blends from additional central excise duty, putting them on par with E20 — a step toward making E27/E30 commercially viable ahead of full E85/E100 rollout.
⚠️
Unresolved Frictions
Where policy lags the ambition
PRICING
Cane-Feedstock Ethanol Prices Frozen
Juice/B-heavy route ethanol procurement prices have not moved for three straight years despite UP SAP hikes — the single biggest unresolved grievance for BCML and peers heading into ESY 2026-27.
COMPOSTING
No Dedicated PLA Composting Infrastructure
PLA is industrially compostable, not home-compostable — India currently lacks widespread industrial composting facilities, so the environmental benefit depends on waste-management infrastructure catching up with production capacity.
INFRA
E100 Retail Rollout Still Pilot-Stage
Only ~500 flex-fuel-ready outlets targeted by Dec 2026, concentrated in NCR and the Mumbai-Pune-Nagpur corridor — nowhere close to nationwide reach; UP (BCML's home market) is not yet in the first rollout phase.

REGULATORY LANDSCAPE — BALRAMPUR CHINI & THE UP SUGAR SECTOR
🏛️
Central Government
DFPD · CCEA · Ministry of Petroleum · MoRTH
CANE PRICE
Fair & Remunerative Price (FRP)
CCEA-set minimum cane price nationally. FRP revised to ₹355/qtl for SS 2025-26 and ₹365/qtl for SS 2026-27. UP's SAP sits well above this floor and is the price that actually binds for BCML.
SUGAR MSP
Minimum Selling Price of Sugar
Ex-mill floor price, unchanged at ₹31/kg since Feb 2019 despite repeated cane price hikes — the central sector-wide grievance, BCML included.
ETHANOL
Ethanol Blending Programme & Procurement Pricing
CCEA fixes annual OMC procurement prices by feedstock route. Cane-based (B-heavy/juice) rates have been static for three years even as E20 was achieved and E100/FFV policy accelerated — the key BCML watch-item for ESY 2026-27 (announced ~Oct-Nov 2026).
EXPORTS
Sugar Export Ban (eff. 13 May 2026)
After initially allowing a 1.58 MMT export quota for SS 2025-26, the Centre banned further exports till 30 September 2026 to protect domestic stock levels — only ~0.7 MMT of the original quota was actually shipped.
BIOPLASTICS
SUP Ban + BioE3 Policy
Structural demand driver for BCML's PLA plant (see Circular Economy tab) — no direct PLA price support scheme exists yet, but MNRE/MoPNG are understood to be evaluating incentive structures.
🏗️
Uttar Pradesh State Government
Cane Commissioner · UPERC
UP — SAP
State Advised Price — the binding cost driver
SS 2025-26 SAP: ₹400/qtl (+8% YoY) for general variety — legally binding, above FRP, and the primary reason BCML's Q4 FY26 EBITDA margin fell YoY despite higher volumes.
UP — POWER
UPERC Co-generation Tariff
Sets the price at which BCML's saleable 175.7 MW of cogen capacity is purchased by the UP grid — periodic revisions add incremental non-sugar revenue.
UP — MOLASSES
Molasses Storage & Excise Rules
UP mandates minimum molasses storage norms and levies state excise; non-compliance across the sector has drawn penalties at peer mills — a compliance area BCML's 5 distillery units must continue to manage carefully.
CANE AREA
Minimum Distance / Cane Reservation Rules
A ~15 km minimum distance between mills for cane-procurement zones is enforced by the UP Cane Commissioner — gives BCML captive cane supply around its 10 mills but ties recovery/yield to hyper-local weather in each catchment.
FRP = Fair & Remunerative Price (Central) · SAP = State Advised Price (UP) · EBP = Ethanol Blending Programme · ESY = Ethanol Supply Year (Nov-Oct). RISKS & OUTLOOK — GOING INTO Q1 FY27 AND BEYOND
⚠️ Key risks
Cane-cost ratchet: UP SAP +8% to ₹400/qtl with no matching ethanol price or sugar MSP relief — every rupee of cane cost increase compresses margin until policy responds.
PLA execution risk: a ~₹3,080 Cr capex project, largely debt-funded, commissioning for the first time at industrial scale in India — construction, ramp-up, and off-take risk are all live until Q3 FY27 and beyond.
Ethanol price stagnation: three years of flat cane-feedstock procurement pricing despite rising cane costs — a continued freeze would keep squeezing the distillery segment that has been the sector's profit engine since 2021.
Export/domestic-stock policy swings: the May 2026 export ban shows how quickly government intervention can change the realisable-price picture for sugar within a season.
PLA demand-side dependency: revenue potential (~₹2,000 Cr) assumes India's compostable-plastics ecosystem — including industrial composting infrastructure — scales in step with production, which is not guaranteed.
Rich valuation: ~29x trailing P/E and ~2.95x book already price in a large chunk of the PLA optionality; disappointment on execution or timeline carries valuation risk, not just earnings risk.
🔭 FY27 outlook
Tight domestic closing stock (~4.3 MMT after the export ban) is supportive of firm sugar realisations through the new season.
Two policy catalysts to track: an ethanol price revision for ESY 2026-27 (expected ~Oct-Nov 2026) and any move on the frozen ₹31/kg sugar MSP — either would re-rate the earnings base.
PLA commissioning (Q3 FY27 guided) is the single largest company-specific catalyst — first revenue recognition would begin changing BCML's earnings mix away from pure sugar-cycle exposure.
National E100/FFV policy momentum (June 2026 onward) is a multi-year tailwind for ethanol demand, though near-term impact on BCML's realisations depends on OMC pricing action, not vehicle-policy headlines alone.
CB stance: results-pending, policy-catalyst-rich. The next hard data point is the Q1 FY27 board meeting (expected mid-August 2026) — track BSE/NSE filings directly for the exact date.

Four streams: bagasse (fibre) fuels power plants and feeds paper mills; molasses feeds ethanol distilleries (and hence the EBP/E20 programme) plus potable-alcohol and cattle-feed industries; press mud is composted into farm bio-manure or fed into bio-CNG plants; and distillery spent wash is digested for biogas or used in field fertigation. What is the difference between E20, E100 and a flex-fuel vehicle?
E20, E100 மற்றும் flex-fuel வாகனத்திற்கு என்ன வித்தியாசம்?
▼
Answer
E20 is petrol with 20% ethanol blended in — India's nationwide standard since March 2025. E100 is fuel that is almost entirely ethanol. A flex-fuel vehicle (FFV) has an engine that auto-detects the ethanol percentage and adjusts itself, so it can run on anything from E20 to E100 in the same tank — India legally recognised E100 fuel and FFVs in June 2026, and Maruti launched the first commercial FFV (Wagon R Flex Fuel) that same month. The trade-off: higher ethanol blends generally mean lower mileage, since ethanol has less energy per litre than petrol.
E20 என்பது 20% எத்தனால் கலந்த பெட்ரோல் — மார்ச் 2025 முதல் இந்தியாவின் தேசிய தரம். E100 என்பது கிட்டத்தட்ட முழுவதும் எத்தனால். Flex-fuel வாகனம் (FFV) தானாகவே எத்தனால் அளவை கண்டறிந்து சரிசெய்யும் என்ஜின் கொண்டது — ஜூன் 2026-ல் இந்தியா இதற்கு சட்டப்பூர்வ அங்கீகாரம் அளித்தது.
