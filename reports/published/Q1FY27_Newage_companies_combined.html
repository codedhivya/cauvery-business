CB Score Methodology

The CB Score for this ranking is built entirely from Q1 FY27 disclosed figures, normalised 0–100 across the eight companies in this cohort on each dimension, then combined with the weights below.

Revenue Growth YoY — 20%

Year-on-year growth in reported revenue/total income for the quarter. Rewards top-line momentum regardless of profitability stage.

PAT Margin — 25%

Net profit (or loss) as a percentage of quarterly revenue — the single best snapshot of current earnings quality, weighted highest since the brief was to rank “based on their earnings.”

Earnings Momentum YoY — 20%

Year-on-year change in PAT/loss — profit growth or loss-narrowing scores positively; profit decline or a swing into loss scores negatively.

Return on Equity — 20%

Latest reported ROE — captures how efficiently each company converts shareholder capital into profit, independent of scale.

Valuation Reasonableness — 15%

Inverse of Price/Book across the cohort (lower P/B scores higher) — a check on whether the market has already priced in the earnings strength.

This ranking is specific to Q1 FY27 and this 8-company cohort; scores will shift each quarter as new results are published. It is an analytical scoring framework for educational comparison, not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security.

At a Glance — Q1 FY27

Eternal's Q1 FY27 was a Blinkit story: quick commerce swelled to ₹15,664 Cr of the group's ₹20,211 Cr revenue, up from just ₹2,400 Cr a year ago, as the company kept opening dark stores aggressively. Consolidated net profit of ₹92 Cr was up 268% YoY but fell 47% sequentially from Q4 FY26, and screener data flags ₹1,417 Cr of other income propping up the reported bottom line — core operating profitability is still a work in progress even as the topline compounds spectacularly.

Business Model

Eternal is the holding company for four consumer-facing businesses built around one delivery fleet and one logistics backbone: Zomato (restaurant food delivery + dining-out discovery), Blinkit (10-15 minute quick commerce grocery/general merchandise), Hyperpure (B2B supply of ingredients and kitchen essentials to restaurants), and District/Going-out (events, dining and live entertainment ticketing spun out of Zomato's dining vertical). Revenue is earned through delivery/platform fees and commissions on food delivery, product margins plus delivery and platform fees on Blinkit orders, wholesale margins on Hyperpure, and ticketing/booking fees on District.

Revenue Streams

  • Commission & delivery fees from restaurant partners and food-delivery customers (Zomato)
  • Product margin + delivery/platform fee on every quick-commerce order (Blinkit)
  • B2B wholesale margin on ingredients and kitchen supplies sold to restaurants (Hyperpure)
  • Ticketing and booking fees on live events, dining and going-out experiences (District)
  • Advertising revenue sold to restaurant and brand partners across all platforms

Competitive Moat

Largest dark-store and delivery-rider network among Indian quick-commerce players, cross-sell between food delivery and grocery on one app, and Hyperpure's B2B supply chain that also feeds Blinkit's private-label push.

Working Capital

Eternal discloses working-capital days at the annual, not quarterly, level; as an asset-light marketplace with negative float from vendor payables it has historically run structurally negative working-capital days, though Blinkit's inventory-led model is pulling the group average up as it scales.

Cash & Funding

Company continues to fund Blinkit dark-store capex from its large IPO/QIP cash pile rather than external debt.

Valuations

At ₹3,09,583 Cr market cap and a trailing P/E north of 700x, Eternal trades almost entirely on a quick-commerce growth narrative rather than current earnings — the market is pricing Blinkit reaching Instamart/Zepto-beating scale and eventual double-digit margins, not today's 2.9% EBITDA margin. Price/Book of ~10x and ROE of 0.38% underline how far current returns lag the valuation.

Peer Context

Trades at a premium P/S to Swiggy given its profitability lead and Blinkit's scale advantage in quick commerce.

Business Model

Swiggy operates a multi-app consumer platform: Swiggy Food Delivery (restaurant discovery and delivery), Instamart (10-30 minute quick commerce grocery and general merchandise), Swiggy Out-of-Home/Dineout (dining and event discovery), Swiggy Genie (pickup-and-drop errands) and a supply-chain/distribution arm supporting dark-store replenishment. Revenue comes from delivery and platform fees charged to customers, commissions from restaurant and merchant partners, product margin on Instamart orders, and advertising sold to sellers across the platform.

Revenue Streams

  • Delivery and platform fees plus restaurant commission (Food Delivery)
  • Product margin + delivery fee on every Instamart quick-commerce order
  • Advertising revenue from restaurants and Instamart sellers
  • Fees from Out-of-Home dining/event bookings and Genie errand deliveries
  • Supply-chain and distribution revenue supporting dark-store operations

Competitive Moat

Established food-delivery brand and rider network reused to bootstrap Instamart's last-mile; dark-store density concentrated in metro clusters where Swiggy already has delivery density.

Business Model

One 97 Communications (Paytm) operates India's largest independent digital payments platform, monetising through payment processing (UPI, cards, wallet), merchant device and subscription fees (Paytm Soundbox, EDC machines), and a growing financial-services distribution business — distributing merchant and consumer loans, insurance, equity broking (Paytm Money) and wealth products on behalf of partner banks/NBFCs rather than lending on its own balance sheet.

Revenue Streams

  • Payment processing fees from merchant and consumer transactions (UPI, cards, wallet)
  • Subscription/rental revenue from merchant payment devices (Soundbox, EDC machines)
  • Loan distribution commissions (merchant and consumer loans) via lending partners
  • Marketing services revenue from merchants and brands on the platform
  • Wealth, broking (Paytm Money) and insurance distribution revenue

Competitive Moat

Large, sticky merchant base built over a decade, deep device penetration (Soundbox/EDC) creating a defensible offline payments moat, and a lending-distribution model (rather than on-balance-sheet lending) that lets it scale credit revenue with limited capital risk.
