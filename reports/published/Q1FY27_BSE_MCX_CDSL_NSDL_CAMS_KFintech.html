Reading Q1 FY27 Correctly — Why Both Trends Matter
BSE's Q1 FY27 print is a genuine YoY and QoQ record — its 14th consecutive quarter of record revenue, aided by an 80% surge in transaction charges and continued Sensex derivatives share gains, even as the April 2026 STT hike created industry-wide volume friction. MCX's Q1 FY27 print is YoY-spectacular (PAT more than doubled) but QoQ-softer, because Q4 FY26 was an exceptional bullion/energy volatility quarter that is not a repeatable quarterly run-rate. Across the depositories and RTAs, CDSL and CAMS both accelerated profitably, NSDL grew revenue fastest of all six but at a compressing margin, and KFintech is the only one of the six with a YoY PAT decline — a deliberate near-term trade-off for its Ascent-led international expansion. Retaining Q4 FY26 figures throughout this dashboard lets all these realities show side by side rather than being masked by a single "latest quarter" number.

Key Takeaways

The 5 most important things to know about each company's Q1 FY27 print
BSE Limited — 5 Key Takeaways
  1. Record on every count. PAT ₹873 Cr (▲62% YoY, ▲9.7% QoQ) — 14th consecutive record-revenue quarter, in BSE's 150th anniversary year.
  2. Still heavily concentrated. Transaction charges (₹1,328 Cr, ▲80% YoY) are ~78% of revenue — the growth engine and the key risk are the same line item.
  3. Margin expansion was cost-led, not just volume-led. EBITDA margin rose to 67–68% from 65% as total expenses actually fell 6.6% QoQ, plus investment income nearly doubled QoQ.
  4. Outgrowing a real headwind. The April 2026 STT hike created industry-wide volume friction, yet BSE still posted a record — a sign of structural Sensex-derivatives share gains, not just a favourable market backdrop.
  5. Brokerages are more cautious than the print. Mostly Neutral/Hold post-results (JPMorgan TP ₹4,090, Jefferies Hold TP ₹3,520) — valuation (~51.6x FY27E) is the pushback, not the business.
MCX India — 5 Key Takeaways
  1. QoQ drop, YoY strength — read both. PAT ₹413 Cr is down 22% QoQ off Q4 FY26's exceptional base, but still more than double (▲103%) the year-ago quarter.
  2. The underlying franchise kept accelerating. Options ADT growth actually rose to 266% YoY (from 224% in Q3 FY26) even as headline profit normalised.
  3. Bullion dominates activity. Combined bullion futures+options ADT hit ₹7.2 lakh crore (▲427% YoY) — gold/silver volatility is still the single biggest swing factor for MCX earnings.
  4. Legal overhangs cleared this quarter. Bombay High Court dismissed a long-pending 2017 writ petition and upheld MCX's crude-oil settlement policy.
  5. Brokerages are genuinely split. Jefferies Buy (TP ₹3,600, 22% earnings CAGR to FY29E) vs JPMorgan Underweight (TP ₹2,550) — the debate is entirely about how much of the volatility cycle is sustainable.
CDSL — 5 Key Takeaways
  1. Sharp recovery from Q4's dip. PAT ₹117.67 Cr, up 14.9% YoY and a strong 47.5% QoQ bounce back after Q4 FY26's margin squeeze.
  2. The account moat keeps compounding. 18.59 crore demat accounts, with 58 lakh added in this quarter alone — each one a recurring AMC-fee source independent of trading activity.
  3. Standalone PAT decline is noise, not signal. The reported 5.3% YoY standalone fall was purely a lower subsidiary-dividend payout; ex-dividend standalone PAT actually grew 15.6% YoY.
  4. Consensus EPS was missed by a wide margin. ₹5.62–5.65 actual vs ~₹7.20 expected (~21.5% miss) — worth understanding why estimates were that far off before reacting to the "miss" headline.
  5. Brokerage views span the full range. From Add (Equirus, TP ₹1,675) to Underweight (JPMorgan, TP ₹1,200) to Reduce (JM Financial) — CDSL's historic premium-to-peers valuation is the central debate.
NSDL — 5 Key Takeaways
  1. Fastest revenue growth, slowest profit growth. Revenue +65.6% YoY was tops among all six companies, but PAT grew just 9.68% YoY — the mix shift matters more than the top line here.
  2. Banking Services is not a depository business. It's NSDL's payments-bank subsidiary (deposits, mobile payments, debit cards) — now 61% of revenue, but only ~1% segment margin vs Depository's ~44%.
  3. Margin compression is structural, not operational failure. EBITDA margin fell to 19.55% from 30.51% YoY purely because the low-margin Banking Services segment is growing faster than the high-margin Depository core.
  4. Two structural items to track. Karvy litigation remains an overhang, and the Insurance Repository business must demerge into a separate entity by December 2026 under an IRDAI mandate.
  5. The stock has been the weakest of the six. Down 22% YTD 2026, with Equirus rating it an outright "Short" (TP ₹950) — the worst brokerage stance among all six companies covered here.
CAMS — 5 Key Takeaways
  1. Best PAT growth among the depositories/RTAs. PAT ₹128.02 Cr, up 17.35% YoY, with EBITDA margin holding near all-time-high levels (46.26%).
  2. The core moat hit a fresh record. MF AUM serviced reached ₹56 lakh crore (▲14.8% YoY) at 67.2% market share — still the dominant RTA in India by a wide margin over KFintech.
  3. Diversification is working, if still small. Non-MF revenue (CAMSPay, Alternatives, KRA) grew 28.4% YoY — more than double the overall growth rate — though it's still only ~15% of revenue.
  4. Management put numbers behind its guidance. ~13% FY27 revenue growth and ~16% EBITDA growth guided explicitly — one of the few companies here giving that level of forward specificity.
  5. The market has rewarded the consistency. Stock +9% YTD / +10% 1-yr, a relative outperformer, with Axis Capital initiating at Buy.
KFintech — 5 Key Takeaways
  1. The only YoY profit decline among the six. PAT fell 2.6% YoY to ₹75.21 Cr despite revenue growing a strong 30.1% YoY — a deliberate near-term trade-off, not a demand problem.
  2. International expansion is the real growth story. Non-domestic MF revenue (via Ascent Fund Services) surged 182% YoY and is now 39.6% of the mix, up from 34.5% in FY26.
  3. Margin compression was flagged in advance. PAT margin fell to 21.1% from 28.2% YoY due to Ascent integration costs; management guides FY27 EBITDA margin back up to 44–45% as the acquisition scales.
  4. The market looked past the headline miss. Stock rose 8.7% on results day — investors rewarded the revenue beat and diversification trajectory over the YoY PAT dip.
  5. A major leadership change is coming. Dinesh Khara (former SBI Chairman) becomes CEO from October 2026 — the most significant governance development of the quarter across all six companies.

How to Read This Page — Definitions
Revenue from Operations vs Total Income
"Revenue from Operations" = money earned from the company's core licensed activity (transaction fees, AMC charges, RTA fees, etc.). "Total Income" adds investment/treasury income and other non-core income on top. The segment splits below are of Revenue from Operations unless stated otherwise.
Segment = How the Company Earns, Not Just a Product Name
Each bar below breaks revenue into the actual business lines a company reports to SEBI/exchanges — e.g. "Transaction Charges" (BSE) is fees on every trade executed; "Banking Services" (NSDL) is fee income from its banking-services subsidiary, not interest income; "Depository Activity" (CDSL) is demat/settlement fees, separate from AMC (account-maintenance) charges.
Where a Company Doesn't Disclose a Rupee Split
MCX and CAMS don't publish an exact rupee-crore product-line revenue split in their Q1 FY27 filings. For those two, this page uses the closest disclosed proxy (turnover/ADT by commodity for MCX; MF vs non-MF revenue share for CAMS) and labels it clearly as such — no invented rupee figures.

BSE's Q1 FY27 revenue grew only 0.1% QoQ, but PAT grew 9.7% QoQ and 62% YoY. How does profit grow so much faster than revenue?

▼
English
Two forces are doing the work. First, total expenses actually fell 6.6% QoQ (₹520 Cr vs ₹557 Cr in Q4 FY26) even as revenue held flat — cost discipline directly lifting the operating margin (EBITDA margin expanded to 67–68% from 65% a year ago). Together, flat topline plus falling costs plus a big jump in other income produces PAT growth well ahead of revenue growth — a genuinely high-quality quarter on the cost side, though the other-income jump specifically is less likely to repeat at the same magnitude every quarter.

▼
English
It's genuinely mixed, and the mix explains the margin move mechanically. Banking Services revenue more than doubled YoY to ₹313.84 Cr and is now NSDL's largest segment (60.75% of total) — but banking-services-type revenue structurally carries a lower margin than the core depository business (custody, settlement), which is a high-margin, largely fixed-cost franchise. So the 1,343 bps margin compression isn't a sign NSDL is becoming less efficient at its existing business — it's a sign the revenue mix is shifting toward a lower-margin (but real, cash-generating) line. The open question for investors is whether Banking Services margins improve with scale over the next several quarters, the way depository margins did in NSDL's earlier growth phase, or whether this is simply a permanently lower-margin business NSDL has chosen to build for diversification's sake.

English
The market appears to have been reacting to the beat-versus-expectations and the growth story, not the headline YoY comparison.
