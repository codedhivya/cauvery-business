Crude oil & raw material impact

In short: management says crude swung from about $60–65 before the war to above $100, pushing input costs up 30–35%. About 90% of the business (Fabric + Home Care) is crude-linked, which is why Jyothy was hit harder than more diversified peers.

Pril & Fa exit: sales impact and Henkel's plans

Short answer: Henko is Jyothy's own brand and is not leaving. The German firm Henkel did not renew its licence for Pril (dishwash) and Fa (personal care) beyond 31 May 2026. Sales will be affected, fully so from Q2 FY27, the first quarter without Pril. I found no official Henkel announcement that it will relaunch Pril or Fa in India, but it already sells directly in India, so commentators see a re-entry as possible.

Why did the share price fall? What are the triggers?

In short: the Henkel exit is only one cause. The stock hit a 52-week low of ₹183.25 on 1 October 2026, down about 42% in a year. The reasons stacked up: (1) loss of Pril/Fa, (2) crude oil and the West Asia war, (3) weak pricing power against competition, (4) the monsoon failure, (5) inflation, (6) weak urban demand, and (7) heavy foreign selling across the whole market.

Working capital & cash flow from operations (CFO)

In short: Jyothy runs a negative cash conversion cycle (FY26: −13 days): suppliers fund inventory and customer credit. FY26 CFO was ₹412 cr, 92% of EBITDA. But receivable days have crept up from 15 in FY22 to 25.5 in FY26, and in FY25 that cut CFO to ₹311 cr. With Q1 FY27 EBITDA halved, FY27 CFO will come under pressure from lower profit; the large cash balance limits the risk.

Outlook, catalysts & risks

Management guidance

  • Double-digit FY27 revenue growth excluding Pril and Fa: high single-digit volume growth plus 3–4% pricing.
  • EBITDA margins stay under pressure through the year; H2 expected to be substantially better than H1.
  • Gradual cost relief, more meaningful from October if crude-linked inputs stay stable (September's spike tests this).
  • Projects are under way to cut crude dependence, including plant-based alternatives.
  • Levers: selective pricing, procurement, value engineering, modern trade, e-commerce, quick commerce.

Catalysts

  • Crude easing → margin recovery
  • Exo bio-enzymatic liquid and Maxo incense scale-up
  • Fabric Care momentum (Ujala, Henko, Morelight)
  • Net cash of about ₹850 cr gives room to invest

Risks

  • Crude back above $110 in September, so relief may slip
  • Pril gap in premium dishwash liquid
  • New competition if Henkel re-enters directly
  • Soap-noodle inflation; subdued urban demand
