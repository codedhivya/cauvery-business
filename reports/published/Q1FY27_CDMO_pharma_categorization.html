India CDMO & Pharma Prospects — Category Map

Where CDMO tiers, domestic-branded plays, exporters and innovators stand after the Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026) print — Anthem Biosciences, Laurus Labs, Neuland Labs, Divi's Laboratories, Sai Life Sciences and Gland Pharma read against Wockhardt, Glenmark and Sun Pharma.

✓ Q1 FY27 Actuals · Apr–Jun 2026 12 companies deep-dived Updated 10 Sep 2026 IST

Q1 FY27: the six CDMO-focused names booked a combined ~₹8,590 Cr of revenue, and five of the six grew both revenue and profit double digits or more YoY — even as the sixth (Anthem Biosciences) posted a scheduled-delivery dip that its own management calls the "softest quarter" of FY27. Layered against Wockhardt's swing to profit, Glenmark's base-effect PAT surge and Sun Pharma's India-led growth offsetting a US decline, the quarter shows a clear pattern: outsourced manufacturing (CDMO) and India-anchored branded/innovative franchises are absorbing global generic-pricing stress better than pure commodity exporters. "Domestic" is not one story either — Mankind Pharma's 13% revenue growth was clean and organic with margins expanding, Torrent Pharmaceuticals' 55% revenue jump was almost entirely the JB Pharma merger and came with profit growth of just 3% and a near-10x jump in debt, while Eris Lifesciences traded a margin dip for a strategic push into insulins and GLP-1s.

🔬 What is a CDMO / CRDMO?

A Contract Development and Manufacturing Organisation develops and/or manufactures drugs, APIs or biologics for global pharma and biotech clients, under the client's brand — not its own. A CRDMO ("Research" added) also runs early drug-discovery chemistry for those clients. The two diagrams below show how the money and the product actually move, and which part of the drug life cycle each model covers.

CDMOs turn pharmaceutical innovation into at-scale, reliable supply — the brand owner keeps the molecule and the market, the CDMO carries the development and manufacturing risk in between, and gets paid a contract fee for it rather than a share of brand economics.

Because CDMO/CRDMO revenue comes from contracts rather than owned brands, order-book visibility, molecule/customer concentration and multi-year capacity commitments matter more here than for a branded-product company — that distinction is the basis for the Category I / Category II split used to compare the six CDMO names on Q1 FY27 results.

🔬 Why we split CDMOs into two categories

Category I and Category II are not official industry or regulatory labels — there is no SEBI or exchange classification that splits CDMOs this way. They are an analytical framework, applied here because Indian markets currently lump every "CDMO stock" into one bucket even though the six names behave very differently:

Category I — Integrated & Scaled Majors: the CDMO business is one large, established leg of a bigger, diversified company. Revenue scale is big (₹1,500+ Cr a quarter), CDMO/custom-synthesis is a meaningful share of revenue (40%+) alongside other businesses (generic APIs, own formulations), manufacturing spans multiple technology platforms (small molecule, peptides, biologics/injectables), the client base is broad and blue-chip, and multi-year capacity expansion is already underway. Divi's Laboratories, Laurus Labs, Gland Pharma.

Category II — Niche & Emerging High-Margin CDMOs: smaller, more specialised, closer to being pure-play CDMOs. Revenue scale is smaller (well under ₹1,000 Cr a quarter), margins are often the best in the sector because the work is highly specialised, but revenue is more concentrated in a handful of customers, products or molecules — which is exactly why their quarterly numbers swing more. They are earlier in scaling a pipeline of late-phase molecules into steady commercial revenue. Anthem Biosciences, Neuland Laboratories, Sai Life Sciences.

In short: Category I is "the CDMO business has grown into scale and diversification," Category II is "the company mostly is the CDMO business, and is still concentrated while it scales."

Criteria used to sort the six names: ₹1,500+ Cr quarterly revenue scale · CDMO/custom-synthesis is ≥40% of revenue · multi-modality platforms (small molecule, peptides, biologics/injectables) · diversified global blue-chip client base · large multi-year capex cycles already underway.

Category I

The scaled CDMO majors

Bigger books, broader client rosters, capacity being added ahead of demand.

Category II

Niche & emerging high-margin CDMOs

Smaller revenue base, best-in-class margins, real customer/molecule concentration.

Why the split matters this quarter: Category I's three names (Divi's, Laurus, Gland) all grew revenue AND margin together — the hallmark of scale absorbing fixed costs. Category II's three (Anthem, Neuland, Sai Life) carry the sector's best margins (27–40% EBITDA) but also its most volatile top lines this quarter — Anthem fell 22.6% YoY on delivery timing, Neuland's 116% YoY "growth" is really a low-base rebound (down 17.6% QoQ from Q4's spillover), and Sai Life's steadier 12% growth still saw the stock mark it down 4% on the print. Read Category II on a full-year, not single-quarter, basis.

🏠 Domestic Players — results, segments & valuation

Three India-anchored branded majors: Q1 FY27 results, segment mix and where the stock trades today. The three sit in two very different growth stories.

Organic compounders

Growing on their own steam

Volume- and mix-led growth, balance sheets that haven't had to stretch for it.

M&A-scaled major

Growth bought with the balance sheet

Headline revenue growth this quarter is real, but it's inorganic — and it came with leverage.

Mankind's 13% revenue growth was entirely organic — volumes up 4.7%, chronic therapies up 15.8%, margins expanding 250bps — the cleanest print of the three. Eris chose to trade margin for optionality, taking DBF EBITDA margin from 37% to 35% to fund a push into insulin analogs and GLP-1s (its Sundae brand already leads India's semaglutide prescriptions), a bet that hasn't paid off in this quarter's numbers yet but is clearly a multi-year call. Torrent's 55% revenue "growth" is the JB Pharma merger showing up in the base, not organic demand — PAT grew just 3% because a near-10x jump in debt (₹1,192 Cr → ₹11,651 Cr) pushed interest costs up 445% YoY and interest cover down to 5.46x. All three are priced as long-term compounders (29–84x trailing P/E) — but Eris is the one name here priced well below its two domestic peers.

Bottom line: on this Q1 FY27 evidence, CDMO exposure (either category) and a strong India-branded or innovation franchise are the two safer places to be positioned inside Indian pharma, while a business model leaning on plain-vanilla US generic exports carries the most structural pricing and policy risk. Category II CDMO's quarterly lumpiness is a real feature, not a flaw to dismiss — it argues for judging these names on order-book visibility and full-year trajectory rather than any single quarter, including this one.
