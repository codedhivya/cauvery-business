5 Key Takeaways — Q1 FY27

01
Profit Doubled — But It's a Price Story
Standalone net profit +112.3% YoY to ₹17,034 Cr came almost entirely from crude realisation jumping to $99.45/bbl. Production was flat, so this isn't an operational re-rating.
02
Consolidated Tells the Opposite Story
Group PAT fell 43.3% YoY to ₹6,554 Cr as HPCL absorbed a ₹12,265 Cr loss from under-recoveries on the same crude spike. Standalone and consolidated diverged sharply this quarter.
03
MRPL Turned Around, Adding Cushion
MRPL swung from a ₹271 Cr loss to a ₹946 Cr profit as refining throughput rose sharply — a rare bright spot on the downstream side that partly offset the HPCL drag.
04
Growth Capex Is Being Funded
₹40,000 Cr+ committed to Western Offshore plus the first deepwater well spudded in Mahanadi basin — ONGC is deploying this windfall into future production rather than just distributing it.
05
Cheap, But This Print Is a High-Water Mark
At ~9.2x P/E and ~5.49% dividend yield, ONGC screens cheap. But each $5/bbl move in Brent swings annual PAT by ~₹3,500-4,000 Cr — so treat this quarter as crude-cycle-driven, not a new steady-state run-rate.

Why ONGC Did Well — Q1 FY27

1 · Crude Price Windfall
$99.45/bbl
Net crude realisation jumped 50.4% YoY from $66.13/bbl, and 66.5% in rupee terms to ₹9,419/bbl, as Brent spiked on the West Asia crisis during the quarter. As an upstream producer, ONGC captures this price rise directly — nearly the entire profit surge traces back to realisation, not volume.
2 · Record Profitability
PBT ₹22,848 Cr
Standalone net profit more than doubled YoY to ₹17,034 Cr — ONGC's highest-ever quarterly profit before tax. Gross revenue grew 45.2% to ₹46,460 Cr even though production volumes were broadly flat, underlining the pure price-driven nature of this beat.
3 · New Well Gas Pricing
₹3,998 Cr NWG Revenue
Revenue from New Well Gas (priced at market rates rather than APM) generated an incremental ₹1,897 Cr over what APM pricing would have yielded — now ~38% of ONGC's nomination gas portfolio revenue, a structurally positive mix shift.
4 · Growth Capex on Track
₹40,000 Cr+ Western Offshore
ONGC has over ₹40,000 Cr of Western Offshore projects under implementation for future production growth, and spudded its first-ever deepwater well in the Mahanadi basin — a new exploration frontier.

Key Themes — Q1 FY27

Theme 1 — Pure Price Play This Quarter

With crude and gas production essentially flat, the entire earnings surge came from realisation. This makes Q1 FY27 a high-quality read on ONGC's operating leverage to oil prices — each $5/bbl move in Brent is estimated to swing annual PAT by roughly ₹3,500–4,000 Cr.

Theme 2 — Standalone vs Consolidated Divergence

The same crude spike that boosted ONGC's upstream earnings hurt HPCL's refining/marketing margins via under-recoveries, dragging consolidated PAT down 43.3% even as standalone PAT surged 112.3%. Investors need to track both numbers — they tell very different stories this quarter.

Theme 3 — Growth Capex & Exploration

₹40,000 Cr+ committed to Western Offshore projects, plus the first deepwater well spudded in the Mahanadi basin, signal ONGC is using this cash-flow windfall to fund the next leg of production growth rather than just distributing it.

Subsidiaries & Group Structure — Q1 FY27

ℹ ONGC's consolidated result reflects a group of downstream and overseas entities whose fortunes can move opposite to the parent's upstream business — exactly what happened this quarter.
HPCL (Hindustan Petroleum) — ~54.9% owned
Loss ₹12,265 Cr
Consolidated net loss driven by under-recoveries on petroleum products as crude prices spiked sharply amid the West Asia crisis — refining/marketing margins were squeezed even as ONGC's own upstream earnings soared. This is the single largest drag on ONGC's consolidated Q1 FY27 numbers.
MRPL (Mangalore Refinery) — ~71.6% owned
PAT ₹946 Cr
Sharp turnaround from a ₹271 Cr loss in Q1 FY26. Revenue nearly doubled to ₹41,609 Cr (+46.1% YoY) as crude throughput rose to 4.43 MMT from 3.52 MMT. Achieved SAF (Sustainable Aviation Fuel) certification and received PNGRB authorisation for a new ATF pipeline during the quarter.
ONGC Videsh (OVL) — Wholly Owned
Overseas E&P Arm
India's largest overseas oil & gas multinational, holding assets across ~15 countries. Also benefits from the same crude price tailwind as the parent, though results are consolidated separately and were not the focus of this quarter's headline commentary.

Risk Matrix

Sector-Wide Risks
🔴 High
Crude Price Volatility
This quarter's entire profit surge came from price, not volume — ONGC's earnings can reverse just as sharply if crude prices retreat from current elevated levels.
🔴 High
Geopolitical Dependence
The West Asia crisis drove this quarter's realisation spike. Any de-escalation or resolution could compress crude prices and reverse the tailwind quickly.
🟡 Medium
Windfall Tax / Policy Risk
Sharp crude price spikes have historically triggered windfall tax discussions in India — a policy risk that could claw back some of the upside.
🟡 Medium
Under-Recovery Burden Sharing
If retail fuel prices are kept administratively low during a crude spike, ONGC has historically been asked to share under-recovery burdens with OMCs — a risk to watch if crude stays elevated.
ONGC-Specific Risks
🔵 Medium
Flat Production Growth
Crude oil and gas production were both broadly flat YoY. Sustained profit growth beyond price cycles requires the Western Offshore and Mahanadi deepwater projects to deliver.
🔴 High
HPCL Consolidation Drag
HPCL's ₹12,265 Cr consolidated loss shows how sharply refining/marketing economics can offset upstream gains — consolidated PAT volatility is now materially higher than standalone.
🟢 Low-Med
Exploration Execution
The first deepwater well in Mahanadi basin is early-stage; commercial success is unproven and multi-year in nature.
🟢 Low-Med
Capital Allocation
₹40,000 Cr+ committed to Western Offshore — execution discipline and returns on this capex will determine medium-term production trajectory.

ONGC screens statistically cheap at ~9x trailing P/E and ~0.82x P/B, with a healthy trailing dividend yield of ~5.49% — classic PSU value-and-yield characteristics. The consensus BUY rating (30 analysts, average target ~₹276) reflects this Q1 FY27 beat, but the wide target dispersion (₹205–₹340) reflects genuine disagreement on where crude prices settle from here.
