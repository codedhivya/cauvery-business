GE Vernova T&D India — NSE: GVT&D · Formerly ALSTOM T&D / GE T&D · MD & CEO: Sandeep Zanzaria · Part of GE Vernova Grid Solutions
Business Overview

GE Vernova T&D India is one of India's oldest transmission-equipment franchises (100+ years, formerly English Electric / ALSTOM T&D), now the listed Indian arm of GE Vernova's Grid Solutions business. It supplies the full range of high-voltage transmission equipment up to 765 kV and beyond — air-insulated switchgear (AIS), gas-insulated switchgear (GIS), locally-manufactured power transformers, grid automation, and rail electrification/signalling equipment.

Q1 FY27 story: Revenue and PAT both grew strongly on execution of the record FY26 order book, but fresh order bookings fell 30% YoY to ₹1,137 Cr as the domestic TBCB (tariff-based competitive bidding) pipeline stayed soft through Jan-Mar before improving into June. Management retained its FY27 base-order guidance of ₹7,000-8,000 Cr (ex-HVDC).

Backlog mix: 77% private developers, 21% central utilities/PSUs, 2% state utilities — a deliberate multi-year shift away from state-utility receivables risk. Export revenue was 30% of Q1 sales (down from 33% in FY26), with Indian factories being qualified to supply the US market.

Balance sheet: Debt-free with ₹2,930 Cr cash generated/available in the quarter. FY26 (year ended Mar-26): revenue ₹6,206-6,712 Cr, PBT ₹1,650 Cr, final dividend ₹10/share (record date 21 Aug 2026); AGM scheduled 9 Sep 2026.

Apar Industries — NSE: APARINDS · CMD: Kushal N. Desai · MD: Chaitanya Desai · Global No.3 in conductors
Business Overview

Apar Industries operates three businesses: Conductors (aluminium/ACSR overhead transmission conductors — top-3 global producer), Transformer & Specialty Oils (4th-largest global transformer-oil maker), and Power & Telecom Cables (largest domestic renewable-sector cable maker). It is a leading exporter, supplying utilities and EPC contractors across 100+ countries.

Q1 FY27 story: Record June-quarter across every metric — revenue, EBITDA and PAT all at all-time highs for the quarter, powered by the Transformer & Specialty Oils division (+34.8% YoY) and Power & Telecom Cables (+29.5% YoY), with the larger but lower-margin Conductors business growing a steadier 19.9% YoY despite metal-price volatility.

International expansion: Board approved a wholly-owned UK subsidiary (Apar Industries UK) for conductor/OPGW/cable trading, and up to BRL 3 mn additional investment into its existing Brazil subsidiary (Apar Industries Latam Ltda).

Capital raise: Shareholders approved a ₹2,500 Cr fundraise at an EGM on 30 Jul 2026; the QIP opened 10 Aug 2026 with a floor price of ₹14,801.25/share, aimed at funding the capex pipeline behind global transmission and data-centre-driven demand.

Reading the Valuation Gap — GE Vernova T&D vs Apar Industries

GE Vernova T&D trades at close to double Apar's earnings multiple despite Apar posting faster PAT growth (+77.8% vs +25.2%) in the latest quarter. The market is pricing GE Vernova T&D's pure-play, debt-free, HVDC/GIS-capable franchise and its scarcity value (limited number of listed pure T&D-equipment names) at a premium, while Apar — a larger-revenue but lower-margin, more working-capital- and commodity-price-sensitive conductor/cable/oils business now raising fresh equity — trades at a relative discount despite the sharper earnings beat. Both multiples sit well above long-run averages for Indian capital goods, reflecting the broader T&D/grid capex re-rating across the sector (Hitachi Energy and Siemens Energy trade even richer).

🎓 School · Business models explained · How to tell the five companies apart
The One-Line Test

All five companies sell into the same India power-grid capex story, which is why their share prices often move together — but they earn their money in five genuinely different ways. The fastest way to tell them apart: ask "what do they actually sell, and does revenue repeat or does it have to be re-won every quarter?"

GE Vernova T&D — The Contractor
Sells: transmission equipment + execution
Wins a project (order), builds/supplies transformers, switchgear and GIS/AIS, then commissions it. Revenue = backlog converted to delivery. One segment, no annuity layer — every rupee of next year's revenue has to be re-won as an order first.
Apar Industries — The Materials Supplier
Sells: conductors, transformer oil, cables (by the tonne/km)
Doesn't build substations or transformers itself — it supplies the raw conductor wire, insulating oil and cable that GE Vernova T&D, Siemens Energy, Hitachi Energy and every utility/EPC contractor need. Revenue = volume × realization, heavily linked to aluminium/copper prices.
Siemens Energy — The Hybrid
Sells: grid equipment (60%) + power-plant O&M (40%)
Power Transmission looks like GE Vernova T&D — order-driven equipment. Power Generation is different: servicing turbines and generators at cement/steel/sugar plants is closer to an annuity — the customer keeps paying for maintenance long after the machine is sold.
ABB India — The Platform
Sells: electrification, motion, automation — across ALL industry, not just power
Power/grid is only a slice of ABB's business. It also sells motors, drives and automation into cement, metals, EV charging, data centres and buildings. This is why ABB's numbers move less in lockstep with pure T&D names — it's a diversified industrial bet with power as one ingredient.
Hitachi Energy — The Full Stack
Sells: everything GE Vernova T&D sells, PLUS HVDC, PLUS the software layer
The only one of the five with a genuine HVDC franchise (ultra-long-distance power transmission) and a real digital/grid-automation software business layered on top of the hardware. This "most complete stack" is the single biggest reason it commands the richest valuation in the group.

How Commodity Prices Move Their Numbers

All five companies buy the same basic inputs — aluminium, copper, CRGO (cold-rolled grain-oriented) electrical steel, mineral/transformer oil, silver (for contacts) — but how much that input bill can swing their margin in a single quarter depends on how "materials-heavy" vs "technology/software-heavy" their business is. Apar sits at one extreme (its product basically is the commodity, shaped); Hitachi Energy and ABB sit closer to the other (commodities are one input into a much more engineered, higher value-add product).

Why Is Hitachi Energy Priced So High?

At a P/E of roughly 145-170x, Hitachi Energy trades at close to double ABB and Siemens Energy's multiples, and far above Apar's ~55-58x — the richest valuation of any name in this comparison set, in a sector that is already expensive. The premium rests on a genuine combination of factors, not just sentiment:

  • Only local, full-stack HVDC franchise: Hitachi Energy is among the very few companies — and the only one manufacturing at Indian scale — supplying HVDC (high-voltage direct current) systems, the technology used for bulk, ultra-long-distance power transfer (e.g. moving renewable power from remote generation sites to demand centres). It commissioned India's first HVDC city-centre infeed, in Mumbai.
  • The deepest backlog cover in the peer set: a record ₹32,222 Cr order backlog is roughly 13x its FY26 annual revenue — far higher backlog-to-revenue cover than GE Vernova T&D or Siemens Energy — giving the market unusually high confidence in multi-year revenue visibility.
  • A genuine digital/software layer: grid-automation, asset-management and power-quality software sit on top of the hardware, adding a stickier, higher-margin revenue stream that pure equipment makers don't have.
  • Demonstrated margin expansion, not just growth: FY26 EBITDA margin rose 610 bps YoY to 15.4% while revenue grew 28% — the market is rewarding proof of operating leverage, not just a growth story.
  • Scarcity value: there are very few listed, pure-play ways in India to buy exposure to the HVDC/grid-technology theme with global (Hitachi Energy Ltd / formerly ABB) technology backing.

See the Charts tab for the P/E-vs-margin chart backing this: Hitachi Energy combines the highest multiple with only a mid-pack margin — the market is paying largely for backlog visibility and technology scarcity, not today's margin alone.

Is the product genuinely unique and outstanding, or is this a growth-extrapolation premium?

Honestly, both — and it's worth separating the two. The technology is real and differentiated: HVDC manufactured locally, the broadest product-plus-software stack among the five, and a backlog that dwarfs peers on a revenue-cover basis. That is not hype; it shows up directly in the FY26 margin-expansion data. But "outstanding" and "worth 150-170x earnings" are different claims — Hitachi Energy's technology edge is shared, to varying degrees, by Siemens Energy and GE Vernova globally (both have HVDC capability elsewhere in their global groups, even if not manufactured at the same India scale), so the moat is deep but not exclusive. A valuation this rich is pricing in years of flawless backlog-to-revenue conversion at expanding margins; any slip — a large order deferral, a margin miss, or a sector-wide de-rating (as already happened once this year on eased Chinese-import norms, which hit Hitachi Energy alongside BHEL, Siemens Energy and Cummins India) — has more room to hurt the stock than it would a name priced at 55-70x. The fair read: differentiated business, yes; a valuation that already assumes it stays differentiated for a long time, also yes.

🏆 Verdict · Synthesis, not a recommendation
🏆 GE Vernova T&D India
A financially pristine, debt-free execution story that delivered another strong quarter on revenue and PAT growth, but the 30% YoY drop in fresh order bookings is the number to track — it will determine whether the extremely rich valuation (~87-90x P/E) is sustained or re-rated lower over the next 2-3 quarters. The private-sector-heavy backlog (77%) and emerging US export qualification are genuine structural positives; the near-term question is whether domestic order momentum, which management says improved into June after a soft Jan-Mar, actually shows up in Q2 FY27 bookings.
🏆 Apar Industries
The standout quarter of this peer set on growth (+78% PAT) with the broadest-based improvement — all three segments (conductors, oils, cables) grew, and margins expanded meaningfully. The ₹2,500 Cr QIP is a rational move to fund the capex behind a genuinely large addressable opportunity (global transmission capex, data-centre power infrastructure), but near-term dilution and Apar's inherently thinner, commodity-linked margins keep its multiple below the pure-equipment names even after the results-day rally. Nomura's post-results target (₹15,400) sitting below the current market price is a useful reminder that a single quarter's re-rating can outrun broker models.
🏆 Peer Set Context
Siemens Energy India posted the best margin profile in the group (21.9% EBIT) and the sharpest one-day re-rating on results (+12%); Hitachi Energy posted the fastest PAT growth (+123.5%) and carries the richest valuation and the largest order backlog (₹32,222 Cr); ABB India's record order intake (+50% YoY) came with only modest margin improvement, leaving its large post-Robotics cash pile as the key catalyst investors are watching. Across all five names, India's grid-investment supercycle (₹9 lakh Cr T&D outlay guided through 2032, 500 GW renewable target by 2030, data-centre power demand) remains the shared structural driver — the differentiation between names is increasingly about margin durability and order-booking consistency rather than the demand story itself, which is common to all.
