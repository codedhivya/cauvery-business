SBI Life led on the two measures that drive a life insurer's economics this quarter: APE grew 36% to ₹5,380 cr — roughly four times HDFC Life's 9% — and VNB rose 29% to ₹1,410 cr, larger in absolute terms than the other two combined. It did this while trading at the lowest P/EV of the three, at 1.32x.

The runner-up case is real, and it belongs to ICICI Prudential. SBI Life's VNB margin contracted 120 bps to 26.2%, meaning its growth came partly at the cost of profitability per rupee written. ICICI Pru moved the opposite way — margin expanded 220 bps to 26.7%, the highest of the three, on a 45.7% surge in protection APE, the most profitable product line in life insurance. ICICI also holds by far the strongest solvency at 225.4%. A reader who weights margin quality and capital buffer over headline growth would reasonably rank ICICI Pru first.

HDFC Life is the clear laggard on this quarter's growth — 9% APE, 9% VNB — while carrying the most expensive multiple at 2.07x P/EV. Its franchise quality is not in question; the gap between its growth and its valuation is.

This is a research read of disclosed fundamentals, not investment advice. "Best" here means best on the specific Q1 FY27 metrics reviewed below — one quarter, on a narrow metric set. It is not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security, and a single quarter is a poor basis for a long-horizon decision in a business whose profits emerge over decades.

The Four Lenses how each company scores, and why
① Growth — SBI Life, decisively

SBI Life's 36% APE growth is an outlier in a sector where low-teens is a good quarter, and it converted into 29% VNB growth — so the volume was genuinely value-accretive, not just premium collection. New business premium rose 23% to ₹8,910 cr and it holds 22.2% private market share on individual rated premium. ICICI Pru's 14.6% APE is respectable and its 24.9% VNB growth outpaced it, meaning better-quality growth. HDFC Life's 9% on both is the weak point of an otherwise strong franchise.

SBI +36%ICICI +14.6%HDFC +9%
② Profitability — ICICI Prudential

ICICI Pru posts both the highest VNB margin (26.7%) and the only clear YoY expansion (+220 bps), driven by a 45.7% surge in protection APE — structurally the highest-margin line a life insurer writes. SBI Life's margin moved the other way, contracting 120 bps, which is the cost of its volume push and the single most important qualifier on its win. Management has guided to 26–28% for FY27, so it expects to recover. HDFC Life sits lowest at 25.0%, though improving off the FY26 exit.

ICICI 26.7% ▲SBI 26.2% ▼HDFC 25.0%
③ Moat — SBI Life, on distribution

SBI Life's moat is the SBI branch network — a bancassurance channel of a scale no competitor can rebuild, underpinning 22.2% private share of individual rated premium. HDFC Life's is brand and product sophistication, holding #2 private position at 16.3% share, though notably its Q1 growth came despite HDFC Bank distribution being flat, which cuts both ways: resilience in other channels, but a stalled primary engine. ICICI Pru's edge is protection leadership and a zero-NPA investment record since inception — a governance signal rather than a distribution one.

SBI 22.2% IRP shareHDFC 16.3% shareICICI protection +45.7%
④ Valuation — SBI Life cheapest, HDFC richest

SBI Life trades at 1.32x P/EV, ICICI Pru at 1.46x, HDFC Life at 2.07x. The striking feature is that the fastest-growing and largest-VNB business carries the lowest multiple, while the slowest-growing carries the highest — HDFC Life's premium reflects franchise pedigree and product mix rather than current momentum. Whether that premium is deserved is precisely the question a reader should form their own view on. These multiples come from a lower-tier source and should be re-verified before they drive any decision — see caveats.

Caveats, Conflicts & Sources
⚠ Source conflicts encountered and how they were resolved
HDFC Life VNB margin. A news report described the margin as "25%, below the 24.5% consensus estimate" — internally contradictory, since 25% exceeds 24.5%. Resolved in favour of the company's own Q1 FY27 investor presentation: 25.0%, up from 24.2% at FY26 year-end. Tier-1 disclosure wins over secondary commentary.

SBI Life PAT. Sources report ₹720 cr and ₹725 cr for the same quarter (both "+22% YoY"), and one headline stated profit was "down from ₹805 cr YoY" — irreconcilable with a 22% increase and most likely a templating error at that outlet. ₹720 cr is used here, being the figure most consistently reported alongside the ₹590 cr base. Treat the last digit as uncertain.

ICICI Pru Embedded Value. No Q1 FY27 EV disclosure could be sourced. The ₹52,989 cr shown is as at 31 March 2026, against 30 June 2026 figures for the other two. The EV row is therefore not like-for-like, and the derived P/EV for ICICI Pru inherits that limitation.
Confidence by figure. HDFC Life's operating metrics are Tier-1 (company investor presentation) and are the most reliable here. SBI Life's and ICICI Pru's figures are drawn from financial news and results coverage of company disclosures — consistent across multiple outlets, but not read directly off the filings. The valuation multiples (P/EV) come from a single low-tier source and should be independently re-verified against current market data before being relied on; they materially affect the Valuation lens and therefore the scorecard.

What this report does not cover. One quarter only. No multi-year trend, no product-mix detail, no channel economics, no brokerage views (none were sourced for this report). Life insurance profits emerge over decades, and a single quarter's APE growth can reflect timing, a large group deal, or a pre-regulatory-change surge as easily as durable franchise strength.
