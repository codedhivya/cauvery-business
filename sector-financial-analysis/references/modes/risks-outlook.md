# Mode: Risks & Outlook — What Could Go Wrong, and What's Guided

Forward-looking, near-term, and specific. Distinct from the Threats quadrant of a SWOT: that quadrant
holds *structural* external risks, while this mode covers **what is live right now** — this year's
guidance, current headwinds, and the specific things that would change the read.

Read the loaded sector file for that sector's recurring risk drivers and its **event transmission map**,
which links common shocks to the metrics they hit.

## Step 1 — Separate the three things readers conflate

- **Management guidance** — what the company itself has said about the period ahead, quoted and
  attributed. Where guidance was withdrawn or revised, that revision is itself the story.
- **Live risks** — headwinds already visible: input costs, a regulatory proceeding, a large customer
  concentration, a plant or channel disruption, refinancing due.
- **Watch items** — not yet risks, but the specific triggers that would become one, each with a
  threshold where possible. "Margin pressure if input costs stay above X" is useful; "cost pressure" is
  not.

## Step 2 — Ground and size each risk

Every risk gets: what it is, the mechanism by which it reaches results, and — where disclosable — its
scale. "Customer concentration" is weak; "top client is 18% of revenue, contract up for renewal in
FY27" lets a reader judge for themselves.

Where a risk genuinely can't be quantified from disclosure, say so plainly rather than implying
precision. Ranking unquantified risks by a made-up severity score is false rigour.

Note the risks management has *acknowledged* separately from those visible in the numbers but unmentioned
by the company. That gap is often the most informative part of this section.

## Step 3 — Build

1. A risk table or card set: risk, mechanism, scale/exposure, and a severity tag where it can be
   justified from evidence (`.badge` with `.b-neg` / `.b-watch` / `.b-info`).
2. A guidance block quoting what management actually said, attributed and dated.
3. A short "what would change this read" list — the concrete triggers to watch.
4. A `.fnote` for anything where disclosure is partial.

## Scope behaviour

**Single company** — full treatment, company-specific.
**Pair / sweep** — separate shared sector-wide risks (which affect everyone, stated once) from
company-specific ones. Repeating the same sector headwind under every company inflates the section
without adding information.
**Cross-sector** — group risks by sector; a common macro driver affecting several sectors can be stated
once, with its differing transmission per sector noted.

## Deliver

Follow `output-conventions.md`. Frame the outlook as a read of disclosed evidence, not a forecast in this
skill's own voice.
