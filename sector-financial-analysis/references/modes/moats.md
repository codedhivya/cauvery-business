# Mode: Moats — Structural Advantage Analysis

Deeper and more durability-focused than a SWOT strength. The bar: **a moat is something a well-funded
competitor could not replicate quickly even if it decided to.** Scale built over decades, licence
scarcity, proprietary data, switching costs, locked-in economics, genuine brand trust.

A high margin this year is a strength. The reason nobody can take it away is a moat.

Read the loaded sector file for **moat candidates by category** — where durable advantage tends to come
from in that sector — and for the definitions underpinning any claim.

## Step 1 — Identify candidates, and be willing to find none

Work from the sector file's candidate list, but test each against the replication bar rather than
assuming it applies. Ask plainly: if a competitor had unlimited capital and five years, could they build
this? If yes, it isn't a moat.

**If a company genuinely has a thin moat — a new entrant, a commodity product, no distribution scale —
say so plainly.** Manufacturing a moat that isn't there destroys the credibility of every other moat
claim in the same artifact, including the real ones.

## Step 2 — Build

1. Category section header, then one `.moat-card` per company: company name in its palette colour, a
   short punchy moat title ("Distribution Scale Built Over Four Decades"), 3–5 sentences of `.moat-body`
   naming the **specific numbers** that back the claim, then a `.moat-tag` one-line label.
2. `.g2` / `.g3` grids for 2–3 cards side by side within a category.
3. For 3+ companies, an optional closing comparison table — moat dimensions as rows, companies as
   columns — so a reader can see who leads on which axis rather than reading five cards and holding it
   all in their head.

A moat claim without a number attached is an opinion. "Largest agent network" is weak; "1.4 million
agents, more than the next four competitors combined" is a moat argument.

## Scope behaviour

**Single company** — one or two cards covering its distinct moat sources.
**Pair / sweep** — one card per company plus the comparison table.
**Cross-sector** — assess each within its own sector. Moat *types* are comparable in the abstract, but
their strength is not: a regulatory licence means something very different in banking than in cement.

## Deliver

Follow `output-conventions.md`. Worth noting in the reply that this analysis ages more slowly than a
quarterly dashboard — the underlying numbers are as of the sourced period, but the moat thesis is a
longer-run view.
