# Sector: Logistics & Supply Chain (India)

Covers express parcel and last-mile delivery, surface and rail freight, contract logistics and
warehousing, and the technology-led 3PL platforms.

**The defining question is whether the operator owns the assets or orchestrates them.** An asset-heavy
integrator owns aircraft, trucks and hubs, carries the depreciation, and earns a fatter gross margin per
shipment. An **asset-light** platform leases its infrastructure and pays a crowdsourced fleet
per-transaction, so its cost base flexes with volume and its fixed depreciation is small. **They are not
comparable on margin.** A 6% EBITDA margin on an asset-light platform and a 6% margin on an asset-heavy
integrator describe entirely different businesses, and reading one through the other's benchmark is the
central error in this sector.

**Margins here are thin by nature.** That makes **operating leverage the whole story**: once volume
passes the network's fixed cost, incremental orders drop through to profit fast — which is why profit
growth of several hundred percent alongside revenue growth of sixty is normal here and not an anomaly to
be explained away.

## 1. Category taxonomy

| Category | What it is | Asset model | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Express parcel — tech 3PL** | E-commerce parcels, same-day, reverse logistics, orchestrated through a proprietary platform | **Asset-light** — leased hubs, crowdsourced fleet | Shadowfax Technologies, Delhivery |
| **Express — integrated** | Air and surface express with owned network and, often, owned aircraft | **Asset-heavy** | Blue Dart |
| **Hyperlocal / last-mile** | On-demand delivery for quick-commerce, food and instant platforms. Short distance, very high frequency | Asset-light, rider-based | the hyperlocal arms of the above |
| **Surface freight (FTL / LTL)** | Full and part truckload road freight between cities | Mixed — owned or attached fleet | VRL Logistics, TCI Express |
| **Contract logistics & warehousing** | Dedicated warehousing, in-plant and fulfilment operations under multi-year contracts | Leased warehouses | contract logistics operators |
| **Rail & multimodal** | Container rail haulage and terminals | **Asset-heavy**, concession-linked | Container Corporation |

**Classify before you benchmark.** Margin, capital intensity, working-capital shape and the right
valuation multiple all follow from the row, not from the sector.

## 2. How companies in this sector make money

Revenue is **volume × realisation per shipment**, and both halves need reading separately. Volume growth
with falling realisation is a mix shift — often toward lighter, cheaper hyperlocal orders — not a
weakening business. Rising realisation without volume is usually a heavier or longer-haul mix.

The cost base splits into three: **line-haul** (moving goods between cities), **last-mile** (the final
delivery), and **network overhead** (sort centres, hubs, technology). The first two flex with volume; the
third is largely fixed, and it is the fixed layer that creates the operating leverage.

**Density is the economic engine.** Cost per shipment falls as more deliveries cluster into the same
route, hub and rider-hour. That is why pincode reach alone means little — a network covering more
pincodes thinly can be *less* profitable than a denser one covering fewer. Judge expansion by whether
density improved, not by the pincode count.

**Asset-light platforms** lease first-mile, last-mile and sort infrastructure and pay delivery partners
per transaction rather than as fixed payroll. The consequence runs through the whole P&L: very high
fixed-asset turnover, little depreciation, minimal idle capacity, and margins that expand quickly once
volume crosses critical mass. The trade-off is a thinner gross margin per shipment and dependence on
partner availability.

**Enterprise contracts dominate revenue** in express and contract logistics — marketplaces,
quick-commerce platforms and D2C brands. This makes **client concentration** a first-order risk that is
often understated: a single marketplace can be a large share of volume, contracts are re-tendered on
price, and the customer frequently has the option to insource.

## 3. Metric definitions

### Core to every category

- **Shipments / orders (volume)** — the primary operating metric. **Report it alongside revenue**, always;
  revenue alone conceals mix.
- **Revenue per order / per shipment** = revenue ÷ volume. The realisation metric. **Falling realisation
  with rising volume is a mix signal, not a pricing failure** — say which.
- **EBITDA margin**, and its direction. **Judge it against the category band, never the sector.**
- **Operating leverage** — incremental EBITDA ÷ incremental revenue across periods. In a thin-margin
  network business this explains most of the earnings movement and belongs in the narrative.
- **Cost per shipment**, split into line-haul, last-mile and overhead where disclosed.
- **Working capital cycle** — receivable days, payable days, and the net. Enterprise customers pay on
  terms while delivery partners are paid quickly, so a lengthening receivable is an early stress signal.
- **Fixed-asset turnover** — revenue ÷ net fixed assets. **The clearest single read on the asset model**:
  an asset-light platform runs at a multiple of an integrator's.
- **Net debt / equity** and free cash flow. Growth funded by dilution or debt is a different equity story
  from growth funded by operations.
- **Client concentration** — top-client and top-five share of revenue, and contract tenure.

### Express, hyperlocal and last-mile

- **Pincodes served**, **touchpoints**, **operating area (sq ft)** — reach. **Reach is not density**; see §2.
- **Active delivery partners** in the period, and partner churn where disclosed.
- **Delivery success / first-attempt rate**, **turnaround time**, and **return-to-origin (RTO) rate** —
  RTO is pure cost with no revenue and rises with lower-quality demand.
- **Reverse logistics share** — returns handling, a distinct and often better-priced service.
- **Segment split** — express parcel, hyperlocal, heavy/oversize, SME self-serve. Each has a different
  ticket size and margin, so a blended realisation moves on mix alone.

### Surface freight and contract logistics

- **Tonnage and freight realisation per tonne**, **truck utilisation**, **empty-return ratio**
- **Owned vs attached fleet** share — the asset-model question in road freight
- **Warehouse area under management** and **occupancy**; contract tenure and renewal record
- **Fuel cost share** and whether fuel escalation is contractually passed through

## 4. Benchmarks — what good looks like

Indicative, and **category-dependent by design**. Verify against the company's own history.

| Category | Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|---|
| Express — tech 3PL | EBITDA margin | mid single digit and rising; **double digit is exceptional, not the target** |
| Express — integrated | EBITDA margin | low-to-mid teens; the owned network earns more per shipment and carries the depreciation |
| Surface freight | EBITDA margin | low double digit |
| Contract logistics | EBITDA margin | high single to low double digit, with contract tenure supporting it |
| All | Volume growth | ahead of the customer industry's own growth — otherwise share is being lost |
| All | Operating leverage | incremental margin **well above** the current margin; that is the whole thesis in a thin-margin network |
| All | Receivable days | stable or falling; **rising receivables with rising revenue is the sector's classic stress signal** |
| Asset-light | Fixed-asset turnover | very high — a defining characteristic, not a strength on its own |
| All | Top-client share | lower is safer; a marketplace-heavy book carries insourcing risk |
| All | Net debt / equity | low; the model should self-fund once past scale |

**What to watch:** Is volume growing faster than revenue, and if so is realisation falling on **mix** or
on **price**? Is incremental margin above the current margin — is leverage actually working? Are
receivables lengthening? Is expansion adding **density** or only pincodes? How concentrated is the client
book, and are any large accounts building in-house capability?

## 5. Regulatory quick reference

| Area | Body / rule |
|---|---|
| Road transport | **Ministry of Road Transport and Highways**; permits, axle-load and driving-hour rules |
| E-way bill and GST | **GST Council / GSTN** — e-way bill thresholds and documentation; a compliance cost and a data source on freight movement |
| Gig and platform workers | **Code on Social Security** and state platform-worker rules — **the sector's most material regulatory exposure.** A crowdsourced fleet paid per transaction is precisely the arrangement these rules address, and social-security contributions would land on the flexible cost base that makes the asset-light model work. Confirm current status at source |
| Air express | **DGCA** for owned or chartered aircraft; airport and customs handling |
| Rail freight | **Indian Railways / Ministry of Railways** haulage charges; terminal concessions |
| Warehousing | state-level; **WDRA** for regulated warehousing |
| Customs and cross-border | CBIC for international express and bonded movement |
| Competition | **CCI** where a marketplace and its logistics arm are related parties |

## 6. Per-mode specifics

### Headline KPIs by category (`dashboard`)

| Category | KPI strip |
|---|---|
| Express — tech 3PL | Revenue, **orders/shipments**, revenue per order, EBITDA margin, PAT, pincodes, active delivery partners, net debt/equity |
| Express — integrated | Revenue, shipments, realisation, EBITDA margin, PAT, network reach, fleet |
| Hyperlocal | Revenue, orders, revenue per order, EBITDA margin, active partners, cities |
| Surface freight | Revenue, tonnage, realisation per tonne, truck utilisation, EBITDA margin, PAT |
| Contract logistics | Revenue, area under management, occupancy, contract tenure, EBITDA margin, PAT |

**Lead with volume beside revenue.** A revenue-only strip cannot distinguish growth from price.

### Table columns by category (`financials`)

Period · Revenue · **Volume (orders or tonnes)** · **Revenue per unit** · EBITDA · EBITDA% · PAT · PAT% ·
Receivable days · Net debt/equity. Add **segment revenue and growth** as its own table wherever more than
one service line is disclosed — the blended number moves on mix.

Where operating leverage is the story, add an **incremental margin** row: change in EBITDA ÷ change in
revenue.

### Chart reference lines (`charts`)

- **Revenue and volume on one chart, dual axis** — the single most informative view in this sector,
  because the gap between the two lines *is* the realisation story.
- **EBITDA margin** — dashed reference line at the **category** band, never a sector-wide line.
- **Revenue per order** — plot it explicitly rather than leaving it implied.
- **Receivable days** — dashed line at the company's own trailing average; the deterioration signal.
- Never chart an asset-light platform's margin against an integrator's on the same axis without labelling
  the asset model.

### Profile coverage by category (`business-profile`)

Asset model — **owned versus leased infrastructure, employed versus crowdsourced fleet** — and what that
implies for cost flexibility. Network shape: hubs, sort centres, pincodes, touchpoints. Service lines and
their differing economics. **Client base and concentration**, with contract tenure and re-tender risk.
Technology layer and what it actually orchestrates. Funding history and listing status.

### Moat candidates by category (`moats`)

- **Network density** — the real moat. A dense network delivers cheaper per shipment, and density
  compounds because lower cost wins more volume which deepens density.
- **Enterprise integration** — deep API and workflow integration with a marketplace raises switching cost
  well above price.
- **Reach into difficult geography** — pincodes competitors serve unprofitably or not at all.
- **Partner supply** — a large, retained delivery-partner pool is hard to rebuild quickly.
- **Not moats**: fleet size, pincode count alone, or a technology platform described in the abstract.
  **And be sceptical of scale by itself** — in a thin-margin business, unprofitable scale is a liability.

### Valuation (`valuation`)

**EV/EBITDA is the primary lens**, with the category stated, because asset models are not comparable.
P/E is workable only for a settled, profitable operator; for a recently profitable platform it will look
extreme and needs the growth and operating-leverage context beside it.

**EV/Sales** is a reasonable secondary for a company at an early margin stage — say so explicitly rather
than presenting a triple-digit P/E without qualification. Where a company has just crossed into profit,
**state the trajectory** rather than a single multiple: a multiple computed on a trough earnings base is
not a valuation statement.

Compare only within the asset model. An asset-light platform will always screen expensive against an
asset-heavy integrator on EV/EBITDA, for structural reasons rather than sentiment.

### CB Rating substitutions (`cb-rating`)

Thin margins and network economics, so growth and leverage carry more weight than absolute margin:

| Component | Weight | Logistics substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Volume & Revenue Growth | 25% | **Order or tonnage growth first**, revenue second — and whether it outpaces the customer industry |
| Operating Leverage | 25% | Incremental EBITDA ÷ incremental revenue, and margin direction against the **category** band. Absolute margin alone says little here |
| Unit Economics & Density | 20% | Revenue per shipment, cost per shipment where disclosed, and whether expansion added density rather than only reach |
| Balance Sheet & Cash | 15% | Net debt/equity, free cash flow, receivable days, and whether growth is self-funded |
| Client & Network Position | 15% | Client concentration and tenure, integration depth, geographic reach quality |

Total 100%. **PAT Quality is not a separate component** — at these margins a single quarter's PAT is
dominated by leverage effects and is read inside Operating Leverage instead. State the parameter set on
any rendered score.

### Extra sections (`quarterly-report`)

- **Segment Performance** — revenue and growth by service line. Effectively mandatory; the blended number
  hides the mix.
- **Network & Reach** — pincodes, touchpoints, operating area, active partners, with density commentary.
- **Unit Economics** — revenue per order, cost per order, incremental margin.
- **Client Concentration** — top accounts, tenure, insourcing risk.
- **Cash Flow & Ratios** — free cash flow, working capital, fixed-asset turnover.

### Event transmission map (`event-impact`, `risks-outlook`)

| Event | Hits | Exposure basis to cite |
|---|---|---|
| Gig / platform-worker social security rules | Last-mile cost base; the flexibility that underpins the model | Share of deliveries by crowdsourced partners vs employed staff |
| Fuel price move | Line-haul cost | Fuel share of cost; whether escalation is contractually passed through |
| A large marketplace insourcing logistics | Volume and revenue | That client's share of revenue; contract tenure remaining |
| Quick-commerce expansion | Hyperlocal order volume | Hyperlocal share of revenue; exposure to those platforms |
| E-commerce demand shift | Express parcel volume | Segment share; customer-industry growth rate |
| E-way bill / GST change | Compliance cost, documentation | Consignment volumes affected |
| Diesel-to-EV fleet transition | Capex and running cost | Owned vs attached fleet; who bears vehicle cost |
| Warehousing rent cycle | Network overhead | Leased area and lease tenure |

## 7. Where to look (sourcing)

- **Tier 1** — the company's quarterly investor presentation and earnings call. **Volume, pincodes,
  active partners, segment splits and revenue-per-order are disclosed there and usually not in the press
  release**, which makes the presentation essential rather than optional in this sector.
- **The IPO prospectus** for recently listed operators — the fullest description of network, unit
  economics and client concentration that will ever be published.
- **Tier 2** — customer-industry data for the growth denominator: e-commerce and quick-commerce volume
  estimates, and the marketplaces' own disclosures. **Ministry of Road Transport** for freight movement,
  **GSTN e-way bill statistics** as an independent read on national freight volumes.
- Rating-agency rationales are informative on working capital and client concentration.

Search phrasing that surfaces Tier 1: `"<company>" investor presentation orders shipments`,
`"<company>" earnings call transcript revenue per shipment`, `"<company>" red herring prospectus network`.

**Cross-sector work** — companies here participate normally on Revenue, EBITDA%, PAT, Mkt Cap, P/E,
EV/EBITDA and Net Debt, with one caveat that must be stated: **EBITDA margins in logistics are
structurally low**, so a cross-sector margin ranking will place even an excellent operator near the
bottom. Note the sector norm beside the figure, or the table implies underperformance that is not there.
Volume, revenue per shipment, density and client concentration are sector-specific and stay in their own
section.
