# Sector: REITs & InvITs (India)

Covers listed Real Estate Investment Trusts and Infrastructure Investment Trusts.

**These are SEBI-regulated trusts, not operating companies** — a REIT under the SEBI (Real Estate
Investment Trusts) Regulations, an InvIT under the SEBI (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) Regulations.
They are grouped here because the *vehicle* is the same; what each holds is not. **This is an asset
class, not an industry.** A REIT or InvIT is a **trust that passes income through to
unitholders**, not a company that retains and reinvests earnings. What it owns — offices, malls,
transmission lines, toll roads, pipelines, telecom towers — changes the operating drivers underneath,
but never the frame you judge it in: distribution, yield, leverage and the durability of the cash that
funds them.

That is why these do not belong inside the sector of their underlying asset. A transmission InvIT is not
a power generator, and grading it on PLF, merchant tariff or fuel cost misreads it entirely; a road InvIT
is not a construction company; an office REIT is not a residential developer with pre-sales.

**This file is self-sufficient — it is the only one needed to analyse a trust.** Every metric a
property, transmission or road portfolio requires is defined below, including the ones that also appear
in operating-company files. That duplication is deliberate: a trust is read through distribution and
coverage, and pulling in an operating file invites its framing along with its definitions. Reach for
another sector file only for **broader market context** — the office leasing cycle, the transmission
capex cycle — never for the metrics themselves.

**The single question**: is the distribution covered by cash the assets actually generate, and is it
durable? Everything below serves that.

## 1. Category taxonomy

| Category | What it holds | How it earns | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Office REIT** | Completed Grade-A office parks | Contracted rent + escalations; occupancy risk | Embassy Office Parks, Mindspace Business Parks, Brookfield India |
| **Retail REIT** | Malls and retail centres | Minimum guaranteed rent + turnover-linked rent | Nexus Select Trust |
| **Transmission InvIT** | Power transmission lines, substations | **Availability-based** — paid for being available, not for volume carried | IndiGrid, PowerGrid InvIT |
| **Roads / highway InvIT** | Toll and annuity road concessions | **Toll = traffic risk**; **annuity/HAM = fixed payment, sovereign counterparty** | IRB InvIT, Cube Highways Trust, Bharat Highways |
| **Pipeline / energy InvIT** | Gas pipelines, energy assets | Contracted transportation or offtake tariffs | listed energy infrastructure trusts |
| **Logistics / warehousing** | Warehouses and industrial parks | Lease rent, tenant-covenant driven | held within diversified REITs and InvITs |
| **SM REIT** | Small & Medium REIT — assets **₹50–500 crore**, each **scheme separately listed**, ₹10 lakh minimum investment | Scheme-level rent from a specific property or group | Property Share Investment Trust (PropShare Platina), BSE, Dec 2024 — India's first |
| **Private-placement InvIT** | Any of the above, unlisted units | Same economics, far thinner disclosure and no live price | — |

**A single trust routinely spans several of these.** The corpus shows one REIT holding Grade-A offices,
**hospitality** and **solar generation**; an InvIT holding transmission, **renewables** and **battery
storage (BESS)**; another holding retail centres alongside **ticketed attractions**. Do not assume a
trust is single-asset because its name suggests one — read the segment disclosure. Trusts are also
**reclassified** as their mix shifts, so a prior period's category is not authoritative.

**Classify before analysing.** The category determines whether the trust carries **volume risk at all** —
the distinction that separates a near-bond from a cyclical.

## 2. How these vehicles make money

The chain is short and worth stating in full, because every metric below sits on one of its links:

**asset-level revenue → operating cost → NOI → interest and trust expenses → NDCF → distribution to
unitholders.**

Regulation requires the large majority of net distributable cash flow to be distributed, so **retained
earnings are structurally small**. There is no compounding-through-reinvestment story. Growth comes from
two places only:

- **Organic** — contractual rent escalations, mark-to-market on renewals, lease-up of vacant area,
  tariff revisions, traffic growth on toll assets. This is same-store growth.
- **Inorganic** — acquiring assets, funded by issuing units or raising debt. **Unit issuance dilutes
  per-unit distribution unless the asset yields above the trust's cost of capital**, which is the central
  test of whether an acquisition helped unitholders or merely grew the trust.

**Availability versus volume is the defining split.** A transmission InvIT is paid for keeping lines
available, largely irrespective of electricity flowing through them — revenue is contracted and highly
predictable, so it behaves like a long-duration bond with an equity wrapper. A toll-road InvIT is paid
per vehicle and carries genuine traffic risk. An office REIT sits between: rent is contracted, but
expiries reprice and vacancy is real. **Ranking these three on the same growth expectation is a
category error.**

Because distributions are largely contracted and leverage is capped, **the unit price trades off yield**
— it is rate-sensitive in a way operating companies are not. Rising bond yields compress unit prices
mechanically, with nothing wrong at the asset level.

## 3. Metric definitions

### Core to every category

- **DPU (Distribution Per Unit)** — the headline. What a unitholder actually receives per unit, per
  period. Track the **trailing twelve-month** figure; single-period DPU is lumpy.
- **Distribution yield (%)** = annualised DPU ÷ unit price. The comparison metric — against the 10-year
  G-sec, against peers, and against the trust's own history.
- **NDCF (Net Distributable Cash Flow)** — cash available for distribution after operating costs,
  interest and reserves. **The coverage denominator.**
- **Distribution coverage** = NDCF ÷ distribution paid. **Below 1× means the trust is distributing more
  than it generated** — funded by debt, reserves or capital return. State it plainly when it occurs.
- **Distribution composition by tax bucket** — interest, dividend, rental income, and **capital return
  (amortisation of SPV debt)**. Each is taxed differently in the unitholder's hands, and a distribution
  heavy in capital return is **partly a return of the investor's own money**, not income. Never quote a
  yield without noting its composition.
- **NAV per unit**, and **premium / discount to NAV** — the market's view of the portfolio versus the
  valuer's.
- **AUM / Gross Asset Value (GAV)** — the portfolio's total asset base, and the denominator for LTV.
- **LTV (loan-to-value)** = net debt ÷ GAV. The regulatory constraint and the acquisition headroom.
- **Cost of debt**, **average maturity**, and the **fixed/floating split** — a floating-rate book
  transmits rate moves straight into NDCF and therefore into DPU.
- **Weighted average cost of capital vs incremental asset yield** — the acquisition accretion test.
- **Credit rating** (ICRA / CRISIL / CARE) and outlook — displayed as a headline item in the corpus, not
  buried. It is the market's independent read on refinancing and coverage risk; state agency and date.
- **Sponsor type and standing** — **sovereign or government-backed**, institutional, or private
  developer. This materially changes perceived counterparty and support risk, and the corpus surfaces it
  as a headline attribute. State it plainly; do not treat it as a guarantee.
- **Weighted average asset life / average concession period** — the portfolio-level residual life.

### Office, retail and hospitality REITs

**Self-contained by design.** Everything needed to read a property portfolio is defined here, so a trust
never requires the developer file to be loaded alongside it.

- **NOI (Net Operating Income)** — property revenue less property operating expense. **The headline
  operating metric**, and the input to NDCF. Track absolute NOI and its growth; the corpus leads with it.
- **Occupancy (%)** — state the basis. **Occupancy by value and occupancy by area differ**, sometimes
  materially, because premium space is priced higher; quoting one as though it were the other overstates
  or understates lease-up. Report committed versus in-place, and like-for-like where disclosed.
- **WALE (weighted average lease expiry)** — longer is more secure; read alongside the expiry schedule,
  since a long WALE with a cliff in one year is not secure
- **Mark-to-market gap** — in-place rent versus current market rent on expiring leases; the organic
  growth engine
- **Re-leasing spread** and **releasing / retention rate**
- **Same-store NOI growth** — the honest organic number, excluding acquisitions
- **Turnover rent share** (retail) and **tenant sales per sq ft**
- **Tenant diversification** — top-10 tenant share of rent, and sector concentration of the tenant base.
  A high-occupancy portfolio resting on a few tenants in one industry is not as secure as its occupancy
  suggests. **Read alongside WALE, never instead of it.**
- **Total leasable area (msf)**, and the completed versus under-development split
- **Rental rate per sq ft** and the **escalation clause** — typically a fixed step-up at a set interval;
  state the mechanism, since it is the contracted portion of organic growth
- **Hospitality assets within a trust** — occupancy, ADR and RevPAR apply to that segment only, and are
  **far more cyclical** than office rent. Never blend them into a portfolio-wide occupancy figure.

### Transmission and pipeline InvITs

- **Availability (%)** — the payment trigger. Contracted revenue is earned on availability against a
  threshold; **this is not PLF and must not be labelled as such**
- **Contracted revenue share** versus merchant or variable exposure
- **Residual contract / concession life** — a portfolio averaging eight years is a fundamentally
  different asset from one averaging thirty, at the same yield
- **Counterparty mix** and receivable days — who pays, and how promptly

### Road InvITs

- **Toll revenue**, **traffic volume (PCU)**, and **realisation per vehicle**
- **Toll rate revision basis** — typically inflation-linked; state the mechanism
- **Annuity / HAM share** versus toll share — annuity carries no traffic risk
- **Residual concession life** and the **major-maintenance (overlay) cycle** — a lumpy, contractually
  required cash outflow that depresses NDCF in specific periods and must not be read as deterioration

## 4. Benchmarks — what good looks like

Indicative. Verify against the trust's own history, its category, and the prevailing G-sec.

| Category | Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|---|
| All | Distribution coverage (NDCF ÷ distribution) | **≥1.0×** — sustained below 1× is the central warning |
| All | Distribution yield | 6–8% typical; **judge as a spread over the 10-year G-sec**, not absolute |
| All | LTV | <30% comfortable; regulatory cap applies and tightens above a threshold |
| All | DPU trajectory | **steady and predictable beats fast** — this is an income vehicle |
| All | Capital-return share of distribution | low and disclosed; a high share means yield overstates income |
| Office REIT | Occupancy | >90%; committed occupancy above in-place signals lease-up ahead |
| Office REIT | WALE | >5 years, with no single-year expiry cliff |
| Office REIT | Mark-to-market gap | positive — in-place below market means embedded organic growth |
| Office / retail REIT | Top-10 tenant share of rent | lower is safer; high concentration qualifies a strong occupancy number |
| All | Credit rating | investment grade with a stable outlook; a change of outlook is an early signal |
| Retail REIT | Tenant sales growth | above retail-sector growth; turnover rent following it |
| Transmission InvIT | Availability | at or above the contractual threshold — consistently; **shortfall is a direct revenue penalty** |
| Transmission InvIT | Contracted revenue share | high; merchant exposure is the exception and should be stated |
| Road InvIT | Traffic growth | positive and above the toll-revision drag |
| Road InvIT | Annuity share | higher annuity share = lower risk, lower upside; state the mix |

**What to watch, by category:**

- **All** — Is the distribution covered by NDCF, or funded? What is the **tax composition** of that
  distribution? Is LTV within cap, and is there acquisition headroom? Is the debt fixed or floating, and
  when does it reprice? Were acquisitions **accretive to DPU**, or merely additive to AUM?
- **Office / retail REIT** — Is occupancy holding and is committed above in-place? What is the
  mark-to-market gap on the next expiries? Is same-store NOI growing, or is all growth acquired?
- **Transmission / pipeline InvIT** — Is availability meeting the threshold? How much residual contract
  life remains? Are receivables from counterparties ageing?
- **Road InvIT** — Is traffic growing? What share is annuity versus toll? **When is the next major
  maintenance cycle**, and is it reserved for?

## 5. Regulatory quick reference

| Area | Body / rule |
|---|---|
| The vehicles | **SEBI (REIT) Regulations** and **SEBI (InvIT) Regulations** — the governing framework |
| Distribution | Mandatory distribution of the large majority of NDCF, at a minimum frequency. **Verify the current percentage and frequency at source** — both have been amended |
| Leverage | LTV cap on consolidated borrowings, with a **lower threshold above which credit rating and unitholder approval are required**. Verify current thresholds |
| Asset composition | Minimum share in completed, revenue-generating assets; caps on under-construction exposure |
| Valuation | Independent valuer, at a mandated frequency; NAV is a valuer's number, not a market price |
| **Sponsor obligations** | **Minimum sponsor unit holding, with a lock-in.** A sponsor reducing its stake is flagged in the corpus as a **key investor risk** — check current holding against the requirement and against its own history |
| **SM REITs** | A separate SEBI framework: assets **₹50–500 crore**, **each scheme separately listed**, ₹10 lakh minimum investment, sponsor holds a minimum share for a lock-in period. **Analyse per scheme, not per trust** — schemes do not cross-subsidise |
| Related-party acquisitions | Unitholder approval and independent valuation requirements — the main governance pressure point, since sponsors commonly sell assets into their own trust |
| Taxation | **Distribution is taxed by component** in the unitholder's hands — interest, dividend, rental, capital return. State the split; it materially changes post-tax yield |
| Underlying assets | The asset's own regulator still applies — **CERC** for transmission, **NHAI** concession terms for roads, **RERA** where relevant to the underlying property |

Regulatory detail here changes more often than in most sectors. **Treat every threshold as requiring
confirmation at source for the period being analysed** — see section 7.

## 6. Per-mode specifics

### Headline KPIs by category (`dashboard`)

| Category | KPI strip |
|---|---|
| Office / retail REIT | NOI, DPU, distribution yield, occupancy, WALE, same-store NOI growth, LTV |
| Transmission / pipeline InvIT | Revenue, EBITDA, DPU, distribution yield, availability, residual contract life, LTV |
| Road InvIT | Toll revenue, traffic (PCU), DPU, distribution yield, annuity share, residual concession life, LTV |

**Never head the strip with PAT.** Reported profit is depressed by depreciation and amortisation on
acquired assets and says little about distribution capacity. NDCF and DPU are the honest headline.

### Table columns by category (`financials`)

Revenue · NOI or EBITDA · Interest cost · **NDCF** · **Distribution declared** · **Coverage (×)** ·
**DPU** · LTV · NAV per unit. Add occupancy and WALE for REITs; availability for transmission; traffic
and annuity share for roads.

Show the **distribution's tax composition** as its own row or note wherever a distribution figure
appears.

### Chart reference lines (`charts`)

- **DPU trend** — the primary chart. Plot per period with a **trailing-twelve-month line over it**, since
  single-period DPU is lumpy by design.
- **Distribution yield** — dashed reference line at the **10-year G-sec**, so the spread is visible.
  A yield chart without the risk-free line invites the wrong conclusion.
- **Coverage** — dashed line at **1.0×**, the solvency-of-the-distribution threshold.
- **LTV** — dashed lines at the **regulatory cap** and at the **lower approval threshold**.
- **Occupancy** — dashed line at 90% (REITs). **Availability** — dashed line at the contractual
  threshold (transmission).
- Never plot EBITDA margin across categories on one axis; the cost structures are not comparable.

### Profile coverage by category (`business-profile`)

Sponsor and manager, and the **degree of sponsor alignment** (unit holding, skin in the game). Asset
list with location, size and vintage. Lease or concession structure. **Governance around related-party
acquisitions.** Debt profile and maturity ladder. Distribution policy and track record. For InvITs,
whether listed or privately placed, and what that means for disclosure and liquidity.

### Moat candidates by category (`moats`)

Moats here are **contractual and positional**, never brand or technology:

- **Concession or contract exclusivity** — a transmission corridor or tolling right no one can duplicate
- **Residual life** — long contracted life is itself the moat; a short one is a liability
- **Irreplaceable location** — a Grade-A park in a supply-constrained micro-market
- **Sponsor pipeline** — a right of first offer over a credible sponsor's assets, *provided* governance
  on related-party pricing is sound
- **Cost of capital** — a lower one converts directly into accretive acquisition capacity

**Not moats**: scale of AUM by itself, or a high current yield — a high yield is frequently the market
pricing risk, not a strength.

### Valuation (`valuation`)

**Never P/E, and never EV/EBITDA as the primary lens.** Depreciation on revalued assets makes reported
earnings a poor guide, and these are compared against income alternatives, not against operating
companies.

Use, in order: **distribution yield and its spread over the 10-year G-sec** · **premium/discount to NAV**
· **yield versus peer trusts of comparable residual life and risk** · **implied cap rate** (NOI ÷ GAV)
for REITs.

State residual contract or concession life alongside any yield. **Two trusts on an identical yield are
not equivalently priced if one has eight years of contracted life and the other thirty** — this is the
most common misreading in the category.

### CB Rating substitutions (`cb-rating`)

An income vehicle cannot be scored on the standard growth-and-margin frame. Substituted throughout:

| Component | Weight | REIT/InvIT substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution Durability | 30% | **NDCF coverage of distribution** and its trend; the tax composition of what is paid; whether distributions are funded from operations rather than from debt or reserves |
| DPU Growth Quality | 20% | DPU growth on a trailing-twelve-month basis, and **whether it is organic** (same-store NOI, escalations, traffic, lease-up) or acquisition-driven and dilutive |
| Asset Quality & Residual Life | 20% | Occupancy, WALE and mark-to-market gap (REIT); availability and contracted share (transmission); traffic and annuity mix (roads). **Residual contract or concession life throughout** |
| Balance Sheet | 20% | LTV against cap, cost and maturity of debt, fixed/floating split, refinancing wall, acquisition headroom |
| Governance & Sponsor | 10% | Related-party acquisition record and pricing, sponsor unit holding and alignment, disclosure quality, manager track record |

Total 100%. **Revenue Growth, EBITDA margin and PAT Quality do not appear** — all three are misleading
for a pass-through trust, and Debt/Leverage is replaced by LTV because gross debt without asset value is
meaningless here. Any rendered score must state that it used the REIT/InvIT parameter set.

### Extra sections (`quarterly-report`)

- **Distribution & Coverage** — DPU, NDCF, coverage, and the tax composition. Effectively mandatory.
- **Asset-Level Performance** — per-asset or per-SPV operating detail, since portfolio averages conceal
  a weak asset
- **Debt Profile & Refinancing** — maturity ladder, cost, fixed/floating
- **Acquisitions & Pipeline** — what was bought, at what yield, funded how, and **whether it was
  DPU-accretive**
- **Unit Price & Yield vs G-sec** — the rate-sensitivity context
- **REIT vs InvIT comparison** — where a report covers both, the corpus gives the contrast its own
  section: what each holds, which regulations govern it, the risk profile, and the tax treatment. It is
  the single most useful explainer for a reader new to the class.

### Event transmission map (`event-impact`, `risks-outlook`)

| Event | Hits | Exposure basis to cite |
|---|---|---|
| Policy rate / G-sec yield move | Unit price via required yield; NDCF via floating debt | Floating-rate share of borrowings; current yield spread over G-sec |
| SEBI regulation amendment (leverage, distribution, composition) | Acquisition headroom, distribution policy | Current LTV against cap; headroom to the approval threshold |
| Taxation change on trust distributions | **Post-tax yield to unitholders**, with no change at asset level | Distribution split by component — interest / dividend / rental / capital return |
| Sponsor asset sale into the trust | DPU accretion or dilution; governance | Acquisition yield versus trust cost of capital; related-party approval process |
| Unit issuance (QIP / preferential) | Per-unit distribution | Units issued as % of base; incremental asset yield versus dilution |
| Office demand shift (hybrid work, GCC expansion) | Occupancy, mark-to-market, re-leasing spread | Expiry schedule by year; micro-market supply |
| Transmission tariff order (**CERC**) | Contracted revenue on affected assets | Share of portfolio revenue from the assets covered |
| Toll rate revision / traffic disruption | Toll revenue | Toll share versus annuity share; traffic on the affected stretch |
| Counterparty stress | Receivables, NDCF timing | Counterparty concentration and receivable days |
| Major maintenance cycle (roads) | NDCF in specific periods | Overlay schedule and reserve provision — **cyclical, not deterioration** |

## 7. Where to look (sourcing)

- **Trust filings first** — the quarterly and annual disclosures REITs and InvITs file with the
  exchanges, plus the **investor presentation** and the **NDCF statement**, which is the authoritative
  source for distribution and coverage.
- **The distribution announcement** — states DPU, record date, and **the component-wise tax split**.
  This is the only reliable source for composition; aggregators routinely omit it.
- **SEBI** — REIT and InvIT Regulations and their amendments, for current thresholds on distribution,
  leverage and asset composition. Do not rely on remembered thresholds.
- **CERC** for transmission tariff orders; **NHAI** for concession and toll-revision terms.
- **The independent valuer's report** for NAV and cap rates.
- **RBI** releases and the 10-year G-sec level for the yield-spread comparison.
- **Credit rating agency rationales** — unusually informative here, since they analyse coverage,
  refinancing and counterparty risk directly.

Search phrasing that surfaces Tier 1: `"<trust name>" NDCF statement`, `"<trust name>" distribution
record date tax component`, `"<trust name>" investor presentation`, `SEBI InvIT regulations leverage
amendment`.

**Cross-sector work** — **a REIT or InvIT does not belong in a cross-sector operating comparison at
all.** It has no meaningful EBITDA margin to rank, its P/E is distorted by depreciation on revalued
assets, and its growth is structurally capped by mandatory distribution. Ranking a distribution vehicle
against manufacturers on Revenue Growth and EBITDA% misrepresents both. If a request places one in such a
table, **say so and give it its own section** on yield, coverage, LTV and residual life. The only
defensible shared columns are Market Cap and Net Debt, and even those carry a caveat.

Comparisons **within** the class are valid and useful — but only against trusts of comparable category
and residual life.
