# Sector: Capital Markets (India)

Covers exchanges, depositories, registrars and transfer agents, brokers, asset managers, and investment
funds — the infrastructure and intermediaries of Indian financial markets.

**These are financial businesses but not lenders.** Unlike banks and NBFCs, most earn **fees on activity
or on assets**, not a spread on a loan book. They carry little or no credit risk, and their operating
leverage is unusually high: incremental volume or AUM costs almost nothing to serve, so revenue growth
drops through to profit at a rate rarely seen elsewhere.

## 1. Category taxonomy

| Category | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Exchange** | Matches trades; earns transaction charges on turnover | BSE, MCX |
| **Depository** | Holds securities in dematerialised form; earns on accounts, transactions and issuer fees | CDSL, NSDL |
| **RTA (Registrar & Transfer Agent)** | Recordkeeping for mutual funds and issuers; earns fees on AUM serviced | CAMS, KFintech |
| **Broker** | Executes trades for clients; brokerage plus float and margin-funding income | Angel One, Groww |
| **AMC (Asset Manager)** | Manages mutual fund assets; earns a management fee on AUM | HDFC AMC, ICICI Prudential AMC, SBI Funds Management, UTI AMC, Aditya Birla Sun Life AMC |
| **Fund / Scheme** | An investment vehicle, **not an operating company** — see §2 | Edelweiss Recently Listed IPO Fund |

The first five are operating companies analysed on revenue and margin. **A fund is not** — it has no
P&L, no EBITDA and no market cap, and forcing operating-company metrics onto it produces nonsense.

## 2. How companies in this sector make money

**Exchanges** earn transaction charges per unit of turnover. Revenue is therefore a function of market
activity, which is cyclical and outside management's control — the strategic question is share of
turnover and the ability to add new segments or products.

**Depositories** are closer to annuities: they earn on the number of demat accounts, on transactions,
and on fees charged to issuers. Account growth is structural rather than cyclical, which makes
depositories the steadiest business in this file. **They are duopolies**, which is the central fact about
them.

**RTAs** earn fees on the AUM they service, so they ride the mutual-fund growth story without taking
market risk on which fund manager wins.

**Brokers** earn brokerage, plus interest on client float and margin funding. Discount brokers compete on
price and monetise through volume and ancillary income; revenue is highly sensitive to retail activity
and to regulatory changes in derivative-segment rules.

**AMCs** earn a management fee expressed as a percentage of AUM. Two things matter: **how much AUM**, and
**what mix** — equity AUM earns several times the fee of debt or liquid AUM, so mix shift moves revenue
even at flat total AUM. Fee rates face persistent regulatory pressure through TER (Total Expense Ratio)
caps, which is the sector's structural headwind.

The common thread: **operating leverage**. Costs are largely fixed, so a rise in turnover, accounts or
AUM converts to profit at very high incremental margin — and falls do the reverse just as sharply.

### Analysing a fund (not an operating company)

A fund is a pool of investor money. Analyse it on portfolio and cost, never on revenue or profit:

- **AUM** and its trend — inflows versus performance-driven change
- **NAV** and returns against a stated benchmark and category peers, over multiple periods
- **Expense Ratio (TER)** — the annual cost to the investor; directly reduces returns
- **Portfolio turnover** — how actively the portfolio is traded; higher turnover means higher hidden costs
- **Active share** — how far the portfolio deviates from its benchmark. Low active share plus a high fee
  is the classic closet-indexing problem, and worth stating plainly
- **Top-10 concentration**, sector and market-cap allocation
- **Fund manager tenure** and mandate consistency

Never assign a fund a P/E, an EBITDA margin, or a CB Rating built for operating companies.

### The analogy

A tollbooth. It earns per transaction passing through, largely regardless of the value of what passes, so the business is volume and take rate — not the direction of the market.

## 3. Metric definitions

### Exchanges and depositories

- **ADTO (Average Daily Turnover)** — the volume driver. Split by segment (cash, derivatives, commodity),
  as economics differ sharply between them.
- **Transaction charges / take rate** — revenue per unit of turnover. Under regulatory scrutiny.
- **Market share of turnover** — the competitive metric.
- **Demat accounts (count and net additions)** — the depository growth engine; structural, not cyclical.
- **Issuer / corporate action income** — the annuity layer for depositories.
- **Revenue per account** and **opex per account** — where operating leverage shows.

### Brokers

- **Active clients** — the definition varies by disclosure (NSE-active is the common standard); state
  which is used.
- **Client acquisition cost** and **ARPU per active client**.
- **Market share** of NSE active clients and of turnover.
- **Float income and margin trading facility (MTF) book** — increasingly material, and interest-rate
  sensitive.

### AMCs

- **QAAUM (Quarterly Average AUM)** — the correct revenue base. **Closing AUM flatters or understates
  depending on where markets moved in the final week**, which is why average AUM is the standard.
- **Equity AUM share** — the mix metric that drives blended yield.
- **Yield / revenue as bps of AUM** — the realised fee rate. Falling yield with rising AUM is the
  sector's recurring pattern.
- **SIP flows / SIP AUM / SIP book** — the sticky, recurring inflow that has structurally changed Indian
  asset management. Rising SIP share reduces the volatility of AUM.
- **Folio count** — investor accounts; the retail penetration measure.
- **Opex / QAAUM (bps)** — the cost structure, and where scale advantage appears.
- **Net sales / net equity flows** — inflows minus redemptions; distinguishes genuine share gain from
  market appreciation.

- **RoNW (Return on Net Worth)** — net profit ÷ net worth. Quoted in IPO offer documents and
  required in prospectus disclosure, so it appears whenever a company in this sector lists. Report
  it alongside the sector's usual return measure rather than in place of it.

## 4. Benchmarks — what good looks like

Indicative; verify against the company's own history and current regulation.

| Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|
| AMC equity AUM share | >45% — drives blended yield |
| AMC revenue yield | 45–55 bps blended; equity schemes materially higher |
| AMC opex / QAAUM | falling with scale; <25 bps is efficient |
| AMC EBITDA margin | 55–70% — genuinely high, reflecting operating leverage |
| SIP share of equity AUM | rising; higher share means stickier AUM |
| Depository EBITDA margin | 55–65% |
| Exchange EBITDA margin | 50–65% |
| Broker EBITDA margin | 40–55% for discount models |
| Demat account growth | double-digit; a structural rather than cyclical driver |

**What to watch, by category:**

- **Exchange** — Is turnover share holding? Any regulatory change to transaction charges or derivative
  lot sizes? Is a new product segment scaling?
- **Depository** — Is account growth sustaining? Is issuer income growing? Is the duopoly structure
  intact?
- **AMC** — Is equity AUM share rising? Is yield holding against TER pressure? Are SIP flows growing? Is
  opex/QAAUM falling? **Is net equity sales positive** — that separates share gain from market lift.
- **Broker** — Are active clients growing? What happened to derivative volumes after the last regulatory
  change? How material is float and MTF income?

### What goes wrong, and the tell

**Volume dependence, and a regulator that can reprice the toll.** The tells: turnover falling faster than the market, which means share loss rather than a soft market; a regulatory consultation on fees or lot sizes; over-concentration in one segment; and for asset managers, net outflows masked by a rising market lifting closing AUM.

## 5. Regulatory quick reference

| Area | Body / rule |
|---|---|
| Securities markets, exchanges, depositories, brokers, AMCs | **SEBI** |
| Commodity derivatives | SEBI (merged from FMC) |
| Mutual fund expense caps | **TER slabs** under SEBI MF Regulations — the structural fee headwind |
| Broker margin, derivative segment rules | SEBI circulars — lot sizes, position limits, upfront margin |
| Investor protection, KYC | SEBI, and industry bodies AMFI (mutual funds) |
| Industry data | **AMFI** for AUM and flows; exchanges publish turnover |

**SEBI regulatory change is the dominant event risk in this sector.** A single circular on derivative lot
sizes or expense ratios can reset revenue for an entire category. Track current rules rather than
carrying a figure forward.

## 6. Per-mode specifics

### Headline KPIs by category (`dashboard`)

| Category | KPIs |
|---|---|
| Exchange | Revenue, ADTO, market share, transaction charges, EBITDA margin, PAT |
| Depository | Revenue, demat accounts, net additions, issuer income, EBITDA margin, PAT |
| RTA | Revenue, AUM serviced, EBITDA margin, PAT, client count |
| Broker | Revenue, active clients, market share, ARPU, EBITDA margin, PAT |
| AMC | Revenue, QAAUM, equity AUM share, yield (bps), SIP flows, opex/QAAUM, EBITDA margin, PAT |
| Fund | AUM, NAV, returns vs benchmark, TER, active share, portfolio turnover — **no P&L metrics** |

### Table columns by category (`financials`)

- **Exchange / Depository / RTA / Broker** — Period, Revenue, YoY, EBITDA, EBITDA%, PAT, YoY, plus the
  category's volume driver (ADTO, accounts, AUM serviced, active clients)
- **AMC** — Period, QAAUM, YoY, Equity AUM share, Revenue, Yield (bps), Opex/QAAUM, EBITDA, EBITDA%, PAT
- **All** — an **Expense & Employee Metrics** table (recurs in existing reports): opex breakdown,
  employee cost, headcount, revenue per employee. Operating leverage is the sector's story and this is
  where it is visible.
- **Fund** — a portfolio table (top holdings, sector allocation, market-cap split) and a returns table
  against benchmark. Not a P&L.

### Chart reference lines (`charts`)

| Metric | Line | Label |
|---|---|---|
| AMC revenue yield | 50 bps | "Typical blended yield" — industry norm |
| AMC opex / QAAUM | 25 bps | "Efficient cost structure" |
| Equity AUM share | 45% | "Mix threshold for yield" |
| EBITDA margin | **use the category's own band** | AMC 55–70%, depository 55–65%, exchange 50–65%, broker 40–55% |

All industry norms, not regulatory thresholds. For funds, plot NAV returns against the benchmark rather
than in isolation.

**Do not draw one EBITDA-margin line across these categories.** The bands span 40% to 70%, so a single
"sector norm" line makes a discount broker at 45% look weak when it is performing normally, and flatters
an AMC at 56% when it is at the bottom of its own band. Draw the relevant category band, or chart the
categories separately.

### Profile coverage by category (`business-profile`)

- **Exchange** — segments operated, turnover share by segment, product pipeline, technology, ownership.
- **Depository** — account base, DP network, issuer relationships, the duopoly position, revenue split
  between transaction and annuity income.
- **AMC** — scheme portfolio and AUM by category, distribution mix (direct vs distributor vs bank
  channel), sponsor/parent, fund manager stability, SIP book, market share trend.
- **Broker** — pricing model (discount vs full-service), client acquisition engine, product breadth,
  technology platform, ancillary revenue lines.
- **Fund** — mandate, strategy, manager, benchmark, and what the fund is actually trying to do.

### Moat candidates by category (`moats`)

- **Depository** — a **regulated duopoly** with network effects. CDSL and NSDL are the strongest
  structural positions in this file: a competitor cannot simply enter, and every DP, issuer and investor
  is already connected.
- **Exchange** — **liquidity begets liquidity**. Order flow concentrates where order flow already is,
  which is why exchange market share is stable for long periods and then moves suddenly.
- **RTA** — high switching costs; changing registrar is operationally painful for a fund house.
- **AMC** — brand and distribution reach, particularly bank-affiliated distribution; fund performance
  track record; SIP book stickiness. But **be sceptical of past performance as a moat** — it does not
  persist reliably, and the durable advantages here are distribution and brand, not returns.
- **Broker** — scale-driven cost advantage in a price-competitive market; the platform itself is
  replicable and should not be claimed as a moat without cost evidence.

### Valuation (`valuation`)

Primary multiple: **P/E**. These are asset-light, high-margin, cash-generative businesses where earnings
are the right basis. **AMCs are additionally valued on market cap as a percentage of AUM** — the standard
cross-check, and more comparable across managers than P/E alone.

Do not use P/BV — book value is small and says little about a fee-earning franchise. Do not use
EV/EBITDA where net cash is large, without stating the cash position.

**Funds are not valued on a multiple at all.** They are assessed on returns net of TER against benchmark
and peers.

### CB Rating substitutions (`cb-rating`)

Financial sector: **Debt/Leverage is never a component.** These businesses are typically net cash.

| Component | Weight | Capital-markets substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 30% | The category's volume driver — QAAUM growth for AMCs, demat account growth for depositories, ADTO for exchanges, active clients for brokers |
| Profitability | 25% | EBITDA margin against the sector's high norm, plus **yield or take rate direction** — a rising AUM on a falling yield is weaker than it looks |
| PAT Quality | 25% | PAT growth adjusted for one-offs and treasury/other income, which can be material for net-cash companies |
| Regulatory & Outlook | 20% | **SEBI regulatory exposure** — TER pressure for AMCs, transaction-charge and derivative rules for exchanges and brokers — replaces generic Forward Outlook, because a single circular can reset a category's revenue |

Total 100%. **Do not score a fund on this framework at all.**

### Extra sections (`quarterly-report`)

- **Expense & Employee Metrics** — recurs in existing reports; where operating leverage is visible.
- **Revenue Segments** — the split by income line, which differs sharply by category.
- **AUM & Flows** — for AMCs: QAAUM, mix, net sales, SIP book.
- **Regulatory Watch** — pending or recent SEBI changes and their revenue implication.
- **Fund Portfolio** — for funds only: holdings, allocation, returns versus benchmark.

### Event transmission map (`event-impact`, `risks-outlook`)

| Event | Reaches results via | Exposure basis to cite |
|---|---|---|
| SEBI TER slab revision | AMC fee yield | equity AUM share, current blended yield |
| Derivative lot-size or position-limit change | exchange and broker turnover | derivative share of turnover/revenue |
| Transaction-charge revision | exchange revenue per unit | take rate, turnover |
| STT or transaction-tax change | trading volumes | retail turnover share |
| Market correction | AUM, turnover, and revenue simultaneously | equity AUM share, cyclical revenue share |
| SIP flow slowdown | AMC AUM stability | SIP share of equity AUM |
| New exchange or depository entrant | market share | current share, network position |
| Demat account growth policy (KYC easing) | depository account additions | account base |
| Interest-rate change | broker float income, debt fund flows | float balance, debt AUM share |

## 7. Where to look (sourcing)

**Tier 1** — quarterly investor presentations. AMCs disclose QAAUM, category mix, yield, SIP book and
opex/QAAUM there; exchanges and depositories disclose segment turnover and account additions. These do
not usually appear in the press release.

**Tier 2 — sector authorities**: **SEBI** (regulations, circulars, and the bulletins carrying market
data), **AMFI** — the authoritative source for industry AUM, category-wise flows and SIP data, and
therefore the right denominator for any AMC market-share claim — and the **exchanges themselves** for
turnover and active-client data. For funds, the scheme's own **factsheet and SID** are Tier 1 for
portfolio, TER and returns.

An AMC's AUM growth means little without AMFI industry growth alongside it: growing 12% while the
industry grew 18% is losing share, and the headline will not say so.

## 10. Mode applicability

All 15 modes apply to the operating companies. Notes:

- **`segments`** — revenue by income line (transaction / annuity / issuer / float) for infrastructure
  names; AUM by category for AMCs. Both well disclosed.
- **`valuation`** — P/E primary; **market cap as % of AUM** is the standard AMC cross-check. Not P/BV.
- **`cb-rating`** — Regulatory & Outlook replaces Forward Outlook, since SEBI circulars reset category
  economics. **Never applied to a fund.**
- **`moats`** — the depository duopoly and exchange liquidity network effects are among the strongest
  moat structures in this repo; fund performance is among the weakest. Say so.
- **Funds** — `dashboard`, `charts`, `school` and `risks-outlook` apply in adapted form; `financials`,
  `valuation`, `cb-rating` and `moats` **do not** and should be skipped rather than forced.
- **Cross-sector work** — Revenue, EBITDA%, PAT, Mkt Cap and P/E are all meaningful, so operating
  companies here participate fully. **Net Debt is usually meaningless** — these businesses are typically
  net cash, so a Net Debt ranking will misrepresent them; mark it "n/a — not comparable for this sector"
  unless the company genuinely carries debt. **EV/EBITDA is distorted by large net cash** and should be
  used only with the cash position stated. AUM, ADTO, take rate and demat accounts are sector-specific
  and stay out of cross-sector tables. **A fund never appears in a cross-sector table at all.**
