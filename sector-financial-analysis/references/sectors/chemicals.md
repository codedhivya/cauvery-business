# Sector: Chemicals (India)

Covers specialty chemicals, commodity chemicals, fertilisers, plastic pipes, and decorative paints.

**These sub-sectors have almost nothing in common except chemistry.** A specialty chemicals company
behaves like pharma — customer-embedded, qualification-gated, 18–25% margins. A commodity chemicals
company behaves like metals — cyclical, spread-driven, 8–14%. A fertiliser company is a
subsidy-administered business whose realisation is set by government. **Classify first; the categories
are not comparable on margin, growth or valuation.**

## 1. Category taxonomy

| Category | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Specialty chemicals** | Custom or differentiated molecules sold into defined applications; qualification-gated, customer-embedded | Vinati Organics, Aarti Industries, Balaji Amines, Alkyl Amines, Galaxy Surfactants, SRF, Deepak Nitrite, Acutaas Chemicals |
| **Commodity chemicals** | Standardised products priced off feedstock spreads; cyclical | bulk chemical producers, and the commodity portion of diversified players |
| **Fertilisers** | Urea, NPK, complexes. **Realisation is administered, not market-set**; subsidy is a receivable, not revenue quality | urea and complex fertiliser producers |
| **Plastic pipes & fittings** | PVC/CPVC pipes for plumbing and agriculture; a building-materials business exposed to PVC resin cost | Finolex Industries, Supreme Industries, Astral |
| **Recycling / circular materials** | Recovers plastic, aluminium, battery and metal scrap into saleable feedstock. **A spread business with a regulatory demand driver**, not a chemistry business | listed plastic and metal recyclers |
| **Decorative paints** | Branded consumer product with distribution moat; a consumer business in chemical clothing | Asian Paints, Berger |

**Two categories are arguably not "chemicals" in behaviour.** Plastic pipes are a building-materials
business tracking construction demand and PVC resin cost. Decorative paints are a branded consumer
business — dealer network, brand, and crude-linked input costs. Both are placed here because the corpus
groups them so, but analyse them on their own terms.

## 2. How companies in this sector make money

**Specialty chemicals** win a molecule into a customer's product, get qualified, and then supply for
years. Qualification is slow and switching is costly for the customer, so revenue is sticky and margins
hold. Growth comes from new molecules, new customers, and the **China+1** shift as global buyers
diversify supply. The risk is concentration: a few molecules or a few customers often carry the book.

**Commodity chemicals** earn a **spread** between feedstock and product price, both set by global
markets. Nobody has pricing power; the levers are integration, scale and utilisation. Chinese supply is
the recurring shock — when Chinese capacity floods a product, the spread collapses regardless of how well
the Indian producer is run.

**Fertilisers** are a policy business. Urea realisation is fixed by government; the company earns a
regulated return and receives a **subsidy** that can be a very large receivable. Two things follow:
revenue quality depends on subsidy disbursement timing, and **working capital is the dominant financial
issue** — an unpaid subsidy is profit that has not become cash. Feedstock is gas for urea, and gas
pricing is itself administered.

**Plastic pipes** convert PVC or CPVC resin into pipes. Margin is the conversion spread plus brand
premium, and **inventory gain or loss on resin price movements can swing a quarter** — a falling resin
price hurts near-term margin on existing inventory even though it lowers future cost.

**Decorative paints** are a distribution and brand business. Crude derivatives are the input, dealer
reach is the moat, and volume growth versus value growth is the key read, exactly as in FMCG.

## 3. Metric definitions

### Common

- **Volume growth vs realisation** — the standard decomposition. In commodity and fertiliser businesses,
  revenue growth is mostly price; volume is the real signal.
- **Capacity and capacity utilisation (%)**.
- **Gross margin and EBITDA margin** — read against the category band, never the sector.
- **Net debt / EBITDA**, **operating cash conversion** — this sector is capital-intensive.

### Specialty

- **Customer and molecule concentration (%)** — top customer and top-5 molecule share. The primary risk.
- **Export share and geography mix** — China+1 exposure.
- **R&D spend**, new-molecule pipeline, **CRAMS/custom synthesis share** — the higher-value portion.
- **Value-added / differentiated share** of revenue.

### Commodity

- **Spread per tonne** — product price minus feedstock cost. The whole business.
- **Feedstock cost and integration** — backward integration into key inputs is the durable advantage.
- **Chinese capacity and price movement** — the external variable that most often decides the quarter.

### Fertilisers

- **Subsidy receivable and days outstanding** — **the critical working-capital metric.** A large
  receivable means reported profit is sitting with the government.
- **Urea vs NPK/complex volumes** — different economics and different subsidy regimes.
- **Energy efficiency vs norm** — urea returns are tied to gas consumption against a prescribed norm;
  beating the norm earns more.
- **Nutrient-based subsidy (NBS) rates** for non-urea products.
- **Imported vs domestic feedstock**, and gas pooling arrangements.

### Pipes and paints

- **Volume growth (tonnes for pipes)**, **realisation per tonne**.
- **Inventory gain/loss on resin** — separate it from operating margin, as with refiners.
- **Dealer network size and reach**, **distribution expansion** — the paints moat.
- **Value growth vs volume growth** — for paints, the FMCG-style decomposition.

### Recycling / circular materials

**This is a spread business whose demand is created by regulation.** It does not behave like the rest of
this file, and the specialty-versus-commodity margin framing does not apply.

- **Spread** = realisation on recycled output − scrap procurement cost. **The single metric**; volume
  without spread is activity, not profit.
- **Scrap procurement cost and availability** — a fragmented, informal supply chain, so **secured
  collection is the real constraint** and the main barrier to entry.
- **Recovery yield (%)** — output tonnes per tonne of scrap fed. Small yield differences move margin
  sharply at these spreads.
- **EPR (Extended Producer Responsibility) credits** — obligated producers must fund recycling of what
  they put into the market, so **EPR creates contracted demand and a tradeable credit price**. State EPR
  revenue separately: it is policy-derived income, and it moves with enforcement, not with the market.
- **Installed vs operating capacity, and utilisation** — capacity is easy to announce and hard to feed.
- **Stream mix** — plastic (PET, rigid), aluminium, lead-acid and lithium battery, and ferrous scrap
  each have different spreads, regulation and cyclicality. **Never blend them into one margin.**

**What to watch**: is the spread holding, or is scrap cost rising into a fixed output price? Is EPR
enforcement tightening or loosening — the demand switch? Is announced capacity actually being fed? How
much of revenue is EPR credits rather than material sales?

## 4. Benchmarks — what good looks like

**Per category — a single sector band would be meaningless here.**

| Category | EBITDA margin | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Specialty chemicals** | **18–25%** | Differentiated, qualification-gated; leaders exceed 25% |
| **Commodity chemicals** | **8–14%** | Spread-driven and cyclical; moves with feedstock |
| **Fertilisers** | **8–12%** | Regulated return; judge cash conversion, not margin |
| **Plastic pipes** | **12–16%** | Conversion spread plus brand; distorted by resin inventory swings |
| **Decorative paints** | **15–20%** | Consumer economics; brand and distribution driven |

| Other metric | Healthy |
|---|---|
| Capacity utilisation | >80% |
| Net debt / EBITDA | <2× |
| Customer concentration (specialty) | top client <25% of revenue |
| Subsidy receivable days (fertiliser) | falling; a rising trend is the warning |
| Export share (specialty) | 30–50% captures China+1 without over-dependence |
| R&D (specialty) | 2–4% of sales |

**What to watch, by category:**

- **Specialty** — Is the molecule and customer base broadening? Is export growth China+1 driven? Are new
  capacities commissioned and utilised? Is any single molecule carrying the growth?
- **Commodity** — Where is the spread, and what is Chinese supply doing? Is utilisation holding? Is
  backward integration progressing?
- **Fertilisers** — Are subsidy receivables rising or falling? Is energy efficiency beating the norm? Is
  the NPK mix growing relative to urea?
- **Pipes** — What did PVC resin do, and how much of the margin move is inventory rather than operations?
  Is agricultural or plumbing demand driving volume?
- **Paints** — Is volume growth carrying value growth? Is dealer network expanding? What is crude doing
  to input costs, and is pricing keeping pace?

## 5. Regulatory and policy quick reference

| Area | Body / issue |
|---|---|
| Fertiliser subsidy | **Department of Fertilizers** — NBS rates, urea pricing, subsidy disbursement |
| Gas pricing and allocation | PNGRB, Ministry of Petroleum — urea feedstock |
| Environmental clearance | MoEFCC, state pollution control boards — a real constraint on chemical capacity |
| Import protection | **DGTR** — anti-dumping duties on chemicals are frequent and materially move spreads |
| Manufacturing incentives | PLI schemes for specific chemical and agrochemical segments |
| Product standards | BIS quality control orders (QCOs) on chemicals and pipes — these restrict imports and have moved competitive dynamics |
| Industry data | Department of Chemicals & Petrochemicals; FAI for fertilisers |

**Anti-dumping rulings and QCOs are the sector's most consequential events** — both directly change who
can compete in a product and at what price.

## 6. Per-mode specifics

### Headline KPIs by category (`dashboard`)

| Category | KPIs |
|---|---|
| Specialty | Revenue, volume growth, EBITDA margin, PAT, export share, customer concentration, utilisation |
| Commodity | Revenue, volume, realisation, **spread**, EBITDA margin, PAT, utilisation |
| Fertilisers | Revenue, urea/NPK volumes, EBITDA margin, PAT, **subsidy receivable and days** |
| Pipes | Revenue, volume (t), realisation/t, EBITDA margin, **inventory gain/(loss)**, PAT |
| Paints | Revenue, volume growth, gross margin, EBITDA margin, PAT, dealer network |

### Table columns by category (`financials`)

- **Specialty / commodity** — Period, Revenue, YoY, Volume, Realisation, Gross margin, EBITDA, EBITDA%, PAT
- **Fertilisers** — add **Subsidy receivable, receivable days, operating cash flow**. A fertiliser P&L
  without the working-capital position is misleading.
- **Pipes** — add **inventory gain/(loss) on resin**, separated from operating margin, with a `.fnote`.
- **All** — a **Debt & Cash Flow** table; recurs as an explicit section in existing reports.

### Chart reference lines (`charts`)

| Metric | Line | Label |
|---|---|---|
| EBITDA margin | **the category's own band** | specialty 18–25%, commodity 8–14%, fertiliser 8–12%, pipes 12–16%, paints 15–20% |
| Capacity utilisation | 80% | "Efficient utilisation" |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 2.0× | "Comfortable leverage" |
| Subsidy receivable days | trend line | fertilisers — direction matters more than level |

**Never draw one EBITDA-margin line across these categories.** The bands span 8% to 25%; a single "sector
norm" would make a fertiliser company look broken and a specialty producer look exceptional when both are
performing normally. Chart categories separately or draw the relevant band.

### Profile coverage by category (`business-profile`)

- **Specialty** — molecule portfolio and applications, customer profile and concentration, qualification
  status, capacity by site, export markets, R&D and pipeline, CRAMS/custom-synthesis share.
- **Commodity** — product slate, feedstock sourcing and integration, capacity, cost position against
  global producers.
- **Fertilisers** — plant capacity and energy efficiency vs norm, product mix, gas sourcing, subsidy
  position, distribution reach.
- **Pipes** — product range (agri/plumbing/CPVC), capacity, dealer network, brand, resin sourcing.
- **Paints** — brand portfolio, dealer and tinting-machine network, product mix, capacity.

### Moat candidates by category (`moats`)

- **Specialty — customer qualification lock-in.** Once a molecule is approved into a customer's product,
  switching means requalification the customer rarely undertakes. **The strongest moat in this file**;
  cite relationship tenure and repeat-business share.
- **Specialty — process chemistry IP** and complex-chemistry capability that few can replicate.
- **Commodity — backward integration and scale.** The only durable advantages when price is set globally.
- **Fertilisers — energy efficiency against norm**, plant location relative to gas, and distribution
  reach. Genuinely modest moats; regulated returns cap the upside.
- **Pipes — brand and dealer network**, plus CPVC technology tie-ups.
- **Paints — the dealer and tinting network.** Among the strongest distribution moats in Indian industry;
  decades to build, and it decides which brand a painter recommends.
- **Be sceptical of "China+1 beneficiary" as a moat.** It is a tailwind available to every Indian
  producer, not a company advantage. What counts is which molecules were actually won, and from whom.

### Valuation (`valuation`)

Primary multiple: **P/E** for specialty, pipes and paints — these are branded or IP-led businesses where
earnings quality is reasonable. **EV/EBITDA** for commodity and fertilisers, where leverage and capital
intensity differ and earnings are cyclical or regulated.

**The categories do not share a multiple band.** Specialty chemicals and paints have historically traded
at large premiums to commodity chemicals, and comparing them on a single P/E table without the category
column misrepresents both. For fertilisers, note that a large subsidy receivable makes reported earnings
less cash-backed than the multiple implies.

### CB Rating substitutions (`cb-rating`)

| Component | Weight | Chemicals substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 30% | **Volume growth** primarily — revenue growth in commodity and fertiliser lines mostly reflects price and subsidy, not performance. For specialty, add new-customer and new-molecule wins |
| Profitability | 25% | EBITDA margin **against the category band** and its direction. Scoring across categories measures positioning, not performance |
| PAT Quality | 25% | PAT adjusted for one-offs; **inventory gain/loss for pipes**; **operating cash conversion for fertilisers**, where profit can sit uncollected in subsidy receivables |
| Forward Outlook | 20% | Capacity pipeline, customer wins, spread and feedstock outlook, anti-dumping/QCO position |

Total 100%.

### Extra sections (`quarterly-report`)

- **Debt & Cashflow** — recurs as an explicit section in existing reports.
- **Peer Comparison** — recurs in existing reports.
- **Raw Materials & Spreads** — feedstock costs, spread movement, pass-through.
- **Subsidy Position** — fertilisers only: receivable, days, disbursement.
- **Business Segments** — essential for diversified chemical companies spanning categories.

### Event transmission map (`event-impact`, `risks-outlook`)

| Event | Reaches results via | Exposure basis to cite |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-dumping duty imposed or lapsed | product pricing, import competition | that product's revenue share |
| BIS QCO on a chemical or pipe product | import restriction, domestic pricing power | affected product share |
| Chinese capacity addition or price cut | commodity spread collapse | that product's share, spread sensitivity |
| Crude / feedstock price move | input cost, spread | feedstock share of COGS, integration level |
| PVC resin price move | pipes margin, **inventory gain/loss** | inventory position, resin share of cost |
| NBS subsidy rate revision | fertiliser realisation and margin | NPK vs urea mix |
| Subsidy disbursement delay | working capital, cash flow | receivable days, borrowing cost |
| Gas price or allocation change | urea cost and regulated return | gas sourcing mix, energy efficiency vs norm |
| Monsoon | fertiliser and agri-pipe volumes | agri revenue share |
| China+1 order shift | specialty export growth | export share, customer additions |
| Environmental clearance or plant shutdown | volumes | affected capacity share |

## 7. Where to look (sourcing)

**Tier 1** — quarterly investor presentations. Specialty companies disclose segment and geography mix and
sometimes customer concentration; fertiliser companies disclose subsidy receivables and energy
efficiency; pipe companies disclose volumes and often the inventory effect. Earnings calls carry spread
and China-supply commentary that rarely appears in print.

**Tier 2 — sector authorities**: **DGTR** for anti-dumping and safeguard proceedings — publicly
searchable and often the single most consequential development for a commodity chemical; **Department of
Fertilizers** for subsidy rates and disbursement; **FAI (Fertiliser Association of India)** for industry
volumes; **Department of Chemicals & Petrochemicals** for production statistics; **BIS** for QCOs.

Feedstock and product prices should be cited with the benchmark named — "spreads improved" is not a
sourced claim.

## 10. Mode applicability

All 15 modes apply. Notes:

- **`segments`** — essential for diversified players spanning categories; a company with both specialty
  and commodity lines must be split, since the two are valued and judged differently.
- **`financials`** — the subsidy-receivable rows for fertilisers and the inventory gain/loss separation
  for pipes are mandatory, not optional.
- **`charts`** — **never one margin band across categories**; the sector spans 8% to 25%.
- **`valuation`** — P/E for specialty, pipes and paints; EV/EBITDA for commodity and fertilisers.
  Categories do not share a multiple band.
- **`cb-rating`** — growth on volume; margin against the **category** band; cash conversion weighted for
  fertilisers.
- **`moats`** — customer qualification lock-in (specialty) and dealer networks (paints) are real;
  "China+1 beneficiary" is a sector tailwind, not a company moat.
- **Cross-sector work** — all universal metrics apply cleanly and this sector participates fully. Spread
  per tonne, subsidy receivable days, customer concentration and inventory gain/loss are sector-specific
  and stay in their own section. **Note the category** in any cross-sector table, since a fertiliser
  company and a specialty producer will screen very differently for structural reasons.
