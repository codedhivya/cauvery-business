# Sector: Cement (India)

Covers integrated and grinding-only cement producers.

**Cement is a regional business masquerading as a national one.** It is heavy, low-value-per-tonne, and
uneconomic to transport far — so pricing, competition and profitability are set region by region, not
across India. Two companies with identical costs can earn very different margins purely because of where
their plants sit. **Establish the regional mix before interpreting any margin.**

## 1. Category taxonomy

| Category | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Integrated producer** | Owns limestone reserves, makes clinker and grinds it. Full control of the chain | UltraTech, Ambuja, ACC, Shree Cement, Ramco |
| **Grinding-only / split unit** | Buys or transfers clinker and grinds to cement near consuming markets. Lower capex, dependent on clinker access | split grinding units of the majors; newer entrants |

The distinction matters because **clinker is where the cost and the capital sit**. A grinding unit close
to demand saves freight but has no limestone security; an integrated plant controls cost but pays to move
product to market.

## 2. How companies in this sector make money

Revenue is **volume × realisation per tonne**, and both are regional. Costs break into four buckets that
between them explain almost all margin variance:

- **Power and fuel** — kilns are energy-intensive. **Pet coke** is the dominant fuel, so pet coke and
  coal prices drive cost directly. Captive power and waste-heat recovery systems (WHRS) reduce exposure.
- **Freight** — genuinely large for cement, often the second-biggest cost line. Governed by **lead
  distance**, the average distance from plant to market. A shorter lead distance is a permanent cost
  advantage, which is why plant location is strategy rather than logistics.
- **Raw material** — limestone, mostly captive; the mineral lease is the underlying asset.
- **Clinker factor** — the ratio of clinker to cement. Blended cements (PPC, PSC) use less clinker per
  tonne of cement, which lowers both cost and carbon intensity. A rising blended share improves margin.

**The industry's operating leverage is severe.** Costs are largely fixed, so utilisation and price move
profit sharply in both directions. A ₹10 per bag price change can move EBITDA per tonne materially, which
is why regional price tracking matters more here than in most sectors.

**EBITDA per tonne is the sector's currency.** Everyone — companies, analysts, this repo — compares on
it, because it strips out differences in realisation and shows the actual unit economics.

## 3. Metric definitions

- **Sales volume (million tonnes)** — the volume driver, disclosed quarterly and often monthly.
- **Realisation per tonne (₹/t)** — revenue ÷ volume. Regional; a national average conceals the story.
- **EBITDA per tonne (₹/t)** — **the headline metric of this sector.** Comparable across companies in a
  way margin percentage is not.
- **Capacity — clinker vs grinding (mtpa)** — state both. Grinding capacity without matching clinker
  means dependence on clinker purchase or transfer.
- **Capacity utilisation (%)** — the operating-leverage driver.
- **Lead distance (km)** — average plant-to-market distance. Lower is structurally cheaper.
- **Freight cost per tonne** — follows lead distance and diesel prices.
- **Power & fuel cost per tonne** — follows pet coke and coal; the largest cost swing factor.
- **Pet coke / coal consumption cost** — state the fuel mix.
- **Blended cement share (%)** — PPC/PSC as a share of volume; lowers clinker factor and cost.
- **WHRS and captive/green power share (%)** — structural cost reduction and carbon position.
- **Regional sales mix (%)** — North, South, East, West, Central. **The most important mix disclosure**,
  because regional prices diverge sharply and persistently.
- **Net debt and net debt / EBITDA** — capital intensity is high; expansion is debt-funded.

## 4. Benchmarks — what good looks like

Indicative and cycle-sensitive; cement prices move regionally and seasonally, so verify against the
current period.

| Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|
| EBITDA per tonne | ₹900–1,200/t is a reasonable mid-cycle band for efficient producers; leaders exceed it |
| EBITDA margin | 15–22% at mid-cycle |
| Capacity utilisation | >75%; >85% is strong |
| Lead distance | <400 km is favourable |
| Blended cement share | >60% |
| Green/WHRS power share | rising; >30% is a meaningful cost and carbon position |
| Net debt / EBITDA | <2× |
| Clinker-to-cement ratio | lower is better — reflects blending |

**What to watch:**

- Is **EBITDA per tonne** improving against the peer set for the same quarter?
- What is happening to **regional prices** in the company's core markets — and is its regional mix
  favourable or exposed?
- Is **pet coke cost** rising, and how much is offset by WHRS and green power?
- Is **utilisation** above 75%, and is new capacity being commissioned into a market that can absorb it?
- Is **lead distance** falling as split grinding units come up near demand?
- Is the **blended share** rising?
- **Seasonality**: monsoon quarters are structurally weak for construction demand. A weak Q2 (Jul–Sep) is
  normal, not a deterioration — compare year-on-year, never sequentially, without saying so.

## 5. Regulatory and policy quick reference

| Area | Body / issue |
|---|---|
| Limestone mining leases | Ministry of Mines; state governments — the underlying resource security |
| Environmental clearance | MoEFCC — required for capacity expansion; a real timeline constraint |
| Carbon and emissions | Perform-Achieve-Trade (PAT) scheme; carbon intensity increasingly material |
| Freight | Indian Railways freight rates and diesel prices drive the second-largest cost |
| Demand drivers | government infrastructure capex, housing schemes, rural demand |
| Competition | **CCI** — the sector has a history of cartelisation proceedings; a live regulatory risk |
| Industry data | **CMA (Cement Manufacturers' Association)**; DPIIT production data |

## 6. Per-mode specifics

### Headline KPIs by category (`dashboard`)

| Category | KPIs |
|---|---|
| Integrated producer | Revenue, sales volume, realisation/t, **EBITDA/t**, EBITDA margin, PAT, utilisation, net debt |
| Grinding-only | Revenue, sales volume, realisation/t, EBITDA/t, clinker sourcing, utilisation, PAT |

### Table columns by category (`financials`)

- **All** — Period, Sales volume (mt), Revenue, YoY, Realisation/t, **EBITDA, EBITDA/t**, EBITDA%, PAT, YoY
- **Cost table** — power & fuel/t, freight/t, raw material/t, other/t. **This is the sector's most
  informative table**, because it shows where margin was won or lost.
- **Capacity table** — clinker and grinding capacity by plant and region, utilisation, expansion pipeline.
- **Regional mix table** — volume and realisation by region. Recurs in existing reports as
  **Geographic Mix / Geographic Sales Mix**.

### Chart reference lines (`charts`)

| Metric | Line | Label |
|---|---|---|
| EBITDA per tonne | peer median for the period | "Peer median" — no fixed standard; it moves with prices |
| Capacity utilisation | 75% | "Healthy utilisation" |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 2.0× | "Comfortable leverage" |
| Blended cement share | 60% | "Efficient clinker factor" |

**Chart EBITDA per tonne rather than EBITDA margin** for peer comparison, and label the period — both
move with regional pricing. When plotting quarters, mark monsoon quarters or compare year-on-year, since
sequential cement charts otherwise show the weather.

### Profile coverage by category (`business-profile`)

Cover: clinker and grinding capacity by plant, **regional presence and sales mix**, limestone reserve
position and lease tenure, fuel mix and WHRS/green power share, lead distance, brand and premium-product
share, expansion pipeline with commissioning timelines, and group structure where the company sits inside
a larger holding.

Existing reports carry **Business Segments** and **Geographic Mix** — both are core here rather than
optional.

### Moat candidates by category (`moats`)

- **Limestone reserves with long lease tenure** — the foundational moat. Reserves are finite,
  geographically fixed and awarded by the state; a competitor cannot conjure them.
- **Regional density and short lead distance** — clustering plants near demand creates a freight cost
  advantage that is structural, not operational.
- **Captive power and WHRS** — permanent cost reduction in the largest variable cost line.
- **Brand and dealer network** — cement is bought largely on trust by contractors and individual home
  builders; premium brands sustain a real price differential.
- **Scale in a region** — a dominant regional position influences local pricing in a way national share
  does not.
- **Be sceptical** of national capacity share as a moat. Cement competition is regional; being India's
  largest producer means little in a region where you have no plant.

### Valuation (`valuation`)

Primary multiple: **EV/EBITDA**, and **EV per tonne of capacity** — the sector's standard cross-check and
the basis on which acquisitions are actually priced. **P/E** is secondary and distorted by depreciation
and leverage differences.

State the cycle position. Cement earnings swing with regional pricing, so a trailing multiple at a price
peak understates risk. Replacement cost per tonne is the natural floor reference in a downturn.

### CB Rating substitutions (`cb-rating`)

Capital-intensive with explicit Debt & Cash Flow, following the `power-energy` and `metals` precedent.

| Component | Weight | Cement substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 25% | **Sales volume growth and capacity addition** — not revenue, which reflects regional prices the company does not set |
| Profitability | 25% | **EBITDA per tonne against the peer median**, plus direction — the price-neutral comparison |
| PAT Quality | 15% | PAT adjusted for one-offs; operating cash conversion |
| Debt & Cash Flow | 20% | Net debt/EBITDA, capex funding, cash conversion |
| Cost Position & Outlook | 15% | Lead distance, fuel mix and WHRS share, blended-cement share, regional price outlook |

Total 100%. As in metals, **growth is scored on volume rather than revenue**, because a revenue rise
driven by a regional price increase is not company performance.

### Extra sections (`quarterly-report`)

- **Cost per Tonne Breakdown** — power & fuel, freight, raw material. The heart of a cement quarter.
- **Geographic Mix** — regional volume and realisation. Recurs in existing reports.
- **Capacity & Expansion** — clinker vs grinding, utilisation, commissioning pipeline.
- **Debt & Cash Flow** — the capital-intensive standard section.
- **"Cement School"** — the `school` mode is presented under this name in the existing sector report;
  keep the rename.

### Event transmission map (`event-impact`, `risks-outlook`)

| Event | Reaches results via | Exposure basis to cite |
|---|---|---|
| Pet coke / coal price move | power & fuel cost per tonne | fuel mix, WHRS and green power share |
| Diesel price or rail freight revision | freight cost per tonne | lead distance, road vs rail mix |
| Regional cement price move | realisation | that region's share of volume |
| Government infrastructure capex announcement | demand volumes | exposure to infra vs housing demand |
| Monsoon (seasonal) | volumes in Q2 | normal seasonality — do not read as deterioration |
| Limestone lease auction or expiry | long-term cost and security | leases due for renewal |
| Environmental clearance delay | expansion timeline | capacity under construction |
| CCI proceeding | penalty, pricing conduct | historical exposure |
| New capacity commissioned regionally (any player) | regional pricing pressure | regional share and utilisation |

## 7. Where to look (sourcing)

**Tier 1** — quarterly investor presentations, which disclose volume, realisation per tonne, EBITDA per
tonne, the cost-per-tonne breakdown, regional mix, utilisation and the expansion pipeline. This sector
discloses unit economics unusually well, so the presentation carries almost everything needed.

**Tier 2 — sector authorities**: **CMA (Cement Manufacturers' Association)** for industry production and
despatch data — the right denominator for share claims; **DPIIT** for production statistics; **Ministry
of Mines** for limestone leases; **CCI** for competition proceedings. Regional price trends are tracked by
broker channel checks — attribute those to the house that published them rather than stating them as fact.

## 10. Mode applicability

All 15 modes apply. Notes:

- **`school`** — presented as **"Cement School"** in the existing sector report; keep that name.
- **`segments`** — **Geographic Mix is the essential cut**, not product mix. Regional prices diverge, so a
  national view hides the story.
- **`charts`** — EBITDA per tonne, not margin, for peer comparison. Mark or avoid monsoon-quarter
  sequential comparisons.
- **`valuation`** — EV/EBITDA primary, **EV per tonne of capacity** as the standard cross-check. State
  cycle position.
- **`cb-rating`** — five components; growth on **volume, not revenue**.
- **`moats`** — limestone reserves and regional density are real; national capacity share is not.
- **Cross-sector work** — all universal metrics apply cleanly and this sector participates fully.
  **Flag cycle and season** when it appears in a cross-sector table: a monsoon quarter or a regional price
  trough will make a cement company screen badly against structurally stable sectors. EBITDA per tonne,
  realisation, lead distance and clinker factor are sector-specific and stay in their own section.
