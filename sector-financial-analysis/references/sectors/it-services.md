# Sector: IT Services & Internet (India)

Covers IT services and consulting, product and platform software, AI and analytics firms, and internet
classifieds and marketplaces.

**The defining characteristic: people are the product.** For IT services, revenue is broadly headcount ×
utilisation × billing rate. That makes this the only sector in this repo where *employee* metrics —
utilisation, attrition, revenue per employee — sit alongside financial ones as primary indicators rather
than footnotes.

## 1. Category taxonomy

| Category | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Large-cap IT services** | Global delivery at scale; multi-vertical, multi-geography | TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Wipro |
| **Mid-cap IT services** | Focused by vertical, service line or geography; higher growth, higher volatility | LTIMindtree, Persistent, Coforge, Mphasis |
| **Products & platforms** | Software licensed or sold as a service; **ARR economics, not headcount economics** | HCLSoftware, product-led businesses |
| **AI / analytics / engineering** | Data science, AI engineering, specialised R&D services | Fractal Analytics |
| **Internet & classifieds** | Marketplaces and platforms monetising traffic, not billable hours | InfoEdge (Naukri, 99acres, Jeevansathi) |

**Internet and classifieds businesses do not belong to the IT-services metric set.** They have no
utilisation, no attrition-driven margin, no TCV. They are traffic-and-monetisation businesses — billings,
paid users, take rate — and their sub-section below reflects that. Do not apply revenue-per-employee to a
marketplace.

## 2. How companies in this sector make money

**IT services** sell time and outcomes. A client signs a contract with a total value (TCV) to be
delivered over years; the company staffs it, delivers, and bills. Profit depends on:

- **Utilisation** — the share of billable staff actually on billable work. Bench time is pure cost.
- **Pyramid** — the mix of junior to senior staff. A "bottom-heavy" pyramid is cheaper to run, so freshers
  added at scale improve margin over time.
- **Onsite vs offshore** — offshore delivery costs far less. Offshore-shifting is a standard margin lever.
- **Billing rate realisation** — pricing, which is where AI-driven productivity pressure now bites.

**The structural question facing this sector is whether AI breaks the headcount-revenue link.** If
delivery becomes materially more productive, clients will expect to pay less for the same outcome, and
revenue per employee rises while total revenue does not. Every large player is positioning around this,
which is why "Innovation & AI" appears as a section in these reports. Treat AI commentary as a
competitive-positioning claim to be tested against pricing and margin, not as evidence in itself.

**Products and platforms** work differently — ARR, renewal rates and gross margins far above services.
Where a company has both, the mix shift matters more than the blended number.

**Deal flow drives everything forward-looking.** TCV won this quarter becomes revenue over the next
several years, which is why TCV is the leading indicator and quarterly revenue is the lagging one.

## 3. Metric definitions

### IT services

- **TCV (Total Contract Value)** — the total value of deals signed in the period, usually split into new
  and renewal. **The single most important forward metric.** Note the disclosure basis: some report net
  new TCV, some total, some only "large deals" above a threshold — state which.
- **Constant currency (cc) growth** — revenue growth stripped of exchange-rate movement. **Always prefer
  cc growth for the underlying trend**; reported growth blends operations with currency, and Indian IT
  earns in dollars while reporting in rupees.
- **Utilisation (%)** — billable staff as a share of total, usually reported excluding trainees. Higher is
  better up to a point; extremely high utilisation means no bench and no capacity to win work.
- **Attrition (%)** — trailing twelve-month voluntary attrition. High attrition raises replacement and
  training cost, disrupts delivery, and is an early margin warning. It is also the sector's clearest
  read on labour-market tightness.
- **Headcount and net additions** — the capacity signal. Falling headcount with rising revenue is either
  productivity or a shift to non-linear revenue; say which.
- **Revenue per employee** — the productivity measure, and the metric most likely to move if AI changes
  delivery economics.
- **Vertical mix** — BFSI, retail, manufacturing, healthcare, telecom. **BFSI is typically the largest
  and the most cyclical**, so its direction disproportionately drives the aggregate.
- **Geography mix** — Americas, Europe, RoW.
- **Client metrics** — $100mn+ / $50mn+ client counts, top-5 and top-10 client concentration. Growth in
  large-client buckets shows account mining, which is cheaper than new-logo acquisition.
- **ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)** — for the products and platforms portion.
- **DSO (Days Sales Outstanding)** — collection efficiency.

### Internet & classifieds

- **Billings** — cash value of contracts sold, which leads recognised revenue; the better growth signal.
- **Paid users / listings / transactions** — the volume driver.
- **Traffic and market share** of the category.
- **Take rate** for marketplaces.
- **Segment profitability** — a mature core (e.g. recruitment) often funds loss-making newer verticals;
  blended margin hides both. Insist on the split.

## 4. Benchmarks — what good looks like

Indicative; verify against the company's own history.

| Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|
| Revenue growth (cc) | mid-to-high single digit for large caps; double digit for mid caps |
| EBIT margin — large-cap IT | 20–26% |
| EBIT margin — mid-cap IT | 15–20% |
| Utilisation (ex-trainees) | 82–87% |
| Attrition (LTM voluntary) | <14% comfortable; >18% is a margin and delivery risk |
| Book-to-bill (TCV ÷ revenue) | >1.0 sustained |
| Revenue per employee | rising YoY |
| Top-10 client concentration | <25% is comfortable |
| DSO | <75 days |
| Products/platform gross margin | far above services — state separately, never blend silently |

**What to watch, by category:**

- **Large-cap IT** — Is cc growth positive and improving? Is TCV converting to revenue, or is the book
  growing while revenue stalls? Is attrition falling? Is BFSI recovering? What is discretionary spend
  doing versus cost-takeout deals — the latter grow revenue at worse margin.
- **Mid-cap IT** — Is client concentration falling? Is the specialisation still scarce? Is growth
  organic or acquired?
- **Products / platforms** — Is ARR growing and renewing? Is the mix shift lifting blended margin?
- **AI / analytics** — Is scarcity of capability holding pricing? Is it a services business labelled AI,
  or genuinely non-linear revenue?
- **Internet / classifieds** — Are billings growing faster than revenue (a leading signal)? Is the core
  segment's margin funding new verticals, and are those approaching breakeven?

## 5. Regulatory quick reference

Lightly regulated domestically; the material exposures are external.

| Area | Body / issue |
|---|---|
| Visas and immigration | US H-1B/L-1 policy, UK and EU work-permit rules — affects onsite delivery cost |
| Data protection | India's DPDP Act; GDPR for European clients; client-country regimes |
| Tax | Transfer pricing, SEZ/STPI incentives, GST on exports |
| Client-side regulation | BFSI clients' own regulators drive their tech spend cycles |
| Trade policy | outsourcing restrictions, tariffs on services where proposed |

Currency is effectively a regulatory-grade variable here: a company earning in USD and reporting in INR
sees reported growth move with the exchange rate independently of operations, which is why cc growth is
the honest measure.

## 6. Per-mode specifics

### Headline KPIs by category (`dashboard`)

| Category | KPIs |
|---|---|
| Large-cap IT | Revenue, cc growth, TCV, EBIT margin, PAT, utilisation, attrition, headcount |
| Mid-cap IT | Revenue, cc growth, EBIT margin, PAT, client concentration, attrition |
| Products & platforms | Revenue, ARR, growth, gross margin, EBIT margin, PAT |
| AI / analytics | Revenue, growth, EBIT margin, PAT, headcount, client mix |
| Internet & classifieds | Revenue, billings, segment revenue, EBITDA margin, PAT, paid users |

### Table columns by category (`financials`)

- **IT services** — Period, Revenue ($mn and ₹cr), cc growth, TCV, EBIT, EBIT%, PAT, YoY
- **Operating metrics table** (mandatory for IT services) — Utilisation, Attrition (LTM), Headcount, Net
  adds, Revenue per employee, DSO. **This table is where the business actually is**; a P&L alone will
  miss a delivery problem for two quarters.
- **Vertical and geography mix table** — revenue share and growth by vertical, with BFSI called out.
- **Internet** — Period, Revenue, Billings, segment split, EBITDA by segment, PAT.

### Chart reference lines (`charts`)

| Metric | Line | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Utilisation | 85% | "Typical optimal utilisation" |
| Attrition | 14% | "Comfortable attrition" — above this pressures margin |
| Book-to-bill (TCV/revenue) | 1.0 | "Order book flat" |
| EBIT margin | 22% | "Large-cap norm" — do not apply to mid caps |

Industry norms, not regulatory thresholds. Plot revenue in **constant currency** where possible, and say
so on the chart; a reported-currency revenue chart mixes two different things.

### Profile coverage by category (`business-profile`)

- **IT services** — service lines, vertical and geography mix, delivery footprint and offshore ratio,
  client pyramid and large-client counts, partnership ecosystem (hyperscalers), **AI/automation
  positioning and what is actually monetised**, and the products/platforms portion if any.
- **Internet** — segment portfolio (each vertical separately), monetisation model per segment, traffic
  position, investments and stakes held in other businesses — for some internet holdings the investment
  portfolio is material to value and deserves its own treatment.

Existing reports carry an **"Innovation & AI"** section — cover it, but test claims against pricing,
margin and disclosed deal wins rather than repeating positioning language.

### Moat candidates by category (`moats`)

- **Client embeddedness** — the genuine moat in IT services. Once a vendor runs mission-critical systems,
  switching means migration risk the client rarely accepts. Cite tenure and the $100mn+ client count.
- **Scale of delivery** — the ability to staff a 5,000-person global programme is available to very few
  firms, and it wins the largest deals.
- **Domain depth in a regulated vertical** — BFSI and healthcare knowledge takes years and is hard to hire
  in bulk.
- **Products and IP** — where revenue is genuinely non-linear, this is the strongest position available in
  the sector; ARR does not depend on headcount.
- **Internet marketplaces** — **network effects**, which is the strongest moat structure in this file: a
  recruitment platform with the most jobs attracts the most candidates, which attracts more jobs.
- **Be sceptical of "AI capability" as a moat.** Every firm in the sector claims it, the tooling is
  broadly available, and hyperscaler partnerships are non-exclusive. It counts only where it shows in
  pricing, margin or a disclosed win — say so plainly rather than repeating the claim.

### Valuation (`valuation`)

Primary multiple: **P/E** — asset-light, cash-generative, usually net cash. **EV/EBITDA** as a
cross-check, stating the cash position, since large net cash distorts it.

Large caps and mid caps trade in different bands, and products/platform revenue commands a materially
higher multiple than services — where the mix is meaningful, a sum-of-the-parts view is more honest than
a blended P/E. For internet businesses with loss-making segments, EV/sales by segment or a
sum-of-the-parts including investment holdings is usually the only sensible approach; a consolidated P/E
on a company subsidising three young verticals means very little.

### CB Rating substitutions (`cb-rating`)

| Component | Weight | IT-services substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 30% | **Constant-currency revenue growth and TCV/book-to-bill** — reported growth blends currency with operations, and TCV leads revenue |
| Profitability | 25% | EBIT margin against the **category** norm (large-cap 20–26%, mid-cap 15–20%) and its direction |
| Delivery Quality | 25% | **Utilisation, attrition and revenue per employee** — replaces PAT Quality, because in a people business the delivery engine is where profit quality actually originates, and it deteriorates before the P&L shows it |
| Forward Outlook | 20% | Deal pipeline, vertical demand commentary (BFSI especially), guidance, AI positioning tested against evidence |

Total 100%. **Delivery Quality replaces PAT Quality** — an IT firm with rising attrition and falling
utilisation is deteriorating even while reporting good profit, and that is precisely the signal this
component exists to capture.

### Extra sections (`quarterly-report`)

- **Operating Metrics** — utilisation, attrition, headcount, revenue per employee, DSO.
- **Deal Wins & TCV** — large deals signed, TCV split, book-to-bill.
- **Innovation & AI** — recurs in existing reports; positioning tested against monetisation.
- **Vertical & Geography Mix** — with BFSI called out.
- **5-Year Trends** — existing reports carry a long-horizon view; useful because this sector's cycles run
  longer than a quarter.
- **Investment Portfolio** — for internet holding companies where stakes are material.

### Event transmission map (`event-impact`, `risks-outlook`)

| Event | Reaches results via | Exposure basis to cite |
|---|---|---|
| USD/INR move | reported revenue and margin | % revenue in USD, hedge position |
| US visa policy change | onsite delivery cost, delivery model | onsite share, visa-dependent headcount |
| Client-sector downturn (esp. BFSI) | discretionary spend, deal flow | that vertical's revenue share |
| US or EU recession signal | discretionary vs cost-takeout mix | discretionary revenue share |
| Large deal win or loss | multi-year revenue visibility | TCV vs annual revenue |
| Wage inflation / attrition spike | margin | employee cost as % of revenue |
| AI-driven pricing pressure | billing rate realisation | revenue per employee trend, fixed-price share |
| Hyperscaler partnership change | joint go-to-market pipeline | partnership-linked revenue where disclosed |
| Data-protection regulation | compliance cost, delivery model | affected geography share |

## 7. Where to look (sourcing)

**Tier 1** — quarterly investor presentations and earnings calls. **Utilisation, attrition, headcount,
TCV, vertical mix and client-bucket counts appear only in the presentation or fact sheet**, never in the
press release. The earnings call is unusually informative here because management commentary on
discretionary spend and deal pipeline is genuinely forward-looking. Large-cap IT companies also publish
a quarterly **fact sheet** with the full operating-metric history — the single most useful document for
this sector.

**Tier 2 — sector authorities**: **NASSCOM** for industry growth, headcount and export data — the right
benchmark for whether a company is gaining or losing share. Also **RBI** for services-export data, and
**Gartner/IDC/ISG** for global tech-spend forecasts and deal data where citable, attributed to the
provider.

A company growing 6% when NASSCOM reports industry growth of 8% is losing share, and the headline alone
will not say so.

## 10. Mode applicability

All 15 modes apply. Notes:

- **`segments`** — vertical and geography mix is the meaningful cut for IT services; **segment-wise
  profitability** is essential for internet companies where a mature core funds loss-making verticals.
- **`financials`** — the operating-metrics table (utilisation, attrition, headcount, revenue per
  employee) is mandatory for IT services, not optional.
- **`charts`** — plot constant-currency revenue and label it as such.
- **`valuation`** — P/E primary; sum-of-the-parts where products/platform or investment holdings are
  material. Not P/BV.
- **`cb-rating`** — **Delivery Quality replaces PAT Quality**; see the table above.
- **`moats`** — client embeddedness and network effects are real; "AI capability" usually is not. Test
  it against pricing and margin.
- **Internet & classifieds** — do not apply IT-services operating metrics (utilisation, attrition, TCV)
  to them; use billings, paid users and segment profitability instead.
- **Cross-sector work** — all universal metrics apply cleanly: Revenue, EBITDA%, PAT, Mkt Cap, P/E and
  EV/EBITDA are meaningful. **Net Debt is usually meaningless** — most of this sector is substantially
  net cash, so mark it "n/a — not comparable for this sector" unless real debt exists, and note the cash
  position when EV/EBITDA is used. TCV, utilisation, attrition and cc growth stay out of cross-sector
  tables.
