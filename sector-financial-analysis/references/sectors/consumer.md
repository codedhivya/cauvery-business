# Sector: Consumer (India)

Covers companies selling to the end consumer — retail, FMCG and foods, alcoholic beverages, sugar and
agri-processing, textiles and apparel, hospitality, and leisure.

This is the broadest sector file. Its categories are measured on genuinely different bases: a hotel is
judged on occupancy and room rate, a sugar mill on cane crush and recovery, a retailer on same-store
sales. **Classify before applying any metric** — the shared thread is consumer demand, not a shared
metric set.

## 1. Category taxonomy

| Category | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Retail & Apparel** | Store-based or omni-channel retail | DMart (Avenue Supermarts), V2 Retail, Aditya Birla Fashion, Page Industries, Raymond Lifestyle |
| **FMCG & Foods** | Branded fast-moving goods | ITC, Hindustan Unilever, and the branded-foods majors |
| **Dairy** | Procurement-led; margin set at the farm gate. **Not a typical FMCG business** | Hatsun Agro, Heritage Foods, Dodla Dairy, Parag Milk Foods, Milky Mist |
| **Alcoholic Beverages** | Spirits, beer, wine — heavily regulated, state-by-state | United Spirits, Radico Khaitan, United Breweries, Tilaknagar, GM Breweries, Globus Spirits, Allied Blenders (ABD), Sula Vineyards |
| **Sugar & Agri-processing** | Cane crushing, ethanol, distillery | Balrampur Chini, and the integrated sugar mills |
| **Textiles** | Yarn, fabric, garments | KPR Mill, Nitin Spinners, Gokaldas Exports, Vardhman |
| **Hospitality** | Hotels | IHCL, EIH (Oberoi), Chalet, Lemon Tree, Juniper, SAMHI, ITC Hotels |
| **Leisure** | Amusement parks, experience venues | Wonderla Holidays, Imagicaaworld |
| **Jewellery — retailer** | Organised gold and diamond retail to consumers | Titan, Kalyan Jewellers, Senco Gold, PN Gadgil, Thangamayil |
| **Jewellery — B2B manufacturer** | Makes and supplies to other brands or overseas buyers. **Order-driven, no store network**, so footfall and SSSG do not apply | Sky Gold, and listed jewellery manufacturers |
| **Jewellery — LGD exporter** | Lab-grown diamond production for export. **Priced and cycled unlike natural stones**, and exposed to destination tariffs | Goldiam International |

Cross-category comparison within consumer is meaningful only on universal metrics (revenue growth,
EBITDA margin, PAT). Do not rank a hotel's occupancy against a mill's capacity utilisation.

## 2. How companies in this sector make money

All of them convert consumer demand into revenue, but the lever differs:

- **Retail** earns on throughput and mix — more stores, more footfall per store, more spend per visit.
  The core question is whether growth is coming from new stores (capital-hungry, dilutive to returns
  initially) or from existing ones (free, and the real quality signal). That is why same-store sales
  growth matters more than headline revenue.
- **FMCG** earns on volume and pricing. A revenue rise driven by price increases is weaker than one
  driven by volume, because pricing has a ceiling and volume signals genuine demand.
- **Alcobev** is a regulated-margin business. State excise policy, pricing approvals and route-to-market
  differ by state, and a single state's policy change can move a company's economics materially.
- **Sugar** is a commodity processor with a policy overlay: cane price is administered, sugar price is
  partly controlled, and ethanol has become the profit swing factor as diversion from sugar to ethanol
  improves realisations.
- **Textiles** is a spread business — the gap between cotton or yarn input cost and realisation — plus
  capacity utilisation. Highly cyclical on cotton.
- **Hospitality** earns on rooms sold × rate. Because costs are largely fixed, incremental occupancy
  falls almost straight to profit, which makes hotels highly operationally geared in both directions.

## 3. Metric definitions

### Retail, FMCG and jewellery

- **SSSG (Same Store Sales Growth, also written same-store sales)** — revenue growth from stores open at
  least a full comparable period.
  Strips out the flattering effect of new-store additions. **The single most important retail metric**:
  a company can grow revenue 25% while its existing stores shrink.
- **Footfall** — customer visits. Paired with **conversion** and **ASP (Average Selling Price)** or
  average bill value, it decomposes SSSG into traffic versus spend.
- **Store count / net additions / retail area (sq ft)** — the expansion engine. Watch revenue per sq ft
  alongside; falling revenue per sq ft while store count rises means expansion is diluting productivity.
- **Volume growth vs value growth** — for FMCG, the gap between the two is price/mix. Value growth on
  flat volumes is a warning, not a result.
- **Gross margin** — input cost pass-through. **EBITDA margin** — after brand spend and store costs.
- **Inventory days / working capital** — retail and jewellery are working-capital intensive; jewellery
  especially, since inventory is gold.

### Jewellery — the mix metrics that decide margin

**Classify first: retailer, B2B manufacturer, or LGD exporter.** The corpus splits its jewellery
coverage three ways and the metrics do not transfer. A retailer is read on footfall, SSSG and studded
ratio; a B2B manufacturer on order book, client concentration and export destination — **it has no
stores, so retail metrics are meaningless for it**; an LGD exporter on LGD share, realisation per carat
and tariff exposure.

Revenue growth in jewellery is mostly gold price, not business performance. Two mix numbers separate
them:

- **Studded ratio (%)** — studded (diamond-set) as a share of sales. **The margin metric.** Plain gold
  carries thin, near-commodity margin; studded carries several times more. A quarter of strong revenue
  growth with a falling studded ratio is weaker than it looks.
- **Making charges** — the value-added fee on gold, and the pricing lever that does not depend on the
  metal price.
- **Lab-grown diamonds (LGD)** — a structural shift, not a product line. Prices behave nothing like
  natural stones, so **state LGD share of revenue or exports separately**, never blended into a
  "diamond" line. An exporter can be LGD-dominant while a domestic retailer is barely exposed.
- **Carat volumes** alongside value, so price and quantity are separable.
- **Store count and format** — own vs franchised, and **e-commerce share of revenue**, which carries a
  different margin and working-capital profile from a store.
- **Old-gold exchange share** — a large share flatters volumes without new metal demand.

**For an exporter, add tariff and destination exposure**: share of revenue by destination market, and any
tariff-mitigation structure. Where a period's other income contains a one-off — a tariff refund, for
instance — **report the steady-state margin excluding it alongside the reported one**, as the corpus
does. A margin expansion driven by a refund is not operating improvement.

### Dairy

Dairy is a **procurement-led** business. Its margin is set upstream, at the farm gate, before anything is
sold.

- **Milk procurement price and volume (LLPD)** — the dominant cost, largely uncontrollable, and the
  driver of margin direction. Track the spread between procurement cost and realisation.
- **Flush vs lean season** — procurement is seasonal. Milk is abundant and cheap in flush, scarce and
  expensive in lean, so **quarters are not comparable year-on-year without naming the season**.
- **VAP (value-added products) share** — curd, paneer, cheese, beverages, as a share of revenue. **The
  single most important mix metric.** Liquid milk is close to a pass-through; VAP carries the margin, so
  a rising VAP share is the whole equity story.
- **Cold chain and chilling capacity** — the physical constraint on both procurement reach and VAP,
  and where the capex goes.
- **Shelf life by product** and distribution reach — fresh products need daily replenishment and a denser
  network than ambient FMCG.
- **Farmer network size** — the real barrier to entry; a procurement network takes years to build and
  cannot be bought quickly.

### Hospitality and leisure

- **Occupancy (%)** — rooms sold ÷ rooms available.
- **ARR (Average Room Rate)** — realised rate per occupied room.
- **RevPAR (Revenue per Available Room)** = Occupancy × ARR. **The headline hotel metric**, because it
  captures both levers at once. A RevPAR rise on rate rather than occupancy is higher quality.
- **ALOS (Average Length of Stay)**, **F&B revenue share**, **management-contract vs owned rooms** — an
  asset-light management model earns fees without capital, and is valued differently from an owner.
- **Footfall and per-capita spend** for leisure venues; **ARPU** where a season-pass model exists.

### Sugar and agri-processing

- **Cane crush (lakh tonnes)** — volume processed.
- **Recovery rate (%)** — sugar extracted per tonne of cane. Small changes move profitability sharply.
- **Ethanol / distillery capacity and realisation** — the diversification that decoupled sugar earnings
  from sugar prices. Watch the sugar-to-ethanol diversion share.
- **Cane arrears** — unpaid dues to farmers; a working-capital and political risk.

### Textiles

- **Spindle count / capacity utilisation (%)** — the asset base and how hard it is running.
- **Cotton and yarn prices** — input and output; the spread is the business.
- **Realisation per kg**, **value-added share** (garments vs commodity yarn — higher value-add means less
  cyclicality).

## 4. Benchmarks — what good looks like

Indicative; verify against the company's own history and its peer set rather than treating as absolutes.

| Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|
| SSSG (retail) | high single digit to low teens; negative is a red flag even with revenue growth |
| Revenue per sq ft | rising YoY; falling while store count grows means dilutive expansion |
| FMCG volume growth | mid single digit; volume growth should carry most of value growth |
| EBITDA margin (grocery retail) | ~7–9% — structurally thin; scale is the moat |
| EBITDA margin (branded apparel/FMCG) | 15–25% |
| Hotel occupancy | >70% healthy; >75% strong |
| RevPAR growth | rate-led is higher quality than occupancy-led |
| Sugar recovery | ~10–12%, varies by region and season |
| Textile capacity utilisation | >85% |
| Net debt / EBITDA | <2× comfortable for asset-heavy categories (hotels, sugar, textiles) |

**What to watch, by category:**

- **Retail** — Is SSSG positive and rate-led? Is revenue per sq ft rising? Is expansion funded by
  operating cash or debt? Is inventory turning faster?
- **FMCG** — Is volume growth carrying value growth? Is gross margin holding through input swings? Is
  rural growing faster than urban (or the reverse), and why?
- **Alcobev** — Any state excise policy change in a major market? Is premium mix rising? Are pricing
  approvals pending?
- **Sugar** — Is recovery improving? What share is diverted to ethanol? Are cane arrears rising?
- **Textiles** — Is the cotton-yarn spread widening? Is utilisation above 85%? Is value-added share rising?
- **Hospitality** — Is RevPAR growth rate-led? Is the management-contract pipeline growing (fee income
  without capital)? What is the demand mix — leisure, business, MICE?

## 5. Regulatory quick reference

No single sector regulator; the touchpoints that move earnings:

| Area | Body / rule |
|---|---|
| Food safety and labelling | FSSAI |
| Alcohol — excise, pricing, route-to-market | **state governments**, and they differ materially |
| Sugar — cane pricing (FRP/SAP), export quota, ethanol blending | Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution |
| Retail FDI | DPIIT policy |
| GST rate changes | affect demand and pricing across every category |

Verify current levels against the relevant circular rather than carrying a figure forward.

### Chart reference lines (`charts`)

| Metric | Line | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Hotel occupancy | 70% | "Healthy occupancy" |
| Textile capacity utilisation | 85% | "Efficient utilisation" |

No regulatory thresholds apply here — every line above is an industry norm and must be labelled as such,
not implied to be a rule.

### Profile coverage by category (`business-profile`)

- **Retail** — format (value/premium, grocery/apparel), store footprint and geography, private-label
  share, omni-channel mix, cluster strategy, supply chain and distribution centres.
- **FMCG** — brand portfolio, category leadership positions, distribution reach (outlets covered),
  rural vs urban mix, new-product contribution.
- **Alcobev** — brand ladder (mass/prestige/luxury), state-wise exposure and route-to-market, own vs
  tie-up manufacturing, pricing approval status.
- **Sugar** — crushing capacity, distillery capacity, co-generation, cane command area, integration level.
- **Textiles** — position in the chain (spinning/weaving/garmenting), capacity, export vs domestic mix,
  key customer concentration.
- **Hospitality** — brand portfolio by segment (luxury/upscale/mid/budget), owned vs leased vs
  management-contract mix, room inventory and pipeline, key city exposure.

Always cover **Key Brands** and **Concentration Risks** — both recur as sections across this sector's
reports, and brand concentration is a real risk here in a way it is not in industrials.

### Moat candidates by category (`moats`)

- **Retail** — cluster density and supply-chain scale (a cost advantage competitors cannot match at lower
  volume), owned real estate, private-label share, cost leadership enabling price leadership.
- **FMCG** — brand equity built over decades, distribution reach into outlets a new entrant cannot
  replicate quickly, category-defining share.
- **Alcobev** — state licences and route-to-market relationships (regulatory scarcity), brand ladder,
  ageing inventory for premium spirits.
- **Sugar** — cane command area (geographically exclusive), integration into ethanol and power.
- **Hospitality** — irreplaceable locations, brand, and the management-contract model (fee income with no
  capital).
- **Textiles** — usually thin. Commodity spinning has no durable moat; say so rather than inventing one.
  Value-added and customer-embedded players are the exception.

### Valuation (`valuation`)

Primary multiple: **P/E** for most categories; **EV/EBITDA** where leverage or asset intensity varies
(hotels, sugar, textiles), because it neutralises capital-structure differences. Retail is often looked
at on EV/EBITDA and on revenue per sq ft against peers. Hotels are also compared on **EV per room**.
Avoid P/B here — book value says little about a brand or a lease-based footprint.

### CB Rating substitutions (`cb-rating`)

| Component | Weight | Consumer substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 30% | Revenue growth, plus **SSSG** for retail / **volume growth** for FMCG / **RevPAR growth** for hotels — same-store or like-for-like growth, not headline |
| Profitability | 25% | Gross and EBITDA margin, and direction |
| PAT Quality | 25% | PAT growth adjusted for one-offs; for asset-heavy categories, **operating cash conversion** — reported profit that never becomes cash is a working-capital problem, and this sector is where that hides |
| Forward Outlook | 20% | Expansion pipeline (stores, rooms, capacity), demand commentary, input-cost outlook |

Total 100%.

### Extra sections (`quarterly-report`)

- **Key Brands** — the portfolio and its positioning; central to how these companies compete.
- **Concentration Risks** — customer, geography, state-policy, single-brand or single-format dependence.
- **Store / Room / Capacity Economics** — unit economics: revenue per store or per room, payback,
  maturity curve of new additions.
- **Working Capital** — for retail, jewellery and textiles, where the cash cycle drives the equity story.
- **Milk Chain** — dairy. Procurement → chilling → processing → distribution, with volumes and margin at
  each stage. The corpus gives this its own section, because the chain *is* the business.
- **Lean Season & Pricing** — dairy. How procurement cost and realisation move between flush and lean,
  and what pricing action was taken. **Recurs as a standalone section**, and is the honest way to explain
  a quarter that looks weak on seasonality alone.

### Event transmission map (`event-impact`, `risks-outlook`)

| Event | Reaches results via | Exposure basis to cite |
|---|---|---|
| GST rate change on a category | pricing, demand elasticity | that category's revenue share |
| State excise policy change (alcobev) | volumes, realisation, route-to-market | that state's share of volumes |
| Cane FRP/SAP revision | input cost, margin | crush volume, cane cost per tonne |
| Ethanol blending target or price revision | diversion economics | distillery capacity, ethanol revenue share |
| Cotton price move | input cost, spread | raw-material share of cost, inventory position |
| Gold price move (jewellery) | inventory gains/losses, demand | inventory value, hedging policy, gold-on-lease share |
| **BIS hallmarking / purity rules** | compliance cost, and a shift of share toward organised players | organised vs unorganised positioning |
| Monsoon / rural demand shift | volume growth, mix | rural revenue share |
| Tourism disruption or travel restriction | occupancy, ARR | leisure vs business mix, city concentration |
| Import duty change on finished goods | competitive pressure, pricing | import share of the category |

## 7. Where to look (sourcing)

**Tier 1** — quarterly investor presentations. Retail and hospitality companies disclose SSSG, store
counts, occupancy, ARR and RevPAR there but often not in the press release, so the presentation is
essential rather than optional. Search `"<company> investor presentation Q<n> FY<yy> filetype:pdf"`.

**Tier 2 — sector authorities**: **FSSAI** (food safety), state excise departments (alcobev — note these
are state-level and fragmented), the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution (sugar,
ethanol), **ISMA** for sugar industry aggregates, **CMIE / Nielsen** for FMCG demand data where citable,
tourism ministry statistics for hospitality demand, and **Cotton Association of India** for cotton prices.

Hotel companies frequently cite third-party RevPAR benchmarks (STR-type data). Attribute those to the
provider rather than presenting them as company disclosure.

## 10. Mode applicability

All 15 modes apply. Notes:

- **`segments`** — the highest-value mode in this sector. Brand-wise, format-wise, and geography-wise
  splits are where consumer stories actually live, and companies disclose them well.
- **`school`** — rename per sub-sector where it helps ("Retail School", "Hotel School"), following the
  cement precedent.
- **`valuation`** — P/E primary; EV/EBITDA for asset-heavy categories; EV per room for hotels. Not P/B.
- **`cb-rating`** — uses like-for-like growth (SSSG / volume / RevPAR) rather than headline revenue, and
  weights operating cash conversion inside PAT Quality.
- **Cross-sector work** — all universal metrics apply cleanly here: Revenue, EBITDA%, PAT, Mkt Cap, P/E,
  EV/EBITDA and Net Debt are all meaningful. This sector participates fully in cross-sector tables. The
  metrics that must **not** cross out are the category-specific ones — SSSG, RevPAR, recovery rate,
  occupancy — which stay in their own per-sector section.
