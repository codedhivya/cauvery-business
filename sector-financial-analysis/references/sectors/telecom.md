# Sector: Telecom (India)

Covers wireless and wireline operators, and passive infrastructure providers.

**Two facts define this sector and both are unusual.** First, it is a near-duopoly-plus-one after a
decade of consolidation, so competitive dynamics turn on a handful of pricing decisions rather than on
market fragmentation. Second, **AGR dues and spectrum liabilities dominate the balance sheet** to a degree
seen nowhere else in this repo — for one operator they are close to existential, and no analysis of the
sector is complete without them.

## 1. Category taxonomy

| Category | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Integrated wireless operator** | Mobile services plus broadband, enterprise and digital; the main business | Bharti Airtel, Reliance Jio (unlisted separately), Vodafone Idea |
| **Passive infrastructure** | Towers and fibre leased to operators; annuity economics, not consumer-facing | Indus Towers |
| **Wireline / enterprise** | Fixed broadband, leased lines, data centres — usually a segment within an integrated operator | Tata Communications, Netweb Technologies |

Compare wireless operators with each other. A tower company is an infrastructure annuity business closer
to `infra-realty` in behaviour than to a consumer telco, and its metrics — tenancy ratio, lease tenure —
do not translate.

## 2. How companies in this sector make money

Revenue is **subscribers × ARPU**. Both matter, but not equally: after the tariff wars, **ARPU is the
lever that actually moves profit**, because subscriber growth in a saturated market largely means taking
share from a weakened competitor rather than expanding the pool.

The economics are extreme operating leverage on a huge fixed asset base. Networks cost enormous capital
to build and almost nothing to run per additional user, so **an ARPU increase falls close to entirely
through to EBITDA**. That is why a tariff hike is the single most consequential event in this sector, and
why the timing of the next one is the perennial question.

Against that sits the capital burden:

- **Spectrum** is bought at auction, typically on deferred payment over many years. It is a liability
  that appears as debt and a right that appears as an intangible asset.
- **AGR (Adjusted Gross Revenue) dues** arise from a long-running dispute over how licence fees are
  calculated on revenue. The Supreme Court's ruling created very large historical liabilities with
  government-set payment schedules. **For a weaker operator these dues are the primary determinant of
  viability**, ahead of any operating metric.
- **Capex intensity** is persistently high — 4G densification, 5G rollout, fibre. Free cash flow only
  appears when capex moderates while ARPU rises.

So the sector's investment question reduces to: is ARPU rising fast enough to service the spectrum and
AGR burden while funding the next technology cycle?

### The analogy

A network with almost no marginal cost. Once the towers and spectrum are paid for, an extra subscriber costs very little — so the business is subscribers × ARPU against an enormous fixed base, and the fixed base has to be rebuilt every technology cycle.

## 3. Metric definitions

- **ARPU (Average Revenue Per User, ₹/month)** — **the headline metric.** State whether it is blended,
  or mobile-only, and which subscriber base it is calculated on; definitions differ between operators.
- **Subscriber base** — total, and split by prepaid/postpaid. **Postpaid carries higher ARPU and lower
  churn**, so mix shift matters.
- **Net subscriber additions** — the share-shift signal in a saturated market.
- **Churn (%)** — monthly subscriber loss. Rising churn precedes ARPU pressure.
- **Data usage per subscriber (GB/month)** and **4G/5G subscriber share** — the upgrade path that
  supports ARPU.
- **EBITDA margin** — high, and the operating-leverage indicator.
- **Capex and capex intensity (capex ÷ revenue)** — the cash drain; watch for the inflection when 5G
  spending moderates.
- **Net debt**, and separately **spectrum liabilities** and **AGR dues** — these should be stated
  individually, not buried in a single net-debt figure, because their repayment schedules are
  government-set rather than negotiable.
- **Net debt / EBITDA** — the sector runs structurally high; interpret against telecom norms, not
  manufacturing.
- **Free cash flow after spectrum and AGR payments** — the honest measure of whether the business
  self-funds.
- **Tower tenancy ratio** — for infrastructure companies: tenants per tower, the operating-leverage
  metric.

## 4. Benchmarks — what good looks like

Indicative; the sector is in an ARPU-repair cycle, so levels move.

| Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|
| ARPU growth | rising; the central thesis of the sector |
| EBITDA margin — wireless | 45–55% |
| EBITDA margin — towers | 55–70% |
| Churn | <2% monthly |
| Postpaid share | rising; higher ARPU and stickier |
| Capex intensity | falling from the 5G peak is the signal to watch |
| Net debt / EBITDA | <3× is comfortable **for telecom**; do not apply a manufacturing threshold |
| Data usage per sub | rising, supporting ARPU upgrades |
| Tower tenancy ratio | >2.0× |

**What to watch:**

- **Is ARPU rising**, and is it price-led or mix-led? Mix-led (prepaid to postpaid, 4G to 5G) is higher
  quality than a one-off tariff increase.
- **Has a tariff hike been taken, and has it stuck** without excessive churn? The churn response is the
  test of pricing power.
- **Is capex intensity moderating** as 5G rollout matures? That is the free-cash-flow inflection.
- **What is the AGR and spectrum repayment schedule**, and is operating cash flow sufficient to meet it?
- **Is subscriber share shifting**, and from whom? Gains taken from a distressed competitor are real but
  finite.
- For the weakest operator: **is funding secured** — equity raise, government conversion, promoter
  infusion? This is a solvency question, not a performance one, and should be treated as such.

### AGR, and what makes it a balance-sheet event rather than a tax

**AGR (Adjusted Gross Revenue) is the base** on which an operator pays its licence fee and spectrum usage
charges. The dispute that reshaped the sector was never about the *rate* — it was about the **base**:
whether it covers telecom revenue only, or **all revenue** an operator earns, including non-telecom
income.

When the wider definition was upheld, the effect was **retrospective**. Years of past dues were
recalculated on a larger base, converting a routine levy into liabilities large enough to threaten
solvency. **Two things follow for any analysis:**

- **Always say which revenue base a levy applies to.** A rate without its base is not a number.
- **A definitional ruling can be a larger event than a rate change**, because it applies backwards. Treat
  a pending definitional dispute as a balance-sheet risk, not a P&L one.

### When the state becomes a shareholder

Where statutory dues exceed what an operator can pay, the government may **convert those dues into
equity** rather than enforce them — taking a large, sometimes near-controlling stake. Alongside
moratoria on payments and waivers of bank guarantees, this is a distinctive feature of this sector and it
changes the analysis:

- **The dues do not disappear; they change form.** Interest and instalments become dilution. Per-share
  metrics must be read against the post-conversion count, and further conversions are usually possible.
- **The state now has an interest in the company surviving** — it holds equity that a collapse would
  zero, and unpaid dues it could no longer collect. That materially changes the probability of further
  relief, and it is a legitimate thing to state.
- **Do not read relief as recovery.** A moratorium defers cash; it does not generate it. **The operating
  question is unchanged: is ARPU rising fast enough to fund the network before the deferral ends?**
- **State the government holding, the conversions to date, and when deferred payments resume.** A reader
  cannot size the risk without that schedule.

### What goes wrong, and the tell

**Capex and dues arriving faster than ARPU rises to meet them.** The tells: ARPU flat while capex intensity climbs; subscriber growth concentrated in low-value users; a spectrum auction or licence payment falling due; and rising churn, which costs acquisition spend twice.

## 5. Regulatory quick reference

Among the most regulated sectors here, and regulation has repeatedly reshaped its economics.

| Area | Body / issue |
|---|---|
| Sector regulator | **TRAI** — tariff, quality of service, and the authoritative subscriber and ARPU data |
| Licensing, spectrum, AGR | **DoT (Department of Telecommunications)** |
| **Licence Fee (LF)** | **3% of AGR** — paid simply to hold the licence *(as at 2026; confirm at source)* |
| **USO / Digital Bharat Nidhi** | **5% contribution**, a **separate line** from the licence fee — do not fold the two into one levy | 
| Spectrum auctions | DoT — pricing, quantum, deferred payment terms |
| AGR dues | Supreme Court rulings and DoT-set payment schedules; moratoria and conversion options have been extended before |
| Right of way | state-level; affects fibre and tower rollout cost |
| Data and privacy | DPDP Act; lawful-intercept obligations |
| Industry data | **TRAI** monthly subscriber and quarterly performance reports — the sector's best public data source |


**AGR is the number the levies sit on, which is why its definition was the whole fight.** The 2019
Supreme Court ruling on what counts as adjusted gross revenue reshaped every balance sheet in the sector
overnight, converting a definitional question into multi-year liabilities. When quoting a levy, state it
as a **percentage of AGR** and say which revenue base is in scope — a levy rate is meaningless without it.

**AGR and spectrum policy decisions move this sector more than operating performance does**, especially
for the weaker operator. Track DoT and Supreme Court developments as first-order events.

## 6. Per-mode specifics

### Headline KPIs by category (`dashboard`)

| Category | KPIs |
|---|---|
| Wireless operator | Revenue, **ARPU**, subscriber base, net adds, EBITDA margin, PAT, capex, **net debt incl. spectrum & AGR** |
| Towers | Revenue, towers, tenancy ratio, EBITDA margin, PAT, net debt |
| Enterprise / wireline | Revenue, growth, EBITDA margin, order book where disclosed |

### Table columns by category (`financials`)

- **Wireless** — Period, Revenue, YoY, **ARPU**, Subscribers, Net adds, EBITDA, EBITDA%, PAT, Capex
- **Liability table — mandatory.** Net debt, **spectrum liabilities**, **AGR dues**, repayment schedule
  by year. Reporting a single net-debt figure for an Indian telco hides the structure of the obligation
  and its government-set timetable, which is the sector's central risk.
- **Subscriber and usage table** — prepaid/postpaid split, 4G/5G share, data usage per sub, churn.

### Chart reference lines (`charts`)

| Metric | Line | Label |
|---|---|---|
| EBITDA margin | 50% | "Wireless sector norm" |
| Churn | 2% | "Comfortable churn" |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 3.0× | "Telecom comfort" — explicitly note this is not a manufacturing threshold |
| ARPU | industry average for the period | "Industry ARPU" — from TRAI; label the period |

**Chart ARPU alongside subscriber trend rather than revenue alone** — revenue conflates the two, and in
this sector they frequently move in opposite directions.

### Profile coverage by category (`business-profile`)

Cover: subscriber base and mix, **ARPU trajectory**, network footprint and 5G coverage, spectrum holdings
by band and expiry, **AGR and spectrum liability position with repayment schedule**, enterprise and
digital-services segments, tower and fibre assets (owned or monetised), and funding position.

Existing reports carry **USPs / Competitive Moat**, **Concentration Risks**, **Management** and
**Sector Metrics** sections — cover each, grounded in disclosed numbers.

**Note the bilingual precedent**: the existing telecom sector report was produced in a bilingual format.
If regenerating it, check whether that format is wanted before defaulting to English-only.

### Moat candidates by category (`moats`)

- **Spectrum holdings** — a licensed, finite, auctioned resource. Genuinely scarce and legally protected
  for the licence term. Cite bands and expiry.
- **Network and fibre footprint** — decades of capital, and impossible to replicate quickly at national
  scale.
- **Scale in a consolidated market** — with few players, scale confers real pricing influence, which is
  why the sector's structure matters more than in fragmented industries.
- **Postpaid and enterprise relationships** — stickier revenue with lower churn.
- **Tower companies — long-tenure master service agreements** with anchor tenants, and the tenancy ratio
  that turns a fixed asset into an annuity.
- **Be sceptical** of "5G leadership" as a moat while the monetisation case is unproven. Coverage claims
  are marketing until they show in ARPU. Also treat subscriber gains from a distressed competitor as
  finite rather than structural.

### Valuation (`valuation`)

Primary multiple: **EV/EBITDA** — the correct basis for a heavily leveraged, capital-intensive business
where P/E is distorted by interest, depreciation and, at times, negative earnings. **EV per subscriber**
is the standard cross-check.

**EV must include spectrum liabilities and AGR dues**, not just reported net debt. A valuation that
treats these as off-balance-sheet materially understates enterprise value, and this is the most common
error in reading Indian telecom.

For a loss-making operator, EV/EBITDA and a solvency assessment are more informative than any earnings
multiple. Tower companies are valued on EV/EBITDA and, like REITs, on cash-flow yield.

### CB Rating substitutions (`cb-rating`)

Capital-intensive with explicit debt weighting, following the `power-energy` precedent — but here the
liability component carries more weight than anywhere else in the repo, because AGR and spectrum dues can
determine survival.

| Component | Weight | Telecom substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 25% | **ARPU growth and subscriber net adds** — not revenue, which conflates the two |
| Profitability | 25% | EBITDA margin against the sector norm, and direction |
| PAT Quality | 10% | PAT adjusted for one-offs; frequently negative or distorted in this sector, so weighted low |
| Debt, Spectrum & AGR | 25% | Net debt **including spectrum and AGR dues**, repayment schedule against operating cash flow, funding secured |
| Capex & Outlook | 15% | Capex intensity trend, 5G monetisation, tariff-hike outlook, regulatory position |

Total 100%. **PAT Quality is deliberately down-weighted and the liability component up-weighted**, because
in this sector reported profit is a weak signal while the ability to service government-set obligations
is the binding constraint.

### Extra sections (`quarterly-report`)

- **ARPU & Subscriber Metrics** — ARPU, mix, net adds, churn, data usage.
- **Spectrum & AGR Liabilities** — holdings, dues, repayment schedule. **The section that most
  distinguishes a telecom report**, and the one a reader most needs.
- **Capex & Network** — 5G rollout, coverage, capex intensity trajectory.
- **Sector Metrics** — recurs in existing reports; industry-level ARPU, subscriber and share data.
- **USPs / Competitive Moat** and **Concentration Risks** — both recur in existing reports.
- **Management** — appears in existing reports.

### Event transmission map (`event-impact`, `risks-outlook`)

| Event | Reaches results via | Exposure basis to cite |
|---|---|---|
| Tariff hike | ARPU, and near-fully through to EBITDA | subscriber base, prepaid share, churn response |
| Spectrum auction | capex, future deferred liability | bands sought, existing holdings and expiry |
| AGR ruling or payment-schedule change | liability, solvency for the weaker operator | AGR dues outstanding, moratorium status |
| Government relief or equity conversion | balance sheet, dilution | dues eligible for conversion |
| Competitor distress or exit | subscriber share, pricing discipline | that competitor's subscriber base |
| 5G monetisation milestone | ARPU upgrade path | 5G subscriber share, device penetration |
| TRAI tariff or QoS regulation | pricing freedom, compliance cost | affected service share |
| Right-of-way policy change | rollout cost and speed | fibre and tower expansion plan |
| Interest-rate move | debt servicing cost | floating-rate share of a large debt book |

## 7. Where to look (sourcing)

**Tier 1** — quarterly investor presentations and earnings calls, which disclose ARPU, subscriber mix,
churn, data usage, capex and the spectrum/AGR liability position with repayment schedules. The
liability schedule is often only in the presentation or annual report and is essential.

**Tier 2 — sector authorities**: **TRAI** is unusually valuable here — it publishes **monthly subscriber
data and quarterly performance indicator reports** with operator-wise ARPU, subscriber counts and market
share. That makes it the authoritative denominator for any share or ARPU claim, and it is public. Also
**DoT** for spectrum, licensing and AGR, and Supreme Court rulings for AGR developments.

An operator's ARPU means much less without TRAI's industry average alongside it, and subscriber-share
claims should be checked against TRAI rather than taken from company slides.

## 10. Mode applicability

All 15 modes apply. Notes:

- **`financials`** — the **spectrum and AGR liability table is mandatory**. A single net-debt figure
  conceals the government-set repayment structure that determines viability.
- **`charts`** — plot ARPU alongside subscribers, not revenue alone; the two often move in opposite
  directions. Include TRAI's industry ARPU as the reference line.
- **`valuation`** — EV/EBITDA primary, EV per subscriber as cross-check. **EV must include spectrum and
  AGR liabilities**; excluding them is the most common valuation error in this sector.
- **`cb-rating`** — five components, with the liability component weighted at 25% and PAT Quality down at
  10%, because reported profit is a weak signal here while obligation servicing is binding.
- **`event-impact`** — tariff hikes, spectrum auctions and AGR rulings are first-order events that move
  the sector more than operating results do.
- **`moats`** — spectrum and network footprint are real; "5G leadership" is marketing until it appears in
  ARPU.
- **Output format** — the existing sector report was **bilingual**; check whether that is wanted before
  regenerating in English only.
- **Cross-sector work** — Revenue, EBITDA%, PAT, Mkt Cap and EV/EBITDA apply. **Net Debt requires a
  flag**: an Indian telco's reported net debt typically excludes spectrum and AGR dues, so a naive
  cross-sector Net Debt ranking will materially understate leverage here. Either state the all-in figure
  or mark the cell with the exclusion. ARPU, subscriber counts, churn and spectrum holdings are
  sector-specific and stay in their own section.
