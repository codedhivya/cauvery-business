# Sector: Automobiles & Auto Components (India)

Covers two-wheeler and passenger-vehicle manufacturers, auto component suppliers, tyres, and automotive
batteries.

**The defining metric is volume.** Everything else — realisation, margin, capacity utilisation — is read
against units sold. Monthly volume disclosure makes this the most transparent sector in this repo: you
know how a vehicle maker is doing weeks before it reports.

For suppliers, the second defining fact is the **OEM versus replacement split**. Selling to a vehicle
manufacturer and selling into the aftermarket are different businesses with different margins, different
cyclicality and different pricing power, and blending them hides the story.

## 1. Category taxonomy

| Category | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Two-wheeler OEM** | Motorcycles, scooters, and increasingly electric | Hero MotoCorp, Bajaj Auto, TVS Motor, Eicher (Royal Enfield), Ather Energy |
| **Passenger vehicle OEM** | Cars and UVs | Maruti Suzuki, Hyundai Motor India, Mahindra & Mahindra, Tata Motors, Kia |
| **Commercial vehicle OEM** | Trucks and buses; the most cyclical category, tied to freight and infrastructure | Tata Motors, Ashok Leyland, VE Commercial Vehicles (Eicher) |
| **Auto components** | Parts sold to OEMs and into the aftermarket | Uno Minda, Endurance Technologies, Craftsman Automation, Pricol, Lumax Auto, Belrise Industries |
| **Tyres** | OEM fitment plus replacement demand | MRF, Apollo Tyres, CEAT, JK Tyre, Balkrishna Industries (BKT) |
| **Batteries** | Automotive and industrial batteries; increasingly EV-linked | Exide Industries, Amara Raja Energy |

Compare within a category. A 2W maker's 18% EBITDA margin and a component supplier's 13% are not the same
achievement — the cost structures and capital intensity differ fundamentally.

## 2. How companies in this sector make money

**OEMs** sell vehicles at a realisation per unit, against a largely fixed cost base. Because operating
leverage is high, volume growth converts to profit sharply — and volume declines do the reverse. Three
levers move margin:

- **Volume** — the dominant driver, disclosed monthly.
- **Mix** — premium models, higher-cc motorcycles and UVs carry far better realisation than entry-level
  products. A flat-volume quarter with improving mix can still be a good quarter.
- **Input costs** — steel, aluminium, rubber, precious metals and battery cells. Pass-through is
  possible but lags, so a sharp input move compresses margin for a quarter or two before pricing catches up.

**Discounting** is the honest read on demand. When inventory builds at dealers, OEMs discount to clear it
— so rising discounts alongside rising volumes means the volumes were bought, not earned. Always look
for the discount commentary.

**Component suppliers** sell to OEMs on multi-year programmes at negotiated prices, and into the
**replacement market** at materially better margins. The aftermarket is also counter-cyclical: when new
vehicle sales fall, the existing fleet still needs parts. A supplier with a high replacement share is
structurally steadier than one dependent on OEM schedules.

**Content per vehicle** is the supplier's growth lever independent of industry volume — selling more
value into each vehicle produced, through new products or premiumisation.

**The EV transition is the sector's structural question.** For 2W makers it is already commercially live.
For suppliers it is existential in a specific way: an engine-component maker faces obsolescence, while a
supplier of chassis, electronics or interiors is largely agnostic. **State a supplier's EV exposure as
the share of revenue at risk versus EV-agnostic** — that distinction matters more than any headline
growth rate.

### The analogy

A factory with a fixed cost base and a variable output. Volume is almost everything: the plant costs the same whether it runs at 60% or 90%, so each extra unit drops through to profit far harder than revenue growth alone suggests — and the reverse is just as sharp.

## 3. Metric definitions

### OEMs

- **Volumes (units)** — domestic and export, split by segment. Disclosed monthly, so a quarter's
  direction is known in advance.
- **Realisation per unit (ASP)** = revenue ÷ volumes. Rising ASP means better mix or pricing; check which.
  A **blended realisation** spans more than one product class (2W + 3W, motorcycles + scooters), so it
  moves on mix alone — decompose it before reading it as pricing power.
- **Market share** — by segment, against SIAM industry data.
- **EV penetration / EV volumes** — the transition metric, and increasingly the valuation driver for 2W.
- **Export share** — geographic diversification; a hedge against domestic cyclicality but exposed to
  currency and destination-market conditions.
- **Discounts per unit** — the demand-health signal.
- **Capacity utilisation (%)** — asset productivity.
- **Inventory at dealers (days/weeks)** — channel health. Building inventory precedes production cuts.

### Components, tyres and batteries

- **OEM vs replacement (aftermarket) revenue split** — **the most important supplier disclosure.**
  Replacement carries higher margin and is counter-cyclical.
- **Content per vehicle** — value supplied per vehicle produced; the growth lever independent of volumes.
- **Customer concentration** — top OEM as a share of revenue. Losing a platform can remove a large block
  of revenue at once.
- **EV-exposed vs EV-agnostic revenue** — the obsolescence question, stated as a share.
- **Capacity utilisation** and **realisation per unit or per tonne**.
- **Radial share** (tyres) — the mix and technology metric; radials carry better realisation.
- **Raw material index** — natural rubber and crude derivatives for tyres, lead for batteries, steel and
  aluminium for components. These are the margin swing factors.
- **Export share**, and **replacement-market brand strength** where relevant.

## 4. Benchmarks — what good looks like

Indicative; verify against the company's own history and the category.

| Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|
| 2W OEM EBITDA margin | 15–20% |
| PV OEM EBITDA margin | 10–14% |
| Component supplier EBITDA margin | 12–16% |
| Tyre EBITDA margin | 13–17% |
| Battery EBITDA margin | 12–15% |
| Volume growth | at or above SIAM segment growth — below it means losing share |
| Replacement share (suppliers) | >30% adds real stability |
| Capacity utilisation | >75% |
| Customer concentration | top client <30% of revenue |
| Net debt / EBITDA | <1.5× — this sector has historically been conservatively financed |
| Export share | 15–30% gives diversification without over-dependence |

**What to watch, by category:**

- **2W OEM** — Is volume growth ahead of SIAM industry growth? Is mix premiumising? What is EV
  penetration and is the EV portfolio profitable or subsidised? Are discounts rising? How is rural
  demand, which drives entry-level motorcycles?
- **PV OEM** — Is the UV mix rising? What is the order backlog and waiting period? Are discounts building?
- **Components** — Is replacement share rising? Is content per vehicle growing? What share is EV-exposed?
  Is customer concentration falling?
- **Tyres** — Is the replacement share holding? What is natural rubber doing? Is radialisation
  progressing?
- **Batteries** — Is lead cost passed through? Is the industrial and EV-adjacent book growing beyond
  automotive replacement?

### What goes wrong, and the tell

**Channel inventory dressed as demand.** Wholesale dispatches are what the company books; retail registrations are what customers actually bought. The tells: dispatches running ahead of registrations quarter after quarter, which means stock is piling up at dealers and a correction is owed; rising discounts; and realisation improving only on mix while volumes stall.

## 5. Regulatory and policy quick reference

| Area | Body / scheme |
|---|---|
| Emission and safety norms | BS-VI and successors; CAFE norms — compliance drives cost and product cycles |
| EV incentives | FAME-type schemes and state EV policies; **subsidy changes move EV demand directly** |
| Manufacturing incentives | **PLI** for auto and auto components, and for advanced cell chemistry |
| Industry data | **SIAM** (vehicle production and sales), ACMA (components) |
| Scrappage | vehicle scrappage policy — affects replacement demand |
| GST | rate changes on vehicles move demand; cess structure varies by segment |

Emission-norm transitions have historically caused pre-buy spikes followed by demand troughs — a pattern
worth checking before reading any single quarter's volumes as a trend.

### Table columns by category (`financials`)

- **OEM** — Period, Volumes (domestic/export), Revenue, YoY, Realisation/unit, EBITDA, EBITDA%, PAT, YoY
- **Suppliers** — Period, Revenue, YoY, **OEM revenue, Replacement revenue** (as separate columns),
  EBITDA, EBITDA%, PAT, YoY
- **All** — a raw-material table where input costs moved materially: key input, price change, pass-through
  status, margin impact.
- **India vs Export revenue** — recurs as a section in existing reports; include as a table or split.

### Chart reference lines (`charts`)

| Metric | Line | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity utilisation | 75% | "Efficient utilisation" |
| Volume growth | SIAM segment growth for the period | "Industry growth" — label the period; this is the share test |
| Replacement share | 30% | "Stability threshold" — industry norm |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 1.5× | "Sector norm" |

The industry-growth line is the most useful chart element in this sector: a company growing 8% when the
segment grew 12% is losing share, and a bare volume chart will not show it.

### Profile coverage by category (`business-profile`)

- **OEM** — product portfolio by segment and price point, manufacturing footprint and capacity,
  distribution and service network reach, export markets, EV strategy and portfolio, R&D and platform
  pipeline.
- **Suppliers** — product range, **customer list and concentration**, OEM vs replacement mix, plant
  footprint, technology partnerships and JVs (common in this sector), **EV-readiness of the portfolio**,
  export markets, content-per-vehicle strategy.

Existing reports carry a **"Unique Strengths (USP)"** section — cover it, grounded in numbers rather than
adjectives.

### Moat candidates by category (`moats`)

- **Distribution and service network** — for OEMs, the strongest and least replicable advantage in this
  sector. Thousands of dealerships and service points built over decades determine where a rural buyer
  can actually buy and maintain a vehicle. Cite the network size.
- **Brand in the replacement market** — a mechanic's or consumer's default choice for a tyre, battery or
  part is durable and slow to shift.
- **OEM platform embeddedness** — a component designed into a vehicle platform stays for that platform's
  life; requalification is costly for the OEM. Cite platforms won.
- **Scale in a cost-competitive segment** — entry-level 2W economics reward the largest player.
- **Technology partnership or JV access** — common route to capability in components.
- **Be sceptical** of "EV-ready" as a moat. It is a survival requirement, not an advantage, and nearly
  every supplier claims it. What counts is disclosed EV revenue, not stated readiness.

### Valuation (`valuation`)

Primary multiple: **P/E**, with **EV/EBITDA** as the cross-check. OEMs and suppliers trade in different
bands, and within OEMs the market applies a premium for EV positioning and mix premiumisation.

Cyclicality matters: **a trailing P/E at a cyclical peak looks cheap and at a trough looks expensive**,
which is the classic trap in this sector. Where possible read the multiple against mid-cycle margins
rather than the last twelve months, and say which basis is used. P/BV has little relevance except for
asset-heavy suppliers.

### CB Rating substitutions (`cb-rating`)

| Component | Weight | Auto substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 30% | **Volume growth against SIAM industry growth** — share gain, not absolute growth — plus revenue growth and realisation |
| Profitability | 25% | EBITDA margin against the **category** norm (2W 15–20%, PV 10–14%, components 12–16%) and direction |
| PAT Quality | 25% | PAT growth adjusted for one-offs, plus **discount levels** — volumes bought with discounting are lower-quality growth, and this is where that shows |
| Forward Outlook | 20% | Order pipeline and capacity plans, **EV positioning with disclosed EV revenue**, input-cost outlook, replacement-share trend |

Total 100%. Growth is scored on **share** rather than absolute volume because industry growth is
disclosed by SIAM and available — growing slower than the segment is losing, however positive the number.

### Extra sections (`quarterly-report`)

- **Volume Analysis** — monthly and quarterly volumes by segment, domestic vs export, market share
  against SIAM.
- **India vs Export Revenue** — recurs in existing reports.
- **OEM vs Replacement Split** — for suppliers, tyres and batteries; the section that most changes the
  read on a supplier.
- **EV Transition** — EV volumes and penetration for OEMs; EV-exposed vs EV-agnostic revenue for
  suppliers.
- **Raw Material & Pricing** — input cost movement and pass-through status.
- **Unique Strengths (USP)** — appears in existing reports.

### Event transmission map (`event-impact`, `risks-outlook`)

| Event | Reaches results via | Exposure basis to cite |
|---|---|---|
| EV subsidy change (FAME/state policy) | EV demand and pricing | EV volumes as % of total |
| Emission-norm transition | compliance cost, pre-buy then trough | affected segment share |
| GST rate change on vehicles | demand, pricing | segment revenue share |
| Steel / aluminium price move | input cost, margin | metal share of COGS, pass-through lag |
| Natural rubber price move | tyre margin | rubber share of COGS |
| Lead price move | battery margin | lead share of COGS |
| Monsoon / rural demand shift | entry-level 2W and tractor-adjacent volumes | rural revenue share |
| Fuel price move | running cost, demand mix (petrol vs EV vs CNG) | portfolio mix |
| Semiconductor supply disruption | production volumes | electronics-dependent models |
| PLI scheme disbursement or change | reported margin | PLI accrual as % of EBITDA |
| Scrappage policy | replacement demand | replacement revenue share |
| Loss of an OEM platform | supplier revenue | that customer's revenue share |

## 7. Where to look (sourcing)

**Tier 1** — **monthly volume disclosures to the exchanges**, which are unique to this sector and mean
quarterly volumes are effectively known before results. Quarterly investor presentations carry
realisation, mix, OEM/replacement splits, content per vehicle and EV numbers. Earnings calls carry
discount and channel-inventory commentary, which is the honest demand read and rarely appears in print.

**Tier 2 — sector authorities**: **SIAM** — the authoritative source for industry production and sales
by segment, and therefore the correct denominator for any market-share or "outperformed the industry"
claim. **ACMA** for component-industry aggregates, **FADA** for retail registration data (which differs
from OEM dispatches and often tells the truer demand story), and **VAHAN** for registration data.

Dispatch volumes and retail registrations diverge when channel inventory is building — comparing SIAM
dispatches against FADA/VAHAN retails is the standard way to spot it, and it is worth doing whenever
volumes look surprisingly strong.

## 10. Mode applicability

All 15 modes apply. Notes:

- **`segments`** — for suppliers, the **OEM vs replacement split is the highest-value cut** and changes
  the read on the business more than any other disclosure. For OEMs, segment and price-point mix.
- **`charts`** — always plot company volume growth against SIAM industry growth; absolute volume charts
  hide share loss.
- **`valuation`** — P/E primary, but flag the cyclical trap: a trailing multiple at a volume peak
  understates risk. State whether trailing or mid-cycle basis is used.
- **`cb-rating`** — Growth is scored on **share against SIAM**, not absolute volume; discount levels feed
  PAT Quality.
- **`moats`** — distribution networks and platform embeddedness are real; "EV-ready" is not.
- **`event-impact`** — one of the most policy-sensitive sectors here: subsidies, emission norms, GST and
  commodity moves all transmit quickly.
- **Cross-sector work** — all universal metrics apply cleanly: Revenue, EBITDA%, PAT, Mkt Cap, P/E,
  EV/EBITDA and Net Debt are all meaningful, and this sector participates fully. Volumes, realisation per
  unit, OEM/replacement split, content per vehicle and EV penetration are sector-specific and stay in
  their own section.
