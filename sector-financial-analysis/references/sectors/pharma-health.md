# Sector: Pharma & Healthcare (India)

Covers pharmaceutical formulations, APIs and CDMO, hospitals, diagnostics and medical technology.

Two very different businesses sit in this file. **Pharma is a product business** — regulatory approvals,
patent cliffs, price erosion, and manufacturing quality. **Hospitals are a services business** — beds,
occupancy and revenue per patient. They share almost no metrics, so classify before applying any.

## 1. Category taxonomy

| Category | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Formulations — US/Export generics** | Generic drugs for regulated markets; approval- and price-erosion-driven | Sun Pharma, Dr Reddy's, Lupin, Aurobindo, Zydus, Glenmark, Alkem, Alembic |
| **Formulations — Domestic (IPM)** | Branded generics for India; brand and distribution-driven, no price erosion | Mankind, Torrent, Eris, Alkem (domestic arm) |
| **API / Intermediates** | Active ingredients sold to formulators | Divi's, Laurus Labs, Neuland, Supriya Lifescience |
| **CDMO / CRO** | Contract development and manufacturing for innovators | Divi's, Sai Life Sciences, Anthem Biosciences, Laurus (CDMO arm), Gland Pharma |
| **Hospitals** | Multi-specialty and single-specialty care | listed hospital chains |
| **Diagnostics** | Pathology and imaging chains | listed diagnostics chains |
| **MedTech** | Devices and equipment | listed device makers |

**CDMO vs non-CDMO classification recurs as an explicit section** in existing reports, and for good
reason: CDMO revenue is contracted, relationship-based and less exposed to price erosion, so it deserves
a different multiple from generic formulations. State the split.

## 2. How companies in this sector make money

**Generic formulations for regulated markets** are an approval business. Revenue comes from getting a
product approved (ANDA in the US), launching, and then defending share against entrants. **Price erosion
is structural** — a generic loses price every year as more competitors approve, typically high single
digit to low double digit annually in the US base business. Growth therefore has to come from new
launches faster than the base erodes. That is the treadmill: a company with a flat launch pipeline
shrinks even while executing perfectly.

The exceptions that break the treadmill are **complex generics, injectables, and Para IV first-to-file**
opportunities, which carry limited competition and real margin — sometimes 180 days of exclusivity. A
single Para IV launch can distort a year's earnings, which is why one-offs must be separated.

**Domestic formulations (IPM)** work completely differently: branded generics, prescriber loyalty, and
field-force reach. No meaningful price erosion, but price control on scheduled drugs via NPPA. Growth is
volume, new introductions, and price within the permitted band. It is a steadier and generally
higher-multiple business than US generics.

**API and CDMO** sell to other manufacturers. CDMO is the structurally attractive model — long
relationships, high switching costs once a molecule is validated at a site, and revenue that scales with
the innovator's product rather than eroding.

**The regulatory overlay applies to all of them**: manufacturing sites are inspected by the USFDA and
other regulators. **An import alert or Official Action Indicated on a site can halt that site's US
revenue entirely** — it is the single largest binary risk in Indian pharma, and it is site-specific,
which is why plant-level revenue exposure matters.

**Hospitals** earn on beds occupied × revenue per bed. Costs are largely fixed, so incremental occupancy
drops through to profit sharply. Maturity matters enormously: a new hospital loses money for two to three
years before breaking even, so a chain in expansion mode reports depressed blended margins that say
nothing about the mature estate.

## 3. Metric definitions

### Pharma / API / CDMO

- **Revenue mix by geography** — US, India (IPM), Europe, emerging markets, and API/CDMO. **The most
  important single disclosure**, because each carries different growth, margin and risk.
- **ANDA filings / approvals / pending** — the US pipeline. Pending approvals are forward revenue.
- **Para IV / first-to-file** — patent challenges carrying potential exclusivity. Materially lumpy.
- **DMF filings** — API pipeline equivalent.
- **Price erosion (%)** — the annual decline in the US base business. Should be disclosed or discussed
  on the call; without it, US revenue growth cannot be interpreted.
- **R&D as % of sales** — the pipeline investment rate. Typically 6–9% for generics, higher for those
  pursuing complex products or novel research.
- **USFDA site status** — EIR (clean), Form 483 observations, Warning Letter, Import Alert, OAI. **Track
  by site, with the revenue exposed to each.**
- **CDMO revenue share and customer concentration**.
- **Capacity utilisation** for API plants.

### Hospitals and diagnostics

- **ARPOB (Average Revenue Per Occupied Bed per day)** — the price/mix metric. Rising ARPOB means a
  richer case mix or better payer mix, not simply higher prices.
- **Occupancy (%)** — beds occupied ÷ operational beds.
- **ALOS (Average Length of Stay)** — falling ALOS is *good*: faster throughput means more patients per
  bed, so ARPOB and ALOS must be read together. Rising ARPOB with rising ALOS is weaker than it looks.
- **Operational bed count**, and **mature vs new** hospitals separately — blending them hides both.
- **Payer mix** — cash/insurance/government scheme. Government-scheme revenue carries lower realisation
  and slower payment.
- **Diagnostics**: test volumes, realisation per test, B2C vs B2B mix, network size.

- **RoNW (Return on Net Worth)** — net profit ÷ net worth. Quoted in IPO offer documents and
  required in prospectus disclosure, so it appears whenever a company in this sector lists. Report
  it alongside the sector's usual return measure rather than in place of it.

## 4. Benchmarks — what good looks like

Indicative; verify against the company's own disclosure.

| Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|
| EBITDA margin (US generics) | 20–25% |
| EBITDA margin (domestic IPM) | 22–28% |
| EBITDA margin (API) | 18–25% |
| EBITDA margin (CDMO) | 28–35% — the structural attraction of the model |
| R&D as % of sales | 6–9% for generics; below 5% is a pipeline warning |
| US price erosion | high single digit is normal; low double digit is pressure |
| Hospital occupancy | >65% healthy; >70% strong |
| Hospital EBITDA margin | 20–25% mature; blended will be lower during expansion |
| ARPOB growth | mid single digit, mix-led |
| Net debt / EBITDA | <2× |

**What to watch, by category:**

- **US generics** — Are launches outpacing base erosion? How many ANDAs pending? Any site under a Warning
  Letter or Import Alert, and what revenue is exposed? Is the complex/injectable mix rising?
- **Domestic IPM** — Is growth ahead of the IPM market? Is the chronic-therapy share rising (stickier,
  higher margin)? Any NPPA price control on key brands?
- **API/CDMO** — Is CDMO share of revenue rising? Is customer concentration falling? Is the order book or
  contracted pipeline growing? Is capacity utilisation improving?
- **Hospitals** — Is occupancy above 65%? Is ARPOB growth mix-led? What is the mature-vs-new split, and
  is the new estate on its breakeven timeline? Is government-scheme exposure rising?

## 5. Regulatory quick reference

Regulation is the sector's dominant risk and its highest-value information.

| Area | Body / rule |
|---|---|
| US manufacturing and product approvals | **USFDA** — inspections, Form 483, Warning Letters, Import Alerts, ANDA/DMF approvals |
| Indian drug regulation | **CDSCO** |
| Drug price control | **NPPA** — ceiling prices on scheduled formulations under DPCO |
| European approvals | EMA, EDQM |
| Quality standards | WHO-GMP, and site-specific certifications |
| Hospital accreditation | NABH, NABL (diagnostics) |
| Government health schemes | Ayushman Bharat / PM-JAY — affects payer mix and realisation |

USFDA inspection outcomes are published and searchable. **A site classification change is a material,
verifiable event** and should be tracked rather than inferred.

## 6. Per-mode specifics

### Headline KPIs by category (`dashboard`)

| Category | KPIs |

| US/Export formulations | Revenue, US revenue & growth, EBITDA margin, PAT, ANDA approvals/pending, R&D % |
| Domestic formulations | Revenue, IPM growth vs market, chronic mix, EBITDA margin, PAT |
| API | Revenue, capacity utilisation, EBITDA margin, PAT, DMF filings |

| Diagnostics | Revenue, test volumes, realisation/test, network size, EBITDA margin, PAT |

### Table columns by category (`financials`)

- **Pharma** — Period, Revenue, YoY, **US revenue, India revenue, API/CDMO revenue** (the geographic
  split is the table's backbone), Gross margin, R&D, EBITDA, EBITDA%, PAT, YoY
- **Hospitals** — Period, Revenue, YoY, Operational beds, Occupancy, ARPOB, ALOS, EBITDA, EBITDA%, PAT
- **All pharma** — a **USFDA site status table**: site, products, status, revenue exposure, last
  inspection date.

A `.fnote` is mandatory wherever a Para IV exclusivity, a one-time milestone payment, or a settlement
contributed to the period — these are large, non-recurring, and routinely misread as run-rate.

### Chart reference lines (`charts`)

| Metric | Line | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital occupancy | 65% | "Healthy occupancy" |
| R&D as % of sales | 6% | "Typical generics R&D intensity" |
| EBITDA margin (CDMO vs generics) | — | plot categories separately; a 30% CDMO margin and a 22% generics margin are not the same scale |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 2.0× | "Comfortable leverage" |

Industry norms, not regulatory thresholds — label as such.

### Profile coverage by category (`business-profile`)

- **Pharma** — therapy areas, geographic mix, manufacturing footprint **by site with regulatory status**,
  R&D centres, front-end presence in key markets, complex-generic/injectable capability, CDMO arm if any.
- **API/CDMO** — molecules and therapy focus, customer profile and concentration, capacity, integration
  from KSM, regulatory certifications by site.
- **Hospitals** — bed count by city, mature vs new estate, specialty mix, payer mix, occupancy by
  cluster, expansion pipeline with commissioning timelines, doctor engagement model (employed vs
  consultant).

Also cover **Key Strategic Projects** — this recurs as a section in existing reports and captures capacity
expansions, new facilities and major approvals.

### Moat candidates by category (`moats`)

- **US generics** — complex-generic and injectable capability (hard to replicate, fewer competitors), a
  clean regulatory track record across sites (genuinely scarce and slow to rebuild after a Warning
  Letter), and vertical integration into APIs.
- **Domestic IPM** — brand equity with prescribers and field-force reach built over decades. One of the
  strongest moats in this file: a new entrant cannot buy prescriber trust.
- **CDMO** — **validated-site switching costs**. Once a molecule is validated at a site, moving it means
  regulatory refiling and revalidation; innovators rarely do it. Cite the length of key relationships.
- **API** — backward integration to key starting materials, and cost leadership at scale.
- **Hospitals** — location and clinical reputation in a catchment, plus the doctor ecosystem. Both take
  a decade and cannot be bought quickly.
- Be sceptical of "large pipeline" as a moat for plain-vanilla generics — filings are not a barrier, and
  everyone has them.

### Valuation (`valuation`)

Primary multiple: **P/E**, with **EV/EBITDA** as cross-check. The important discipline here is that
**different parts of the same company deserve different multiples** — CDMO and domestic branded revenue
are valued richer than US plain generics, and a sum-of-the-parts view is often more honest than a blended
multiple. Where a company is mid-transition (generics to CDMO, or acute to chronic), say what the blended
multiple is obscuring.

Hospitals are commonly valued on **EV/EBITDA** and on **EV per bed**, with mature-estate margins
normalised for the loss-making new estate.

### CB Rating substitutions (`cb-rating`)

| Component | Weight | Pharma-health substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 30% | Revenue growth, decomposed — **US growth net of price erosion**, domestic IPM growth vs market, CDMO growth; or for hospitals, ARPOB and occupancy growth |
| Profitability | 25% | EBITDA margin against the **category** norm (CDMO 30% and generics 22% are both good) |
| PAT Quality | 25% | PAT growth adjusted for **Para IV exclusivity, milestone payments and settlements**; for hospitals, mature-estate margin excluding new-hospital drag |
| Regulatory & Outlook | 20% | **USFDA site status across the manufacturing footprint**, pending approvals, pipeline — replaces generic Forward Outlook, because regulatory standing *is* the outlook here |

Total 100%. Regulatory status substitutes into the outlook component because an Import Alert can remove a
site's revenue outright — no other forward indicator matters as much.

### Extra sections (`quarterly-report`)

- **Geographic Revenue Mix** — US / India / Europe / EM / API-CDMO. Recurs as a section in existing
  reports and is the primary lens on a pharma company.
- **CDMO vs Non-CDMO Classification** — an explicit section in existing reports; the split drives the
  multiple.
- **Regulatory Status** — USFDA site table with exposure.
- **R&D & Pipeline** — spend, ANDA/DMF filings, approvals, Para IV opportunities.
- **Key Strategic Projects** — capacity, facilities, major approvals.
- **Debt Overview by Segment** — appears in existing reports.
- **Hospital Operating Metrics** — ARPOB, occupancy, ALOS, mature vs new estate.

### Event transmission map (`event-impact`, `risks-outlook`)

| Event | Reaches results via | Exposure basis to cite |
|---|---|---|
| USFDA Import Alert / Warning Letter on a site | that site's US revenue halts or is at risk | **% of revenue from the affected site** |
| USFDA EIR (clean inspection) | removes an overhang, enables approvals from that site | pending approvals tied to the site |
| ANDA approval (especially complex/injectable) | new revenue, limited competition | expected market size, competitor count |
| Para IV outcome | exclusivity revenue, lumpy | product's addressable market |
| US tariffs on generics | export realisation, margin | **% of revenue from US generics** |
| NPPA price control revision | domestic realisation | share of revenue from scheduled formulations |
| Patent expiry in a target market | new opportunity or base erosion | product-level exposure |
| Key raw material (KSM) price or China supply change | gross margin | import dependence for that input |
| Ayushman Bharat / scheme rate revision | hospital realisation | government-scheme share of revenue |
| New hospital commissioning | near-term margin dilution, medium-term growth | beds added vs existing estate |

## 7. Where to look (sourcing)

**Tier 1** — quarterly investor presentations and earnings calls. The geographic revenue split, ANDA
counts, R&D spend and site-wise regulatory commentary appear there rather than in the press release.
Hospital chains disclose ARPOB, occupancy and ALOS in the presentation, usually with mature vs new splits.

**Tier 2 — sector authorities**: **USFDA** — the inspection classification database, Warning Letter
listings and Import Alert lists are public, searchable, and authoritative; cite them directly rather than
relying on news coverage. Also **CDSCO** (Indian approvals), **NPPA** (ceiling price notifications),
**IQVIA** for IPM market growth (the right benchmark for whether domestic growth is ahead of market),
**Pharmexcil** for export data, and **NABH/NABL** for accreditation status.

For any regulatory claim about a site, the USFDA's own published classification is Tier 1 and settles the
question — this is one of the few places where a primary source is this directly accessible.

## 10. Mode applicability

All 15 modes apply. Notes:

- **`segments`** — the highest-value mode here. Geographic mix (US/India/EU/EM) and CDMO-vs-generics are
  the two cuts that decide how a pharma company should be valued, and both are well disclosed.
- **`event-impact`** — unusually important. USFDA site actions are binary, verifiable, and can remove a
  site's revenue outright; US tariff policy and NPPA revisions are recurring.
- **`risks-outlook`** — regulatory risk deserves its own treatment, tracked by site with revenue exposure.
- **`valuation`** — prefer sum-of-the-parts where CDMO or domestic branded revenue is material; a blended
  multiple obscures the mix that matters most.
- **`cb-rating`** — regulatory standing substitutes into the outlook component; see the table above.
- **Cross-sector work** — all universal metrics apply cleanly: Revenue, EBITDA%, PAT, Mkt Cap, P/E,
  EV/EBITDA and Net Debt are all meaningful, and this sector participates fully. ARPOB, occupancy, ANDA
  counts, price erosion and USFDA status are sector-specific and stay in their own section.
