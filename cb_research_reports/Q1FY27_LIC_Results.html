What Happened This Quarter

LIC posted a margin-and-mix quarter, not a volume quarter. PAT rose 22.8% YoY to ₹13,492 Cr and VNB surged 61.3% to ₹3,136 Cr — comfortably beating the Street's ₹2,638 Cr estimate. VNB margin expanded 750 bps to 22.9%, well ahead of the 17.8% consensus, driven by the Non-Par share of individual APE rising to 32.49% from 30.34%. The soft spot: APE growth of just 8.2% missed the ₹14,841 Cr poll estimate, and 61st-month persistency slipped under the 50% healthy-franchise mark. Solvency at 242% is exceptionally strong. On July 28, 2026, LIC appointed Shatmanyu Shrivastava as CFO, replacing Sunil Agrawal.

Corporate Actions & Context

LIC completed a 1:1 bonus issue in June 2026, doubling its share count to improve liquidity ahead of further government stake sales. In early August 2026, the government announced an Offer for Sale (OFS) of up to 6.5% stake, and the stock fell over 7% across five sessions to trade near ₹387–392. LIC completes 70 years on September 1, 2026, and management has flagged new product launches to mark the milestone, alongside continued focus on scaling high-margin term/protection products. The Board approved Q1 FY27 standalone and consolidated results on August 6, 2026.

Business Profile What LIC does · Revenue model · Products · Distribution
LIC — Life Insurance Corporation of India · State-Owned · Founded 1956 · Largest Financial Institution in India

LIC operates a largely mutual-style model where a significant share of profits is shared with policyholders as bonuses (₹59,726 Cr paid to policyholders in FY26). Revenue is built on four pillars: (1) First Year Premium from new policy sales, (2) Renewal Premium from the existing ~29 crore-plus policy book — by far the largest recurring component, (3) Group Business covering corporate gratuity, superannuation, and group term policies (₹51,834 Cr in Q1 FY27, +8.61% YoY), and (4) Investment Income from a ₹59.39 lakh Cr AUM (+6.24% YoY investment income growth this quarter). Distribution runs through roughly 1.3–1.4 million individual agents (the overwhelming majority of new business), supplemented by bancassurance and digital channels. The critical structural shift underway: Non-Par products rising from 30.34% to 32.49% of individual APE this quarter (35.1% for full FY26) — this mix change, more than volume growth, is the primary driver of VNB margin expansion to 22.9% in Q1 FY27.

SWOT Analysis Q1 FY27 lens

💪 Strengths

  • VNB grew 61.3% YoY, beating Street estimates by a wide margin
  • VNB margin expanded 750 bps to 22.9%, ahead of consensus (17.8%)
  • Solvency at 242% gives substantial capital headroom
  • Unmatched distribution scale — ~1.3-1.4 million agents, 60.10% market share by FYPI
  • ₹59.39 lakh Cr AUM makes LIC one of India's largest institutional investors

⚠️ Weaknesses

  • APE growth of 8.2% missed the Street's ₹14,841 Cr estimate
  • 61st-month persistency fell to 48.74%, below the 50% healthy-franchise mark
  • 13th-month persistency (66.45%) still trails private peers who post 85-90%+
  • VNB margin (22.9%) still behind private leaders (SBI Life 27.5%+, HDFC Life ~24%)
  • Embedded Value growth has been subdued (+1.6% YoY as of Mar-2026)

🚀 Opportunities

  • Continued Non-Par/protection mix-shift can push VNB margin toward the 25-30% private-peer range
  • 0% GST on term insurance (effective Sep 2025) is a structural tailwind for protection sales
  • New product launches planned around LIC's 70th anniversary (Sep 1, 2026)
  • Rural/semi-urban penetration remains a long runway private insurers can't easily replicate
  • A re-rating toward even 1.0x P/EV (vs current ~0.63x) would be a purely multiple-driven upside if fundamentals hold

⚡ Threats

  • Government OFS of up to 6.5% stake (announced early Aug 2026) pressured the stock ~7% in five sessions
  • Intensifying competition from digitally-led private insurers leveraging bank networks
  • Equity market volatility can swing Embedded Value sharply (EV fell 6.2% in a single quarter, Q4 FY26)
  • Upcoming IRDAI distribution/commission reforms — management expects minimal impact, but execution risk exists
  • CFO transition (Sunil Agrawal exited July 14, 2026; Shatmanyu Shrivastava took over July 28, 2026) during an active OFS period

Moats & USPs Structural competitive advantages
LIC India
A distribution network of roughly 1.3-1.4 million agents built over nearly 70 years cannot be replicated quickly by any competitor. LIC's sovereign/government backing creates a claim-payment-certainty perception no private insurer can fully match, particularly in rural and semi-urban India. A 70.90% share of the Group business segment and 60.10% overall market share by First Year Premium Income reflect a scale moat that is self-reinforcing — more policies, more renewal premium, more investable float. The ₹59.39 lakh Cr AUM generates a large, steady stream of investment income (≈₹1.09 lakh Cr this quarter alone) that compounds over time. Trust compounds too: LIC paid ₹59,726 Cr in policyholder bonuses in FY26, deepening the loyalty loop each year — a mutual-style dynamic private, fully shareholder-owned insurers don't replicate in quite the same way.

🛡️ Life Insurance Metrics — Explained with Examples
GWP
Gross Written Premium
= Revenue in Manufacturing
What it is: Total premium collected from all customers in a period — the "top line" of an insurer.
💡 Example: LIC collected Total Premium of ₹1,27,250 Cr in Q1 FY27 (₹75,416 Cr Individual + ₹51,834 Cr Group).
Important: This is NOT profit — claims, salaries, agent commissions, and policyholder bonuses are all deducted from this before anything reaches shareholders.
APE
Annualised Premium Equivalent
Standardised New Business Measure
Formula: Regular Annual Premium + 10% of Single Premium.
Why 10%? A single ₹10 Cr premium is economically equivalent to ~₹1 Cr/year regular premium — so both are compared on the same basis.
💡 Example:
LIC's Q1 FY27 APE = ₹13,692 Cr, up 8.2% YoY.
Use APE — not raw premium — to compare LIC fairly against SBI Life or HDFC Life, since insurers collect single vs regular premium in different proportions.
VNB
Value of New Business
= Future Order Profitability
What it is: Present value of all future profits from policies written during the period. This is the single most important life insurance profitability metric.
💡 Example: LIC sold policies worth ₹13,692 Cr APE in Q1 FY27. These policies will earn premium for 15-30 years and pay claims along the way. The present value of net profits from all of them = VNB ₹3,136 Cr.
Unlike PAT which shows THIS quarter's profit, VNB shows the profit baked into business ALREADY SOLD this quarter.
VNB%
VNB Margin
= Gross Margin % in Manufacturing
Formula: VNB ÷ APE.
For every ₹100 of new business written, what is the future profit created?
💡 Example: LIC — VNB ₹3,136 Cr ÷ APE ₹13,692 Cr = 22.9% margin.
This means for every ₹100 of premium business written, ₹22.90 of future profit is locked in.
LIC's margin jumped 750 bps YoY (from 15.4%) but still trails SBI Life (27.5%) and HDFC Life (~24%) — the gap analysts watch closing over FY27-28.
EV
Embedded Value
= Net Worth + Future Profits
What it is: Adjusted net worth plus present value of future profits from all in-force (existing) policies. It is the true economic value of a life insurer — richer than book value.
💡 Example: LIC's last-disclosed Embedded Value (Mar-2026) = ₹7.89 Lakh Cr, up just 1.6% YoY — hurt by a ~10% equity market correction that alone cut EV 6.2% in Q4 FY26.
Analysts use P/EV (Price-to-EV) to value life insurers — similar to P/B in banks. LIC trades at just ≈0.63x P/EV vs 2x+ for private peers.
RoEV
Return on Embedded Value
= ROE for Life Insurers
What it is: How efficiently is the insurer growing its embedded value? Operating RoEV excludes market movements, isolating the genuine business performance.
💡 Example: LIC doesn't disclose RoEV quarterly — brokers (JM Financial) estimate it around 11-12%, right at this framework's 12% healthy threshold.
Compare: SBI Life's RoEV of 19.7% is best-in-class among large private life insurers — the gap shows how much room LIC has to improve capital efficiency even with strong VNB growth.
PERSIST
Persistency Ratio
= Customer Retention Rate
What it is: % of policyholders who continue paying premium at the 13th or 61st month. Low persistency = revenue leakage + agent mis-selling signal.
💡 Example: LIC sells 100 policies. By the 13th month, 66-67 customers are still paying — 13th month persistency = 66.45% in Q1 FY27.

By the 61st month (5 years later), only ~49 are still paying — 61st month persistency = 48.74%, down from 51.12% a year ago — this is the leakage point investors watch, and it's the one retention metric moving the wrong way this quarter.
🏥 Health & General Insurance Metrics — Explained with Examples
LIC is a pure life insurer, so these don't apply directly to LIC's results — but they're included here because they come up constantly when comparing LIC to diversified peers, and understanding them sharpens your read of the life-insurance metrics above.
COMB
Combined Ratio
= Operating Ratio in Manufacturing
Formula: (Claims + All Expenses) ÷ Net Premium Earned.
This is the single most watched metric for health and general insurers — LIC doesn't report one since it's a pure life insurer.
💡 Example: Star Health FY26: Premium ₹20,369 Cr, Claims ₹14,000 Cr (68.7% loss ratio), Expenses ₹6,136 Cr (30.1% expense ratio) → Combined Ratio = 98.8% ✅

Below 100% = underwriting profit. Above 100% = underwriting loss (must rely on investment income).
LOSS
Loss Ratio / Claims Ratio
= Raw Material Cost %
Formula: Claims Incurred ÷ Net Premium Earned.
The "input cost" of insurance — like raw material as % of revenue in manufacturing. Lower is better, but too low may mean claim denials.
💡 Example: Niva Bupa premium ₹9,433 Cr, Claims paid ₹6,120 Cr → Loss Ratio = 64.9%

During COVID-19 (2021), loss ratios of health insurers shot to 90%+ — like a steel plant's coal cost doubling overnight. LIC's claims ratio equivalent — its Claims Settlement Ratio (~97.5% FY26) — measures something different: the % of claims paid vs received, not cost as a share of premium.
CISR
Combined Insurance Service Ratio
IFRS Version of Combined Ratio
What it is: The IFRS 17-based equivalent of Combined Ratio. Used by health insurers like Niva Bupa. Interpretation is identical — below 100% means underwriting profit.
💡 Example: Niva Bupa FY26 CISR = 101.4% — underwriting loss for the full year. But Q4 FY26 CISR = 97.4% — first quarter where underwriting was profitable. Think of it as: for every ₹100 premium collected, ₹97.40 was spent on claims+expenses — ₹2.60 underwriting profit.
SOLV
Solvency Ratio
= Capital Adequacy (like CRAR for banks)
Formula: Available Capital ÷ Required Capital.
IRDAI mandates minimum 150%. Below this = regulatory action. Above = safety cushion.
💡 Example:
IRDAI's minimum required capital for LIC's risk profile works out to a base of 150%.
LIC's Q1 FY27 Solvency Ratio = 242% — up from 217% a year ago.

Think of it like a dam. IRDAI requires a dam 10 metres high (150%). LIC's dam is 24.2 metres high. Even an unprecedented flood (mass claims) won't breach it.
TPA
Third Party Administrator
The Middleman of Health Insurance
What it is: A specialised company that processes health insurance claims on behalf of insurers. NOT an insurer. Earns service fees — like a claims processing factory. LIC doesn't use TPAs the same way a health insurer does, but it's a useful concept when comparing LIC's fully in-house claims process to health insurers.
💡 Example (Real Life):
You are hospitalised at Apollo. The hospital sends your claim to Medi Assist (TPA).
Medi Assist: ✅ Verifies your policy → ✅ Checks treatment against guidelines → ✅ Approves cashless amount → ✅ Pays Apollo on behalf of Star Health.

Medi Assist charges Star Health a processing fee — that's its revenue, not premium income.
🧠 How to Read LIC in 60 Seconds

The one number to track next quarter:
